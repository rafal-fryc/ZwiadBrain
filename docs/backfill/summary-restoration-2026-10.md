### wiki/states/al/bills/al-2024-hb-21.md
- clobbered by `c37b2ac`
- now: Genetic Testing Companies, requiring consent to release customers' genetic data
- restored: Alabama's HB 21, the Alabama Genetic Data Privacy Act, requires genetic testing companies to prominently disclose their privacy policies and to obtain a consumer's express written consent before using a biological sample or genetic data, retaining it for future testing, or transferring/disclosing it to a third party for purposes beyond fulfilling the consumer's order; separate express written consent is required before disclosing genetic data to insurers or employers, and informed consent under 45 C.F.R. Part 46 is required for research uses. It requires companies to let consumers access their data, delete accounts, and request destruction of samples/data (within 30 days) or return of transferred data after revoking consent (within 60 days), and it binds contractors to the same confidentiality duties via contract. The law exempts HIPAA-covered entities/business associates, noncommercial research by higher-education institutions, and law-enforcement-obtained samples, and it is enforced by the Alabama Attorney General's Consumer Division through civil actions carrying penalties up to $3,000 per violation. The act took effect October 1, 2024.

### wiki/states/al/bills/al-2026-sb-63.md
- clobbered by `af892e5`
- now: Health care plans; to regulate the use of artificial intelligence in determinations of coverage
- restored: Alabama SB 63 (2026), enacted, restricts health benefit plan providers' use of artificial intelligence in making prior-authorization and medical-necessity determinations. Insurers using AI for these determinations must base decisions on the enrollee's medical history and individual clinical circumstances rather than group datasets, certify annually that their AI is applied fairly and non-discriminatorily, and ensure that any denial, delay, or modification of a request is ultimately made by a licensed physician or qualified health care professional; providers must also disclose their AI use in policies and periodically review AI outcomes for accuracy. The Alabama Department of Insurance may investigate violations and, for repeat violations amounting to a business pattern, impose fines up to $5,000 or suspend/revoke a provider's certificate of authority. The act takes effect October 1, 2026.

- restored: The My Health My Data Act, signed April 2023, establishes privacy protections for consumer health data that falls outside HIPAA's scope.
