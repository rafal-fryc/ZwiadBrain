## Summary

This bill addresses health information technology provisions concerning cybersecurity practices and information blocking. The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) may reduce fines and penalties for violations of certain federal privacy standards for health information if an entity subject to those standards has adopted particular cybersecurity practices. This bill also authorizes HHS's Office of the Inspector General to obtain information, assistance, and other support from federal agencies when investigating claims of information blocking by developers of, or entities offering, health information technologies. Information blocking refers to a practice that is likely to interfere with, prevent, or materially discourage access, exchange, or use of electronic health information, such as the imposition of fees that make exchanging this information cost prohibitive.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

H.R. 7898 was introduced on July 31, 2020, by Rep. Michael C. Burgess (R-TX-26) as a targeted amendment to the HITECH Act directing the HHS Secretary to consider whether a HIPAA-covered entity or business associate had recognized cybersecurity practices in place for the preceding 12 months when making enforcement determinations — including civil money penalties, audit scope, and remedial terms. Referred to the House Energy and Commerce Committee, the bill carried no formal committee report. On December 9, 2020, the House considered H.R. 7898 under suspension of the rules; Energy and Commerce Chairman Frank Pallone managed the floor, while Ranking Member Greg Walden joined sponsor Burgess in bipartisan support, and the bill passed by voice vote with the required two-thirds majority (166 Cong. Rec. H7088–H7092, Dec. 9, 2020). The Senate received the measure and on December 19, 2020, Senator Cory Gardner moved for its immediate consideration by unanimous consent; the bill passed the Senate without amendment or a recorded vote (166 Cong. Rec. S7853, Dec. 19, 2020). President Trump signed H.R. 7898 into law on January 5, 2021, as Public Law 116-321, creating a HIPAA safe-harbor incentive for healthcare entities that proactively adopt recognized cybersecurity frameworks.
