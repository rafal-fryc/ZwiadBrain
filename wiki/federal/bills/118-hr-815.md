## Summary

PL 118-50 is primarily an emergency supplemental appropriations vehicle funding U.S. security assistance to Ukraine, Israel, and Indo-Pacific allies. It contains two company-facing technology mandates tracked by ZwiadBrain: the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act (Division H) bars app stores and internet hosting services from distributing, maintaining, updating, or providing hosting for any 'foreign adversary controlled application' — defined to include TikTok/ByteDance and any social media platform controlled by China, Russia, Iran, or North Korea — unless the President determines the application has executed a qualified divestiture severing all operational ties to foreign adversary entities; violations carry civil penalties up to $5,000 per U.S. user affected, enforced by the Department of Justice, with the prohibition taking effect 270 days after enactment and a possible one-time 90-day presidential extension. The Protecting Americans' Data from Foreign Adversaries Act (Division I) makes it unlawful for data brokers — entities that sell or provide personal data they did not collect directly from individuals — to sell, license, rent, trade, transfer, or otherwise disclose U.S. residents' sensitive personally identifiable data (including government IDs, financial account numbers, biometric and genetic data, precise geolocation, and private communications) to China, Russia, Iran, or North Korea, or to any entity those countries control; the Federal Trade Commission holds enforcement authority with no stated penalty cap in the bill text.
