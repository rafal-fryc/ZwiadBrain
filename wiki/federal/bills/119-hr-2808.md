## Summary

The Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act (Public Law 119-36) amends the Fair Credit Reporting Act to restrict "trigger leads" — the practice where credit bureaus sell a consumer's mortgage inquiry data to competing lenders the consumer never contacted. A consumer reporting agency may only share a credit report tied to a residential mortgage application with a third party if that party has the consumer's explicit consent, originated or currently services the consumer's existing mortgage, or holds a qualifying banking relationship with the consumer. The law takes effect 180 days after enactment. It matters for privacy tracking because it closes a longstanding data-brokering loophole that exposed homebuyers to unsolicited contact based on their credit activity without any affirmative consent.
