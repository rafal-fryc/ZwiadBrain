## Summary

Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act This bill limits the circumstances in which credit reporting agencies may provide consumer credit reports to third parties in connection with residential mortgage transactions. Specifically, the bill prohibits a credit reporting agency from providing a consumer's credit report to a third party in connection with a residential mortgage transaction unless the transaction consists of a firm offer of credit or insurance and (1) the third party provides documentation certifying that it has the consumer's consent; or (2) the third party has originated a mortgage on behalf of the consumer, is a current mortgage loan servicer to the consumer, or has a current specified banking relationship with the consumer. These provisions take effect 180 days after the bill's enactment.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

H.R. 2808, the Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act, was introduced on April 10, 2025, by Rep. John W. Rose (R-TN-6) with a broad bipartisan cosponsor list; Sens. Jack Reed (D-RI) and Bill Hagerty (R-TN) carried the Senate companion, S. 1467, which the Senate passed on June 12, 2025. The House Financial Services Committee ordered the bill favorably reported on June 10, 2025, by a unanimous recorded vote of 46 to 0, producing H. Rept. 119-166. On June 23, 2025, the full House considered the bill under suspension of the rules, debated it for forty minutes with floor remarks led by Rep. Rose and Rep. Brad Sherman (D-CA-32), and passed it by voice vote with the required two-thirds majority (Congressional Record Vol. 171, No. 107, p. H2876). The Senate took up the House-passed text as Calendar No. 104 and on August 2, 2025, Majority Leader Thune secured passage by unanimous consent without amendment (Congressional Record Vol. 171, No. 134, p. S5522). President Trump signed the bill on September 5, 2025, enacting it as Public Law 119-36; the law amends the Fair Credit Reporting Act to bar credit bureaus from selling mortgage-inquiry trigger leads except to entities with a pre-existing consumer relationship or explicit consumer opt-in, with most provisions taking effect 180 days after enactment.
