## Summary

The FTC charged VTech Electronics Limited and VTech Electronics North America, LLC with violating COPPA by collecting personal information from children under 13 through its online services without parental consent and without adequate privacy notices. VTech also made false representations that registration data was transmitted using HTTPS encryption when it was not. A 2015 data breach exposed millions of records stored in clear text, underscoring the company's inadequate security practices. The matter was resolved via a federal consent order imposing a $650,000 civil penalty and a 20-year compliance program.

## Background

VTech developed and operated children's electronic learning products and online services — including Learning Lodge Navigator, Kid Connect, and Planet VTech — that collected names, birthdates, photos, and audio files from children under 13. In November 2015, a hacker exploited well-known vulnerabilities to move from VTech's test environment to its live environment and exfiltrate personal data stored in clear text for millions of parents and children. VTech's Privacy Policy falsely stated that personally identifying information would be transmitted using HTTPS encryption, when in fact no registration information was encrypted in transmission or storage for Learning Lodge or Planet VTech.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged COPPA Rule violations: VTech collected personal information from children under 13 through Kid Connect, an online service directed to children, with actual knowledge that children used the service, without verifiable parental consent and without adequate notice. Count 2 alleged a false representation under FTC Act Section 5: VTech's Privacy Policy claimed that most personally identifying information would be transmitted in encrypted HTTPS form, when in fact this was not the case.

## Outcome

The court entered a consent order requiring VTech to pay a $650,000 civil penalty jointly and severally, permanently enjoining the company from further COPPA violations and from misrepresenting its data security and privacy practices, and mandating a comprehensive written information security program with biennial third-party assessments for 20 years. VTech was also required to submit annual compliance reports, maintain records for 10 years, and acknowledge and distribute the order to relevant personnel.

## Significance

The action demonstrated the FTC's willingness to pursue COPPA enforcement against international consumer electronics makers whose connected children's products collect sensitive data, and to pair COPPA counts with FTC Act deception counts when a company's privacy policy contains materially false security representations. The 20-year compliance and assessment period signals heightened long-term scrutiny for children's data handlers.
