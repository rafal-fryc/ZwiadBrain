## Summary

The FTC and the State of New York charged Google LLC and YouTube, LLC with violating the Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule by collecting persistent identifiers from child viewers of child-directed YouTube channels and using them to serve behavioral advertising without providing parental notice or obtaining verifiable parental consent. Despite knowing YouTube was a primary destination for young children and earning approximately $50 million from behavioral advertising on child-directed channels, Google and YouTube never complied with COPPA's core requirements. The matter was resolved by a consent order requiring a combined $170 million civil penalty and injunctive relief.

## Developments

- 2019-09-15 — Consent order issued requiring Google and YouTube to pay $170 million in civil penalties and implement COPPA compliance measures for child-directed content on YouTube. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/172-3083-google-llc-youtube-llc)

## Background

Google and YouTube operated a video-sharing platform hosting numerous channels directed to children under 13. Defendants had actual knowledge—through direct communications with channel owners, manual curation of content for the YouTube Kids app, and their own content rating system—that they were collecting personal information, including persistent identifiers for behavioral advertising, from child viewers. YouTube was marketed as the 'favorite website for kids 2-12' while earning close to $50 million from behavioral advertising on child-directed channels.

## Allegations

Defendants violated the Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule by collecting, using, and disclosing personal information—including persistent identifiers used for behavioral advertising—from children under 13 through child-directed YouTube channels while having actual knowledge they were doing so, and by failing to post adequate privacy notices, failing to provide direct notice to parents, and failing to obtain verifiable parental consent.

## Outcome

The consent order permanently enjoins Defendants from violating the COPPA Rule, requires them to develop and maintain a system for channel owners to designate content as directed to children, provide annual COPPA training, provide direct parental notice, post a prominent privacy notice, obtain verifiable parental consent, and refrain from using previously collected personal information from child-directed content. Defendants were ordered to pay $136,000,000 to the FTC and $34,000,000 to the State of New York in civil penalties and damages, both due within 30 days of entry.

## Significance

At $170 million combined, this was the largest civil penalty ever obtained under COPPA at the time of its issuance, establishing that a general-audience platform with actual knowledge of substantial child viewership cannot avoid COPPA obligations and that behavioral advertising revenue earned from child-directed content carries significant enforcement risk.
