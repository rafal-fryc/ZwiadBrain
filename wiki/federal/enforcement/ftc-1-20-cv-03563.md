## Summary

The FTC charged AppFolio, Inc., a consumer reporting agency offering tenant background screening services, with violating the Fair Credit Reporting Act by including obsolete eviction and criminal records older than seven years in consumer reports and by failing to follow reasonable procedures to ensure the accuracy of tenant screening reports. The inaccurate reports — sourced partly from an unvetted vendor whose contract disclaimed accuracy guarantees — contained records belonging to wrong individuals, wrong dispositions, and duplicate entries, potentially causing housing denials. The matter was resolved by a consent order entered December 15, 2020, requiring a $4.25 million civil penalty and permanent injunctive relief.

## Background

AppFolio provided tenant background screening reports to property management companies. Until at least June 2019 it included obsolete eviction and criminal records more than seven years old, and until at least April 2019 it relied heavily on a third-party vendor whose contract disclaimed accuracy guarantees, resulting in reports with misattributed records, inaccurate dispositions, and duplicate entries.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged AppFolio provided consumer reports containing obsolete adverse information — civil suits, judgments, arrests, and other adverse items more than seven years old — in violation of FCRA's prohibition on reporting obsolete information. Count 2 alleged AppFolio failed to follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy of consumer report information, in violation of FCRA's accuracy-procedures requirement.

## Outcome

AppFolio agreed to a consent order permanently prohibiting it from reporting obsolete adverse information and from failing to maintain reasonable accuracy procedures. AppFolio was required to pay a $4,250,000 civil penalty to the U.S. Treasury. The order also imposed acknowledgment, compliance-reporting, recordkeeping, and compliance-monitoring obligations for up to ten years, and the court retained jurisdiction for enforcement.

## Significance

The action reinforces that consumer reporting agencies operating in the tenant-screening space bear affirmative FCRA duties both to purge time-barred records and to vet third-party data vendors for accuracy — contractual disclaimers by vendors do not satisfy the agency's own statutory obligations. The $4.25 million penalty signals meaningful FTC enforcement of FCRA accuracy requirements beyond credit bureaus.
