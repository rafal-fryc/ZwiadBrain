## Summary

The FTC alleged that Ring LLC gave employees and hundreds of third-party contractors unrestricted access to customers' private home camera recordings without adequate notice or consent, and failed to implement basic security measures to protect accounts from credential-stuffing and brute-force attacks. More than 55,000 U.S. customer accounts were compromised between January 2019 and March 2020, with bad actors using two-way camera features to harass and stalk consumers. Ring settled with the FTC under a consent order requiring a $5.8 million payment, mandatory deletion of certain data, and a 20-year comprehensive privacy and data security program.

## Background

Ring LLC, a maker of internet-connected home security cameras, marketed its products as tools to protect consumers' homes while simultaneously allowing every employee and hundreds of Ukraine-based contractors to access all customer video recordings — including footage from bedrooms, bathrooms, and children's nurseries — without adequate notice or consent. Multiple employees exploited this access to spy on customers. Ring also failed to implement standard defenses against credential-stuffing and brute-force attacks despite repeated internal warnings, bug bounty reports, and media coverage, delaying deployment of multi-factor authentication, rate limiting, and video encryption for years.

## Allegations

The FTC charged Ring with three counts: (1) deceptive security representations — falsely claiming it took reasonable steps to secure cameras used to monitor private areas of homes; (2) unfair access to customer videos without consent — allowing thousands of employees and contractors to view intimate recordings without customers' knowledge; and (3) failure to provide reasonable security against unauthorized access, resulting in over 55,000 compromised accounts and enabling bad actors to harass, threaten, and stalk consumers and children via two-way camera features.

## Outcome

Ring entered a consent order requiring payment of $5,800,000 to the FTC within seven days of entry; deletion of pre-March 2018 customer recordings within 30 days and face embeddings and affected work product within 90 days; establishment of a comprehensive privacy and data security program within 180 days, maintained for 20 years; biennial third-party assessments of that program; annual CEO certifications; covered-incident reporting within 10 days; and customer notices to all pre-February 2018 account holders.

## Significance

The Ring action illustrates that inadequate internal access controls over sensitive home surveillance footage — not just external breaches — can constitute an unfair practice under FTC Act Section 5, and signals that the FTC treats employee misuse of customer data as independently actionable alongside failure to secure accounts against external attacks. The 20-year order duration and mandatory data deletion provisions set a notable precedent for IoT/surveillance device makers.
