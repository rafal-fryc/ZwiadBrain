## Summary

The FTC and CFPB jointly charged TransUnion Rental Screening Solutions (TURSS) and Trans Union LLC with violating the Fair Credit Reporting Act by furnishing inaccurate tenant background screening reports — including duplicated eviction entries, wrong case dispositions, mislabeled debt fields, and sealed records — and by concealing that third-party vendors such as LexisNexis were the actual sources of criminal and eviction data in consumer files. The matter was resolved via a federal court consent order. Defendants must pay $4 million in civil penalties and reserve $11 million for consumer redress, alongside broad injunctive and compliance obligations.

## Background

TURSS is a TransUnion subsidiary that prepares tenant background screening reports used by landlords to make rental decisions. It obtained eviction and criminal records from third-party data vendors such as LexisNexis but identified only the originating court jurisdiction — not the actual vendor — when providing consumers with file disclosures. Its reports routinely contained duplicative eviction entries, missing or incorrect case dispositions, mislabeled data fields, and sealed records that should have been suppressed.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged failure to follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy under FCRA § 607(b), based on duplicated eviction entries, inaccurate or omitted case dispositions, mislabeled 'Judgment Amount' fields, and inclusion of sealed records. Count 2 alleged failure to disclose the actual sources of information in consumer file disclosures under FCRA § 609(a), because TURSS identified only the originating jurisdiction rather than the third-party vendors from whom it actually obtained the data.

## Outcome

A federal consent order permanently enjoins defendants from failing to maintain reasonable accuracy procedures and from withholding source disclosures. Defendants must implement written accuracy procedures, maintain a compliance committee, provide free consumer file disclosures on request, and make an FCRA-compliant adverse-action notice template publicly available. They must pay a $4 million civil money penalty to the CFPB within 10 days of order entry and reserve $11 million for pro-rata consumer redress under a Bureau-approved plan. Five-year recordkeeping, compliance reporting, and monitoring provisions also apply.

## Significance

The action is a prominent joint FTC-CFPB enforcement of FCRA accuracy and disclosure obligations against a major tenant-screening consumer reporting agency, highlighting that duplicated public-record entries and opaque vendor sourcing in background reports constitute cognizable FCRA violations — and that screening subsidiaries of large credit bureaus are not exempt from these requirements.
