## Summary

The FTC charged Monument, Inc. — an online alcohol addiction treatment platform — with deceiving users by falsely claiming their health information was '100% confidential' and HIPAA-compliant while secretly sharing sensitive addiction-treatment data with advertisers including Meta and Google via tracking pixels and APIs, affecting up to 84,468 users. The agency alleged five counts under FTC Act Section 5(a) and the Opioid Addiction Recovery Fraud Prevention Act. The matter resolved via a federal consent order imposing a $2.5 million civil penalty (suspended), a permanent ban on disclosing health information for advertising, and a 20-year compliance and privacy-assessment regime.

## Background

Monument operated an online platform offering therapy and medication-assisted treatment for alcohol use disorder. From January 2020 through December 2022 it embedded tracking pixels and APIs from Meta, Google, AdRoll, and others that transmitted users' enrollment in addiction treatment to those advertising platforms without user knowledge or consent. Internal warnings from a Monument engineer and from Meta were ignored. An external assessor found Monument only 60–71% HIPAA compliant despite its public claims of full compliance.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged five counts: (1) unfair privacy practices for failing to implement reasonable measures to prevent disclosure of health information via tracking technologies; (2) unfair disclosure of consumers' health information without affirmative express consent; (3) misrepresentation that Monument would not share health information with third parties; (4) misrepresentation of HIPAA compliance; and (5) a deceptive privacy claim violating OARFPA by representing it would not disclose personal health information to third parties without consent.

## Outcome

A federal consent order was entered requiring Monument to: permanently cease disclosing health information for advertising purposes; obtain affirmative express consent before any other health-data disclosure; notify and instruct all third parties that received data without consent to delete it; notify all 84,468 affected users; implement a comprehensive privacy program within 60 days; undergo independent third-party privacy assessments biennially for 20 years; and submit annual compliance certifications for 10 years. A civil penalty of $2,500,000 was imposed but suspended based on Monument's claimed inability to pay.

## Significance

The action is one of the FTC's first enforcement cases applying OARFPA's data-privacy provisions to an addiction-treatment platform, and it signals that health tech companies using advertising pixels on sensitive-health-data pages face both FTC Act and sector-specific liability regardless of whether they are covered entities under HIPAA. The permanent ban on using health data for advertising and the 20-year assessment obligation set a high remedial bar for the digital health sector.
