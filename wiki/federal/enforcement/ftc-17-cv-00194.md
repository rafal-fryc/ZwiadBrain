## Summary

The FTC charged Credit Bureau Center, LLC with using fake Craigslist rental ads to lure consumers onto websites that deceptively enrolled them in paid credit monitoring subscriptions falsely advertised as free. Consumers were charged $29.94 per month without meaningful consent and without required FCRA disclosures. A federal court permanently enjoined the negative-option marketing scheme and ordered over $5.26 million in consumer restitution, affirmed on appeal.

## Developments

- 2022-04-15 — Consent order finalized permanently enjoining Credit Bureau Center's deceptive negative-option credit monitoring scheme and ordering $5.26 million in consumer restitution, as affirmed and modified by the Seventh Circuit. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/162-3120-x170014-credit-bureau-center-llc-formerly-known-myscore-llc)

## Background

Affiliate marketers posted fraudulent rental property ads on Craigslist and sent fake landlord emails directing prospective renters to Credit Bureau Center websites to obtain credit reports before touring properties. The sites enrolled consumers in a seven-day negative-option trial that converted to a $29.94 monthly subscription without clear disclosure of terms, express informed consent, or the legally required notice pointing consumers to AnnualCreditReport.com. The scheme generated over 500 consumer complaints and caused millions of dollars in losses.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged five counts: (1) false representations about rental property availability under FTC Act Section 5(a); (2) deceptive omissions regarding a purportedly free credit score offer under FTC Act Section 5(a); (3) failure to disclose all material terms before obtaining billing information under ROSCA Section 4(1); (4) failure to obtain express informed consent under ROSCA Section 4(2); and (5) failure to display the required free credit report disclosure under FCRA Section 612(g) and the Free Reports Rule.

## Outcome

The court issued a permanent injunction barring Credit Bureau Center and Brown from negative-option marketing that auto-enrolls consumers without clear and conspicuous disclosure. Brown was ordered to pay $5,260,671.36 in consumer restitution. On appeal, the Seventh Circuit affirmed the judgment but modified it to remove a direction that excess funds be deposited to the U.S. Treasury as disgorgement, finding that exceeded the remedial scope of FTC Act Section 19.

## Significance

The case illustrates the FTC's use of ROSCA and FCRA alongside FTC Act Section 5 to reach deceptive negative-option subscription schemes that exploit consumer credit-report rights, and it produced a Seventh Circuit ruling clarifying the limits of disgorgement remedies under FTC Act Section 19 following AMG Capital Management.
