## Summary

The FTC alleged that Facebook violated a 2012 consent order by misleading users about privacy controls, sharing user data with third-party developers without adequate consent, misusing phone numbers collected for security purposes to serve ads, and misrepresenting facial recognition opt-in requirements. The matter resulted in a finalized 20-year consent order requiring sweeping governance, privacy program, and disclosure reforms, accompanied by a record $5 billion civil penalty.

## Developments

- 2019-07-15 — Final 20-year consent order issued against Facebook for violating its 2012 FTC order by misrepresenting privacy controls, sharing user data with third-party developers without consent, misusing security phone numbers for advertising, and misrepresenting facial recognition opt-in requirements, accompanied by a record $5 billion civil penalty. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/092-3184-182-3109-c-4365-facebook-inc-matter)

## Background

From 2012 through 2018, Facebook misled users about privacy controls, allowed developers to access 'Affected Friends' data without user consent, failed to maintain adequate privacy safeguards, used phone numbers provided for two-factor authentication for advertising, and misrepresented facial recognition as opt-in. Facebook's enforcement of its own privacy policies was influenced by the financial benefit violators provided to the company.

## Allegations

The FTC charged Facebook with six counts: (1) misrepresenting that users could control data sharing via privacy settings (Dec 2012–Apr 2014); (2) continuing the same misrepresentations after the April 2014 F8 Conference announcement that Affected Friend data access would end; (3) secretly whitelisting certain developers to continue accessing Affected Friend data through at least June 2018; (4) falsely claiming in assessment reports that it had implemented adequate privacy controls for third-party developer access; (5) misrepresenting facial recognition as requiring an affirmative opt-in turn-on; and (6) deceptively using phone numbers collected for two-factor authentication to serve targeted advertising.

## Outcome

A 20-year final consent order was issued requiring Facebook to: obtain affirmative express consent before sharing nonpublic user data beyond privacy settings; delete third-party access to covered information within 30 days of user deletion; cease using security-purpose phone numbers for advertising; implement a comprehensive information security program; delete existing facial recognition templates for non-consenting users within 90 days; establish and maintain a comprehensive Privacy Program with independent third-party assessments every two years; create an Independent Privacy Committee with board-level governance authority; submit quarterly and annual certifications signed by the principal executive officer; and report covered incidents within 30 days of verification.

## Significance

This was the largest FTC privacy enforcement action at the time of issuance — the accompanying $5 billion civil penalty remains the largest privacy penalty in U.S. history — notable for its 20-year duration, mandatory independent privacy committee with corporate governance authority, CEO-signed compliance certifications, and structural requirements mandating board-level privacy oversight. The order directly addressed the Cambridge Analytica scandal and Facebook's systematic failure to honor its 2012 consent decree commitments.
