## Summary

The FTC charged Sprint Corporation with violating the Fair Credit Reporting Act's Risk-Based Pricing Rule by enrolling consumers with weaker credit into a fee-bearing Account Spending Limit program without providing required pre-contractual notices disclosing key factors adversely affecting their credit scores. Many consumers discovered the extra fees only after becoming contractually obligated and unable to cancel without penalty. Sprint settled with a $2.95 million civil penalty and a permanent injunction requiring compliant notices going forward.

## Developments

- 2015-10-15 — Consent order entered against Sprint Corporation imposing a $2.95 million civil penalty and permanent injunction for failing to provide required Risk-Based Pricing Notices to consumers enrolled in its Account Spending Limit program. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/142-3094-sprint-corporation-sprint-asl-program-0)

## Background

Sprint extended closed-end credit to consumers for mobile, data, text, and third-party services paid in arrears. For consumers with weaker credit, Sprint placed them in an Account Spending Limit (ASL) Program charging an additional monthly fee of up to $7.99, determined using consumer report information and credit scores. Federal law required Sprint to notify affected consumers—before they became contractually obligated—with a detailed Risk-Based Pricing Notice that included the key factors adversely affecting their credit scores, an explanation of consumer report contents, encouragement to verify accuracy, a CFPB website reference, and credit score details.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: Sprint violated FCRA Section 615 and the Risk-Based Pricing Rule (12 C.F.R. § 1022.70 et seq.) by offering credit on materially less favorable terms based on consumer report information without delivering a proper Risk-Based Pricing Notice before transaction consummation. Specifically, Sprint's notices omitted required key factors adversely affecting credit scores and other mandatory disclosures, and in some instances were delivered only after consumers were already bound by the contract and subject to the ASL fees.

## Outcome

Sprint agreed to a consent order entering a $2,950,000 civil penalty judgment payable to the U.S. Treasury within seven days of the order's entry, plus a permanent injunction barring Sprint from failing to send compliant Risk-Based Pricing Notices whenever credit is offered on materially less favorable terms based on a consumer report. The order also imposed ten-year recordkeeping, compliance reporting, and monitoring obligations.

## Significance

The action signals that the FTC will pursue civil penalties against large telecommunications carriers for FCRA Risk-Based Pricing Rule violations, particularly where consumers are enrolled in fee-bearing programs tied to their credit profiles without receiving the pre-contractual disclosures the rule requires. It reinforces that the notice obligation must be fulfilled before—not after—consumers become contractually bound.
