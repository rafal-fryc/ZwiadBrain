## Summary

The FTC charged EMP Media, Inc., operator of MyEx.com, with nonconsensually posting victims' intimate images and personal information and then extorting them to pay fees of up to $2,800 for removal. The complaint alleged three counts under FTC Act Section 5 and Nevada's deceptive trade practices statute. A consent order was finalized on September 15, 2020, imposing a $205,000 judgment (largely suspended), permanent prohibitions on disseminating nonconsensual intimate images, and a 20-year compliance regime.

## Background

EMP Media operated MyEx.com, a revenge-porn website that solicited intimate images and personal identifying information from ex-partners of victims and posted them publicly without consent. The site included a searchable database allowing public identification and contact of victims. Defendants also operated or facilitated purportedly independent reputation management services that charged victims $499–$2,800 — often via Western Union to the Philippines — to remove material the defendants themselves had posted. Between January 2014 and April 2016, EMP collected more than $4 million.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts: (1) unfair nonconsensual disclosure of intimate images and personal information by soliciting and publicly posting material without the depicted individuals' consent in a manner enabling identification or contact; (2) unfair extortion-based removal fees by charging victims $499 or more to remove content defendants had themselves posted without consent; and (3) violation of Nevada's Deceptive Trade Practices Act through coercion, duress, or intimidation in seeking payment for removal.

## Outcome

A consent order entered September 15, 2020 permanently enjoins the defendant from disseminating intimate images or personal information without verifiable affirmative written consent, from charging any takedown fees, and from benefitting from previously collected nonconsensual material (which must be destroyed within 30 days). A monetary judgment of $205,000 was entered, with $15,000 due immediately and the remainder suspended contingent on the accuracy of the defendant's financial disclosures. The order carries a 20-year compliance and reporting obligation.

## Significance

This was among the earliest FTC enforcement actions directly targeting a revenge-porn operator, establishing that nonconsensual publication of intimate images combined with extortionate removal fees constitutes an unfair practice under FTC Act Section 5. It also invoked state deceptive trade practices law in tandem with federal authority, and imposed permanent injunctive relief well beyond the typical order duration. A direct enforcement precursor to the TAKE IT DOWN Act's NCII framework (PL 119-12).
