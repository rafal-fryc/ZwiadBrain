## Summary

The FTC charged Prime Sites, Inc. (doing business as Explore Talent) with collecting personal information from over 100,000 children under age 13 without parental notice or consent in violation of COPPA, while falsely claiming in its privacy policy that it did not collect such data. The company also used deceptive telemarketing calls to falsely tell users they had been selected for film roles, inducing purchases of $39.95/month memberships. The matter was resolved via a consent order imposing a $500,000 civil penalty and a 20-year compliance regime.

## Background

Prime Sites operated ExploreTalent.com, an online talent search network that collected names, email addresses, phone numbers, mailing addresses, and photographs from children under 13 while knowingly misrepresenting that it did not do so. The site made children's profiles publicly visible and allowed adults to message child users without parental notification. Explore Talent's telemarketers placed unsolicited calls falsely claiming casting directors had selected users for major film roles to induce purchase of paid memberships, when no such interest existed and purchasers received none of the promised opportunities.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts: (1) COPPA Rule violations for failing to provide website notice, direct parental notice, and verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information from children under 13; (2) false representation that Explore Talent did not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13; and (3) false representations to consumers that they had been specifically chosen for film roles or were of interest to casting directors in order to sell paid memberships.

## Outcome

A consent order was entered imposing a $500,000 civil penalty judgment, with $235,000 payable within 90 days and the remainder suspended based on the accuracy of Defendant's financial disclosures. Defendant was permanently enjoined from violating COPPA, required to destroy all previously collected children's personal information within ten days, and permanently barred from misrepresenting casting opportunities or data practices. The order carries a 20-year compliance reporting and recordkeeping obligation.

## Significance

This action demonstrates the FTC's willingness to combine COPPA enforcement with separate deceptive-practices charges where an operator exploits children's personal data to facilitate fraudulent marketing, and illustrates how a suspended penalty contingent on financial representations can be used to ensure payment from a respondent with limited assets.
