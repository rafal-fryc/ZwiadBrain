## Summary

The FTC charged Musical.ly and Musical.ly, Inc. with violating the Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule by knowingly collecting personal information from millions of children under 13 without parental notice or consent. Despite thousands of parental complaints and visible child user profiles, the company failed to notify parents, obtain verifiable parental consent, or delete children's data upon request. The matter was resolved through a consent order requiring a $5.7 million civil penalty and permanent injunctive relief.

## Developments

- 2019-02-15 — Consent order entered against Musical.ly imposing a record $5.7 million COPPA civil penalty and permanent injunction for knowingly collecting children's personal data without parental notice, consent, or timely deletion. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/172-3004-musically-inc)

## Background

Musical.ly and Musical.ly, Inc. operated a video social networking app (later TikTok) used by millions of children under 13, including those who self-identified their age in profile bios. Despite having actual knowledge that children under 13 were registering and using the app — evidenced by thousands of parental complaints and visible user profiles — Defendants failed to comply with the COPPA Rule. When parents complained and accounts were closed, Defendants retained children's videos and profile information on their servers rather than deleting them.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged five counts: failure to provide notice of information practices to parents; failure to provide direct notice to parents before collecting children's data; failure to obtain verifiable parental consent prior to collection and use of children's personal information; failure to delete children's personal information upon parental request when accounts were closed; and unlawful retention of children's personal information beyond what was necessary.

## Outcome

The court entered a consent order imposing a $5,700,000 civil penalty, payable jointly and severally within 7 days of order entry. Defendants were permanently enjoined from violating the COPPA Rule and required to destroy all personal information associated with existing user accounts under specified procedures. The order also imposed 10-year recordkeeping, annual compliance reporting, and compliance monitoring obligations.

## Significance

At the time of entry this was the largest civil penalty ever obtained in a COPPA case, setting a benchmark for the FTC's enforcement posture against children's app operators that collect data from under-13 users while ignoring visible evidence of their age. The respondent later became TikTok, making this order the foundation of TikTok's ongoing U.S. children's-privacy obligations.
