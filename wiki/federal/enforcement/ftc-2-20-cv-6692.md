## Summary

The FTC charged MyLife.com and its CEO Jeffrey Tinsley with displaying deceptive search results falsely implying individuals had criminal or sex offender records to drive subscription sales, making cancellations deliberately difficult, and violating the FCRA by furnishing consumer background reports without verifying that subscribers had permissible purposes. The parties entered a consent order requiring $21 million in combined monetary judgments, a permanent ban on negative-option features, and a 20-year FCRA compliance monitoring program.

## Background

MyLife.com operated as a consumer reporting agency selling background reports on individuals. It lured users with teaser search results that falsely suggested the searched person had criminal, arrest, or sex offender records regardless of whether any such records existed. Subscribers were enrolled in automatically renewing subscriptions without clear disclosure of material terms, charged lump-sum multi-month fees, and faced a deliberately obstructive cancellation process requiring phone calls to unavailable agents. The company also ran deceptive outbound telemarketing misrepresenting subscription benefits and cancellation rights.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged five counts: (1) deceptive misrepresentation of criminal and sex offender records in violation of FTC Act Section 5(a); (2) Telemarketing Sales Rule violations for failure to disclose material cancellation and refund terms; (3) ROSCA violations for negative-option enrollment without clear and conspicuous pre-billing disclosures; (4) FCRA violation for furnishing consumer reports to subscribers without verifying permissible purpose; and (5) FCRA violation for failure to maintain reasonable procedures and provide required User Notices.

## Outcome

The consent order imposed a $28,945,968 monetary judgment against the corporate defendant and a $5,000,000 judgment against CEO Jeffrey Tinsley, with actual payments of $16,000,000 and $5,000,000 respectively secured by a deed of trust on Tinsley's property. Defendants are permanently banned from offering any product or service with a negative-option feature and from misrepresenting the likelihood a consumer has criminal or other covered records. MyLife must establish a 20-year FCRA compliance monitoring program, provide customer data to the FTC for redress administration, and submit annual certifications and compliance reports for 20 years.

## Significance

The action established that a people-search site operating as a consumer reporting agency faces FCRA liability for failing to verify subscriber purposes before furnishing background reports, and combined that data-accuracy theory with FTC Act, ROSCA, and TSR counts targeting the full deceptive funnel — from fake criminal-record teasers to dark-pattern cancellation flows. The permanent negative-option ban against both the company and its CEO individually signals FTC willingness to impose sweeping injunctive relief on people-search business models.
