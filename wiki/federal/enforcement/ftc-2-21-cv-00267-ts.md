## Summary

The FTC charged Vivint Smart Home with allowing its door-to-door sales force to pull credit reports of uninvolved third parties without consent — a practice called 'white paging' — to qualify unqualified customers for financing. When fraudulently-opened accounts defaulted, Vivint transmitted the innocent individuals' information to debt collectors, who then pursued them for debts they never incurred. Vivint also failed to maintain a written Identity Theft Prevention Program despite knowing about the fraud since at least 2016. The matter resolved via a 20-year consent order carrying $20 million in combined civil penalty and consumer redress.

## Background

Vivint sold home security systems through commission-based door-to-door representatives equipped with credit-check software. Representatives circumvented credit requirements by pulling credit reports of unrelated third parties with similar names ('white paging') or by adding impermissible co-signers, allowing unqualified customers to obtain financing on innocent parties' credit histories. Vivint knew of these practices since at least 2016 but failed to take meaningful corrective action, rehired implicated representatives, and did not establish a written Identity Theft Prevention Program until January 2020, months after the FTC began investigating.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts: (1) Vivint failed to develop and implement a written Identity Theft Prevention Program as required under the Red Flags Rule; (2) Vivint permitted sales representatives to obtain consumer credit reports without the subjects' permission and without a permissible purpose under the FCRA; and (3) Vivint unfairly transmitted the names and addresses of innocent third parties to debt buyers, causing those individuals to be pursued for debts they never incurred.

## Outcome

A 20-year consent order was entered requiring Vivint to pay a $15 million civil penalty to the U.S. Treasury and $5 million in equitable monetary relief for consumer redress. The order permanently prohibits obtaining consumer reports without permissible purpose and selling or transferring debt linked to white-paging or impermissible co-signing. Vivint must establish a Customer Service Task Force, an Employee Monitoring Program, and a written Identity Theft Prevention Program; employ a Chief Compliance Officer reporting to the Board; undergo biennial third-party compliance assessments for 20 years; and submit annual sworn certifications of compliance.

## Significance

The action illustrates that the FTC will hold companies liable under both the FTC Act and the FCRA when internal sales incentive structures predictably generate identity theft at scale and management fails to act on known misconduct. The $20 million combined judgment and 20-year oversight term signal that prolonged inaction after discovering systematic misuse of consumer reports will draw substantial penalties.
