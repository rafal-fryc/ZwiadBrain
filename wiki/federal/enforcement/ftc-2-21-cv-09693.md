## Summary

The FTC charged OpenX Technologies, a programmatic advertising exchange, with collecting precise location data through a backdoor method that bypassed users' location permission denials, and with collecting personal information from children via child-directed apps without verifiable parental consent. The matter was resolved by a federal consent order imposing a $7.5 million civil penalty, mandatory data deletion, and a 20-year privacy compliance program.

## Developments

- 2021-12-15 — Consent order entered against OpenX Technologies for covertly collecting location data in defiance of user permission denials and harvesting children's personal data from child-directed apps without parental consent, resulting in a $7.5 million penalty and a 20-year privacy compliance program. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/1923019-openx-technologies-inc)

## Background

OpenX operates a programmatic advertising exchange. It collected precise location data (BSSIDs) from consumers who had denied location permissions by accessing the ARP cache rather than using sanctioned APIs. It also processed ad requests from hundreds of child-directed mobile applications, transmitting children's personal information to advertisers for behavioral advertising without COPPA-required parental notice or consent.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts: (1) deceptive misrepresentation that consumers could opt out of location data collection via device settings, when in fact OpenX used a backdoor method to collect location data regardless; (2) deceptive misrepresentation that OpenX did not engage in activities requiring COPPA parental consent and that it properly flagged child-directed inventory; and (3) a direct COPPA violation for collecting personal information from child-directed app users without providing proper notice or obtaining verifiable parental consent.

## Outcome

A federal consent order was entered requiring OpenX to pay a $7.5 million civil penalty ($2 million due immediately, remainder suspended based on financial representations), delete all ad request data collected prior to the order within 90 days, establish and maintain a comprehensive privacy program, submit to biennial third-party privacy assessments for 20 years, provide annual senior management compliance certifications for 10 years, and notify all demand-side partners of the order.

## Significance

The case illustrates FTC enforcement against ad-tech companies that engineer technical workarounds to circumvent user privacy controls, and signals that programmatic advertising intermediaries bear direct COPPA liability for children's data flowing through their exchanges — not just the app publishers.
