## Summary

The FTC charged ITMedia Solutions LLC with operating approximately 200 loan-application websites that collected consumers' sensitive personal and financial data under the false pretense of connecting them with lenders, then sold that data to marketers, debt negotiators, and other unvetted third parties. The company also improperly obtained and used consumers' credit scores without a permissible purpose under the FCRA. A consent order was finalized on January 15, 2022, imposing a $1.5 million civil penalty and permanent prohibitions on misrepresenting data practices and sharing sensitive personal information without informed consent.

## Background

ITMedia operated roughly 200 websites soliciting consumers to fill out online loan applications, collecting sensitive details including Social Security numbers, bank account numbers, and driver's license numbers. Despite representing that information would be shared only with lenders, ITMedia sold the data broadly — including to entities whose business was unknown to ITMedia — and obtained credit scores from a national consumer reporting agency to price and filter leads without a permissible FCRA purpose.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged five counts: (1) deceptive representations that consumer data would be shared only with lenders to secure loan offers; (2) unfair distribution of sensitive personal and financial information to a wide range of buyers; (3) obtaining and using consumer reports without a permissible purpose under FCRA; (4) failure to establish procedures to limit resale of consumer report information to permissible users and purposes; and (5) reselling consumer reports filtered by credit score to unidentified users.

## Outcome

The finalized consent order imposes a $1,500,000 civil penalty payable within seven days of entry. Defendants are permanently prohibited from misrepresenting data-sharing practices, selling or disclosing sensitive personal information without informed consumer consent and recipient screening, and obtaining or using consumer reports outside permissible FCRA purposes. Defendants must destroy all previously collected consumer data used in lead-generation activities within 30 days and instruct recipients to do the same. Ten years of recordkeeping and compliance reporting are required.

## Significance

This action illustrates the FTC's use of both FTC Act Section 5 and FCRA to attack the lead-generation industry's practice of harvesting loan-application data under deceptive pretexts and reselling it indiscriminately, including to non-lenders. The permanent data-destruction and recipient-screening provisions go beyond the monetary penalty and signal enforcement expectations for data brokers handling sensitive financial information.
