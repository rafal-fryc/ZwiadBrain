## Summary

The FTC charged Amazon with deceiving millions of Alexa users by falsely representing that voice recordings and geolocation data could be fully deleted, while systematically retaining transcripts, storing geolocation data in deletion-insulated locations, and keeping children's voice recordings indefinitely. Amazon also granted approximately 15,000 employees unnecessary access to sensitive voice recordings. The matter resolved through a consent order requiring deletion of covered data, a 20-year comprehensive privacy program, and a $25 million civil penalty.

## Developments

- 2023-07-15 — Consent order issued against Amazon requiring a $25 million civil penalty, 20-year privacy program, and mandated deletion practices for Alexa voice and geolocation data following COPPA and FTC Act charges. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/192-3128-amazoncom-alexa-us-v)

## Background

Amazon collected voice recordings and geolocation data from millions of Alexa users — including over 800,000 children under 13 — through Echo smart speakers and the Alexa mobile app. Despite public assurances that users and parents could delete their data at any time, Amazon retained written transcripts of deleted voice recordings, stored geolocation data in secondary locations not subject to deletion requests, and applied default settings that preserved children's voice recordings indefinitely. Internal 'least permission' access policies were also violated by granting roughly 15,000 employees access to users' sensitive recordings.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged four counts: (1) deceptive representation that Alexa App users could delete their geolocation information; (2) deceptive representation that users and parents could delete voice recordings, including all transcripts; (3) unfair privacy practices including unreasonable retention, failure to correct recurring deletion failures, and failure to notify consumers when deletions were not honored; and (4) COPPA violations for indefinitely retaining children's voice recordings, failing to provide truthful deletion notices to parents, and failing to delete transcripts upon parental request.

## Outcome

Amazon entered a consent order requiring deletion of children's personal information linked to inactive Alexa child profiles, permanent prohibition on misrepresenting its data retention and deletion practices, mandated deletion of covered data upon user or parent request, public disclosure of retention and deletion practices, a 20-year comprehensive privacy program, 10 years of annual compliance certifications, and payment of a $25,000,000 civil penalty to the U.S. Treasury.

## Significance

The action is one of the largest COPPA-related penalties imposed on a major technology platform and establishes that cosmetic deletion controls — where backend transcripts or secondary data stores are insulated from user deletion requests — constitute deception under Section 5 of the FTC Act. The 20-year privacy program requirement signals durable behavioral relief beyond the monetary penalty.
