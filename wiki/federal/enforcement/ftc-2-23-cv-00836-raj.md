## Summary

The FTC charged Microsoft Corporation with violating COPPA by collecting personal information from children under 13 on its Xbox Live service without providing required parental notices or obtaining verifiable parental consent. Microsoft also retained incomplete-registration data from approximately 10 million individuals, including children, indefinitely from 2015 through at least October 2020. The matter was resolved through a federal court consent order requiring Microsoft to pay a $20 million civil penalty and implement comprehensive COPPA compliance reforms.

## Developments

- 2023-06-15 — Federal consent order entered requiring Microsoft to pay a $20 million civil penalty and implement COPPA compliance reforms for Xbox Live's collection of children's personal information without parental consent. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/1923258-microsoft-corporation-us-v)

## Background

Microsoft operated Xbox Live and collected personal information from children — including phone numbers, email addresses, names, and dates of birth — during account creation before notifying parents or obtaining consent. Its Privacy Statement was incomplete regarding children's data practices, and it failed to notify third-party game and app developers when sharing children's personal information. From 2015 until at least October 2020, Microsoft retained incomplete-registration data from roughly 10 million individuals far longer than reasonably necessary.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged a single COPPA Rule violation covering four failures: (1) not providing complete direct notice to parents about information collected from children and how it is used or disclosed; (2) not providing a complete online notice of information practices regarding children; (3) collecting, using, and disclosing children's personal information without first obtaining verifiable parental consent; and (4) retaining children's personal information longer than reasonably necessary.

## Outcome

A federal consent order (Docket 2:23-cv-00836-RAJ) entered on June 15, 2023 permanently enjoined Microsoft from repeating the violations, required a $20 million civil penalty payable to the U.S. Treasury within 7 days, mandated retroactive parental-consent outreach for existing child accounts with deletion of unconsented data within one year, required Microsoft to notify third-party publishers whenever it discloses data from a child account, imposed a two-week deletion window for unaccepted consent data, required internal documentation of every child-data field, and established 10-year recordkeeping and annual compliance-reporting obligations.

## Significance

At $20 million, this was one of the largest COPPA civil penalties assessed at the time and established that major platform operators must apply COPPA protections to incomplete registrations, not merely finalized accounts. The order's affirmative obligation to notify third-party game publishers of child-account status extended COPPA compliance requirements down the data-sharing chain, setting a precedent for downstream disclosure controls in gaming ecosystems.
