## Summary

The FTC alleged that Rite Aid deployed inaccurate facial recognition technology in hundreds of retail pharmacy locations from 2012 to 2020 without adequate safeguards, causing wrongful surveillance and harm to innocent consumers — disproportionately affecting minority shoppers. The company also violated a 2010 FTC Order by failing to maintain a comprehensive information security program. The matter was resolved through a consent order issued in March 2024.

## Developments

- 2024-02-26 — Consent order issued prohibiting Rite Aid from using facial recognition in retail for five years and imposing 20-year data security oversight following FTC allegations of harmful AI surveillance practices and violation of a prior 2010 order. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/2023190-rite-aid-corporation-ftc-v)

## Background

Rite Aid used facial recognition technology to identify 'persons of interest' in its stores without informing consumers. The system generated thousands of false-positive alerts, leading employees to surveil, detain, publicly accuse, ban, and call police on innocent people. Black, Asian, Latino, and women consumers were disproportionately harmed. Rite Aid never tested the technology for accuracy, failed to enforce image quality standards, failed to train employees properly, and did not monitor false positive rates. Separately, Rite Aid had been subject to a 2010 FTC Order requiring a comprehensive information security program, which it failed to maintain — including inadequate vendor vetting and missing written documentation.

## Allegations

Count 1 charged Rite Aid with unfair facial recognition technology practices under FTC Act Section 5(a), alleging it deployed the technology without reasonable steps to address risks of harm from false-positive alerts. Count 2 charged violation of Section II of the 2010 FTC Order, alleging Rite Aid failed to establish and maintain a comprehensive information security program, including failures in service provider oversight and written documentation of the program.

## Outcome

Rite Aid agreed to a 20-year consent order. Key provisions include: a five-year prohibition on deploying facial recognition or analysis systems in any retail or online platform; deletion of all consumer photos and videos used with facial recognition within 45 days; mandatory monitoring programs and consumer notice procedures before any future use of automated biometric surveillance systems; biometric data retention limits; a comprehensive information security program with biennial independent third-party assessments for 20 years; annual senior-executive compliance certifications; and covered incident reporting obligations. The 2010 Order was superseded and replaced.

## Significance

This is the first FTC enforcement action specifically targeting a retailer's deployment of facial recognition technology. It establishes that deploying inaccurate AI-based surveillance systems without testing, safeguards, or employee training — and without informing consumers — constitutes an unfair practice under Section 5. The order's explicit attention to disproportionate racial and demographic harms signals that algorithmic bias in automated decision-making systems is within the FTC's unfairness framework. The five-year facial recognition ban and 20-year oversight period set a notable precedent for AI surveillance accountability.
