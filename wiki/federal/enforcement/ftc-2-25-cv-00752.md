## Summary

The FTC and Utah Attorney General charged Aylo Group Ltd., operator of Pornhub and over 100 related pornographic websites, with distributing child sexual abuse material and non-consensual content while running a deceptive public-relations campaign falsely claiming robust content moderation. The complaint also alleged that Aylo secretly routed performers' government-issued identity documents to its own unsecured database, storing them in plain text and emailing them unencrypted in violation of its own security policies. The matter resolved in a consent order requiring a $15 million civil penalty, a comprehensive CSAM/NCM prevention program, a privacy and information security program, and ongoing third-party assessments.

## Background

Aylo Group Ltd. and related entities own and operate Pornhub.com and over 100 other pornographic websites that collectively received more than 800 million monthly U.S. visits as of 2021. Since at least 2012, the defendants allegedly solicited and distributed tens of thousands of videos and images constituting child sexual abuse material and non-consensual material, including rape, spy-camera, and revenge-porn content, while earning tens of millions of dollars in advertising and subscription revenue from it. Performers who enrolled in the defendants' Model Program submitted government-issued identification documents that Aylo secretly routed through a third-party vendor into its own internal database, where they were stored in plain text and emailed unencrypted to unauthorized employees.

## Allegations

The eleven-count complaint alleged unfairness for actively distributing CSAM and non-consensual material; seven deception counts for falsely representing that flagged content was reviewed and removed, that CSAM uploaders were banned, that removed CSAM could not be re-uploaded, that age-verification paperwork was maintained, that all uploads were manually reviewed before going live, and that the sites contained no CSAM or NCM; a failure-to-disclose count for misrepresenting that performers' personal information would be held only by a third-party vendor when it was actually collected and stored by Aylo; a data-security misrepresentation count for falsely claiming reasonable precautions protected that personal information; and two counts under the Utah Consumer Sales Practices Act for unconscionable and deceptive acts and practices toward Utah consumers.

## Outcome

A consent order entered in federal court required Aylo to pay a $15 million civil penalty judgment to Utah (with $5 million due immediately and $10 million suspended conditioned on compliance); permanently prohibited misrepresentations about CSAM/NCM prevention and about privacy or data security; required establishment within 90 days of a comprehensive CSAM and NCM Prevention Program and within 60 days of a Privacy and Information Security Program, each subject to biennial independent third-party assessments; mandated suspension of unverified pre-program content; required posting of a court-mandated consumer notice for two years; and imposed ten years of recordkeeping and annual CEO or senior-officer compliance certifications.

## Significance

The action is one of the FTC's most prominent enforcement matters against an online platform for combining content-harm, deceptive data-collection, and data-security charges in a single complaint, and it resulted in comprehensive operational mandates—including independent third-party assessments of both content-moderation and information-security programs—that could serve as a template for future platform-accountability orders.
