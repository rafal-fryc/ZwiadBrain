## Summary

The FTC charged Disney Worldwide Services and Disney Entertainment Operations with violating COPPA by failing to designate child-directed YouTube videos as 'Made for Kids,' which allowed YouTube to collect personal data from children via persistent identifiers and serve targeted advertising without parental notice or consent. The complaint covers over 1,250 Disney-operated YouTube channels featuring content from properties such as Frozen, Toy Story, and Mickey Mouse. Disney compounded the violation by maintaining a blanket channel-level designation policy even after YouTube alerted it in 2020 that over 300 videos had been reclassified. The matter was resolved through a consent order imposing a $10 million civil penalty and a mandatory ten-year audience-designation compliance program.

## Background

Disney operated over 1,250 YouTube channels carrying child-directed video content. YouTube requires creators to label videos as 'Made for Kids' or 'Not Made for Kids,' which controls whether targeted advertising and personal data collection are permitted. Disney's corporate policy set designations only at the channel level, so child-directed videos on NMFK-designated channels defaulted to NMFK. YouTube notified Disney in June 2020 that it had reclassified more than 300 videos as MFK, but Disney did not revise its policy and continued enabling targeted advertising on improperly labeled child-directed videos.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged a single count of violations of the COPPA Rule: Disney operated portions of online services directed to children and, in numerous instances, allowed personal information to be collected from children on its behalf without providing direct notice to parents, posting required online notice of information practices, or obtaining verifiable parental consent before collection.

## Outcome

The court entered a consent order permanently enjoining Disney from future COPPA Rule violations and requiring it to implement and maintain for ten years a formal program to review all YouTube videos and designate child-directed content as Made for Kids. Disney was ordered to pay a $10,000,000 civil penalty to the U.S. Treasury within seven days of entry. The order also imposed ten-year recordkeeping and compliance-reporting obligations and authorized ongoing FTC compliance monitoring.

## Significance

The action underscores that large media companies operating YouTube channels at scale cannot rely on channel-level audience designations to satisfy COPPA obligations for individual child-directed videos, and that failure to act on platform notifications of misclassified content constitutes an independent aggravating factor. The $10 million penalty signals the FTC's willingness to impose substantial monetary relief against well-resourced respondents for systemic children's privacy violations on third-party platforms.
