## Summary

The FTC sued HoYoverse (Cognosphere, LLC and Cognosphere Pte. Ltd.), the developer of Genshin Impact, alleging it collected personal data from children under 13 without parental consent in violation of COPPA and deceived players about loot box odds and costs through a multi-tier virtual currency system. The case resolved as a consent order requiring $20 million in civil penalties, permanent injunctions against COPPA violations and misrepresentations, and new age-gating and parental-consent requirements for in-game purchases by minors.

## Background

Cognosphere operates the popular online video game Genshin Impact, which is directed to children and teenagers. Despite knowing that users under 13 were playing, the company collected their personal information without parental notice or verifiable parental consent. The game also used a confusing three-tier virtual currency system — real dollars converted to Genesis Crystals, then to Primogems, then to Wish tokens — that obscured the true cost of loot box prizes, whose actual odds of yielding rare items were as low as 0.3% despite advertising that promoted 'huge drop-rate boosts.'

## Allegations

The FTC charged five counts: (1) COPPA Rule violations for collecting children's personal information without required parental notice or consent; (2) misrepresenting loot box odds by advertising inflated drop rates that did not reflect the true 0.3% probability; (3) misrepresenting the cost of loot box prizes through the multi-tier currency system; (4) unfairly selling a multi-tier virtual currency system to children and teenagers; and (5) unfairly promoting and selling chance-based loot boxes to minors without age-gating, odds disclosure at point of download, or spending restrictions.

## Outcome

A consent order entered a $20 million civil penalty judgment jointly and severally against the defendants. The order permanently enjoins COPPA violations, requires deletion of previously collected children's data for users who cannot confirm their age, mandates an age gate and parental consent before minors can spend real money in-game, requires a direct fiat-currency purchase option alongside any virtual-currency loot box mechanism, and mandates clear and conspicuous disclosure of odds and costs at point of sale. Defendants must also submit compliance reports and maintain records for ten years.

## Significance

The action is a notable COPPA enforcement in the video-game industry, pairing children's data privacy violations with deceptive loot box practices. The $20 million penalty and the structural remedy requiring a direct fiat-currency purchase option alongside virtual currency systems set a precedent for how the FTC addresses opaque in-game monetization schemes targeting minors.
