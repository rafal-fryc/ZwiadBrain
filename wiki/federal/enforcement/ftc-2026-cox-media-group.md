## Summary

The FTC alleged that Cox Media Group and two marketing firms it worked with, MindSift LLC and 1010 Digital Works LLC, deceived customers by falsely claiming their “Active Listening” service used an AI algorithm to intercept smart-device conversations in real time to target localized ads, and that consumers had opted into such targeting. In reality the service did not use voice data at all and instead resold marked-up email lists from data brokers without obtaining consumer consent. The three companies agreed to proposed consent orders requiring a combined $930,000 in consumer redress and prohibiting future misrepresentations about their advertising services.

## Allegations

In three separate complaints, the FTC alleged the companies falsely claimed their Active Listening service listened in on consumers’ smart-device conversations in real time to place geographically targeted ads. The service did not capture voice data at all; it resold third-party email lists at a significant markup and did not accurately place ads in customers’ desired locations. The FTC further alleged the companies falsely told potential customers that consumers had opted in to the service by accepting mandatory app terms of service, which the agency found does not constitute opt-in consent for voice-data collection. MindSift and 1010 Digital Works were charged with a second count of furnishing CMG with the means and instrumentalities to deceive its customers.

## Outcome

The Commission voted 2-0 to issue the proposed administrative complaints and accept consent agreements. CMG will pay $880,000 and both MindSift and 1010 Digital Works will each pay $25,000, funds to be used for consumer redress. All three defendants are prohibited from misrepresenting the features of their advertising services, consumer consent for voice-data collection, and geographic targeting capabilities. The proposed orders are subject to a 30-day public comment period before the Commission decides whether to make them final.

## Significance

This action targets AI-washing in adtech — companies that falsely invoke AI or device-surveillance capabilities to sell marketing services. The FTC’s finding that click-through app terms of service do not constitute opt-in consent for voice-data collection sets a clear standard, and the action puts the broader advertising industry on notice that false claims about AI-powered targeting violate Section 5 of the FTC Act.
