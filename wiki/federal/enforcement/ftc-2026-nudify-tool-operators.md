## Summary

The FTC sent warning letters to a dozen websites offering so-called “nudify” tools — AI services that generate nonconsensual sexualized images from clothed photos — alleging they appear to violate the TAKE IT DOWN Act by failing to provide a mechanism for victims to request removal of nonconsensual intimate images. The letters demand immediate compliance and warn that failure could result in FTC legal action and civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation. The FTC began enforcing the TAKE IT DOWN Act on May 19, 2026.

## Allegations

The twelve targeted companies offer AI-powered nudify tools that strip clothing from images of individuals to produce nonconsensual intimate imagery. The FTC’s letters allege these platforms appear to violate Section 3 of the TAKE IT DOWN Act by failing to establish a process through which victims can request removal of such images and to remove them within 48 hours of a valid request, as the law requires.

## Significance

This is among the first enforcement signals under the newly effective TAKE IT DOWN Act, signed in May 2025 and enforceable from May 19, 2026. By targeting AI-powered nudify tools specifically, the FTC signals that generative-AI services facilitating nonconsensual intimate imagery fall squarely within TIDA’s scope, establishing a precedent for how the agency will pursue AI-enabled image-abuse platforms.
