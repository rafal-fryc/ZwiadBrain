## Summary

The FTC alleged that Kuuhubb Inc. and its affiliates violated COPPA by operating the Recolor App — marketed as an adult coloring book but containing a child-directed section — and collecting children's personal information for behavioral advertising without parental notice or consent. The matter was resolved by a consent order entered July 15, 2021. Defendants paid $100,000 immediately, with the remaining $2.9 million of a $3 million civil penalty suspended on compliance conditions.

## Background

Kuuhubb Inc., Kuu Hubb Oy, and Recolor Oy operated the Recolor App, a coloring-book mobile application with 1–5 million monthly active U.S. users. Although marketed as an adult product, the App included a 'Kids' category featuring child-oriented content such as My Little Pony characters and cartoonish animals. The App collected names, photos, profile pictures, comments, and persistent identifiers from child users without an adequate privacy policy, direct parental notice, or verifiable parental consent, and allowed third-party advertising networks to harvest persistent identifiers from child users for behavioral advertising without disclosing that portion of the App was child-directed.

## Allegations

Defendants violated the COPPA Rule by collecting, using, and disclosing personal information from children under 13 — including names, photos, comments, and persistent identifiers — through the Recolor App and via third-party behavioral advertising networks, without providing required privacy policy notice, direct notice to parents, or obtaining verifiable parental consent.

## Outcome

A $3,000,000 civil penalty judgment was entered jointly and severally against defendants; $100,000 was due within seven days and the remainder was suspended upon timely compliance. Defendants were permanently enjoined from further COPPA violations, required to delete all children's personal information within 60 days and instruct third parties to do the same, required to provide refunds to underage subscribers, and required to post in-app notices about the settlement.

## Significance

The case illustrates that an app nominally targeted at adults can still qualify as child-directed — and thus trigger COPPA — when it contains a discrete section with content clearly appealing to children under 13. It also reinforces the FTC's expectation that app operators affirmatively inform third-party ad networks when any portion of an app is child-directed.
