## Summary

The FTC charged Edmodo, LLC with violating COPPA by collecting personal information from hundreds of thousands of children under 13 without verifiable parental consent and improperly attempting to shift its compliance obligations onto schools and teachers. Edmodo also collected persistent identifiers from children for contextual advertising and retained children's data indefinitely without justification. A federal consent order was entered imposing a $6 million civil penalty (suspended based on financial representations) and permanent injunctive relief.

## Developments

- 2023-08-15 — Consent order entered against Edmodo for COPPA violations, imposing a suspended $6 million civil penalty and permanent injunctions against collecting children's data without parental consent or improperly delegating compliance duties to schools. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/202-3129-edmodo-llc-us-v)

## Background

Edmodo operated an online educational platform directed to children under 13, collecting personal information from approximately 600,000 students under 13 in 2020 alone. Rather than fulfilling its own COPPA obligations, Edmodo buried misleading language in its Terms of Service to shift compliance duties onto schools and teachers without providing adequate notice or instructions. It also collected persistent identifiers for contextual advertising — a non-educational commercial purpose schools lacked authority to authorize — and retained roughly 36 million student accounts indefinitely until adopting a deletion policy in March 2020 that it also failed to justify under the COPPA Rule.

## Allegations

Count 1 charged Edmodo with multiple violations of the COPPA Rule, including collecting and using children's personal information while improperly relying on schools for COPPA notice and consent, and retaining children's data without a justified retention policy. Count 2 charged Edmodo with unfair practices under FTC Act Section 5 for outsourcing its legally mandated COPPA compliance duties to schools and teachers without providing them adequate information or support to meet the Rule's requirements.

## Outcome

The court entered a consent order imposing a $6 million civil penalty judgment, suspended based on Edmodo's sworn financial statements but revivable upon material misstatement. The order permanently prohibits Edmodo from failing to provide direct notice, failing to obtain verifiable parental consent or school authorization, conditioning participation on excess data disclosure, and violating the COPPA Rule. Edmodo must destroy previously collected non-consented children's data within 60 days, comply with data minimization requirements, and is subject to 10 years of compliance reporting and recordkeeping obligations.

## Significance

The action established that an ed-tech platform cannot use buried Terms of Service language to offload its own COPPA obligations onto schools and teachers, and confirmed that collecting persistent identifiers from children for advertising — even in an educational context — requires direct parental consent that schools cannot substitute. The suspended penalty structure also signaled that financial inability may reduce actual payment but does not eliminate the legal judgment.
