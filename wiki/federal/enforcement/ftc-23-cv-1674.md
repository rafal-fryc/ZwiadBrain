## Summary

The FTC charged Instant Checkmate, LLC and TruthFinder, LLC with making deceptive claims about the accuracy of their consumer background reports, falsely implying searched individuals had criminal records, offering fake data-correction tools, and soliciting undisclosed paid reviews. Both companies, operated as a common enterprise under parent PubRec, LLC, also violated multiple provisions of the Fair Credit Reporting Act as consumer reporting agencies. A consent order was finalized on October 15, 2023, imposing a $5.8 million civil penalty and permanent injunctions.

## Background

Instant Checkmate, LLC and TruthFinder, LLC sold consumer background report subscriptions through their respective websites while operating as a common enterprise under parent PubRec, LLC. As consumer reporting agencies, they were subject to the Fair Credit Reporting Act's requirements governing accuracy, permissible purposes, user notices, and dispute reinvestigation.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged eleven counts spanning deceptive practices and FCRA violations. Defendants falsely advertised their reports as the most accurate information available to the public, and deceptively implied that searched individuals may have criminal or arrest records to drive subscription purchases. They displayed 'Remove' and 'Flag as Inaccurate' buttons that created a false impression consumers could correct inaccurate data, when in fact no investigation or correction ever occurred. They also solicited reviews on the third-party site HighYa by offering free premium report credits without requiring reviewers to disclose that material connection, misrepresenting those reviews as unbiased. On the FCRA side, counts covered failure to maintain permissible-purpose procedures, furnishing reports without a permissible purpose, failure to assure maximum possible accuracy, failure to follow employment-screening certification requirements, failure to provide required User Notices, and failure to conduct reasonable reinvestigations of consumer disputes.

## Outcome

The finalized consent order entered a joint and several civil penalty of $5,800,000 payable to the U.S. Treasury. Settling Defendants were permanently enjoined from FCRA non-compliance, from misrepresentations about report accuracy, criminal-record probability, and data-correction mechanisms, and from misrepresentations about endorser independence. They must establish a comprehensive FCRA monitoring program before advertising or selling any covered report, implement endorser-disclosure compliance steps within 90 days, and maintain records for 15 years with 5-year retention periods. The court retained continuing jurisdiction for enforcement.

## Significance

This action established that background-report subscription services acting as consumer reporting agencies face combined FTC Act and FCRA liability when their marketing deceptively implies searched individuals have criminal records and when fake dispute tools mislead consumers about their data-correction rights. The $5.8 million civil penalty and permanent injunctions against deceptive accuracy claims and undisclosed paid endorsements signal the FTC's willingness to pursue dark-pattern and endorsement-disclosure violations alongside FCRA structural requirements in the consumer-data industry.
