## Summary

The FTC charged Cerebral, Inc. and its CEO Kyle Robertson with secretly sharing millions of patients' sensitive mental health and personal health data with more than twenty advertising platforms — including Facebook, Google, and TikTok — while falsely promising confidential, secure care. The company also failed to implement basic data-security safeguards, resulting in multiple breaches, and used dark-pattern cancellation flows to trap subscribers. A consent order requires Cerebral to pay over $15 million in combined monetary relief and civil penalties and imposes a 20-year compliance program.

## Background

Cerebral operated a telehealth subscription service offering mental health and substance-use-disorder treatment. Between at least October 2019 and March 2023 it shared patient data with advertising platforms without consent, suffered multiple data breaches, fabricated and suppressed consumer reviews, and designed cancellation flows that collected more than $8 million from consumers who had already requested cancellation. Robertson subsequently launched Zealthy (later Gronk) and Bruno Health, which committed similar negative-option violations.

## Allegations

The FTC's nine-count complaint alleged: deceptive privacy practices (falsely promising confidentiality and no marketing use of personal health information); deceptive data-security practices (falsely claiming PHI was secured); unfair privacy and data-security practices; deceptive cancellation practices ('Cancel anytime' misrepresentation); unfair and deceptive consumer-review practices (fabricated and incentivized reviews); violations of the Opioid Addiction Recovery Fraud Prevention Act in connection with substance-use-disorder services; ROSCA violations for Cerebral's negative-option subscription; and parallel unfair/deceptive and ROSCA counts against Robertson, Echeverry, Zealthy, and Bruno Health.

## Outcome

The court entered a consent order requiring Cerebral to pay $5,087,252.89 in monetary relief and a $10,000,000 civil penalty ($2 million paid immediately, remainder suspended based on financial condition). Cerebral must delete consumer data not needed for treatment or healthcare operations, post prescribed notices to affected consumers, establish a comprehensive privacy and information security program within 60 days, obtain biennial independent third-party assessments for 20 years, provide a simple cancellation mechanism, and obtain affirmative express consent before any future data disclosure for marketing. The order runs for 20 years.

## Significance

This action is one of the FTC's largest telehealth-privacy enforcement matters, establishing that pixel-tracking technologies used to share sensitive mental-health data with advertising platforms constitutes a deceptive and unfair practice. It also demonstrates the agency's willingness to pursue individual executives and successor companies, and extends the Health Breach Notification framework to a subscription telehealth context alongside ROSCA dark-pattern liability.
