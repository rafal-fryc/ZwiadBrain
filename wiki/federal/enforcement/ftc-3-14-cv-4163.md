## Summary

The FTC charged Yelp Inc. with violating COPPA after its app registration feature accepted sign-ups from children under 13 for four years without parental notice or consent. From April 2009 to April 2013, Yelp collected personal information from several thousand children through both iOS and Android versions of its app. Yelp settled the charges by paying a $450,000 civil penalty and agreeing to a 10-year consent order.

## Background

Yelp introduced an in-app registration feature in 2009 that lacked a functional age-screening mechanism, allowing children under 13 to register. Both iOS and Android versions of the Yelp App accepted registrations from users whose inputted birthdates indicated they were under 13, resulting in the collection of personal information from several thousand children without parental consent or required notices.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three COPPA violations: (1) failure to provide sufficient notice on its website or online services of information collected from children and how it is used; (2) failure to provide direct notice to parents of such collection and use practices; and (3) failure to obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting, using, or disclosing personal information from children.

## Outcome

Yelp paid a $450,000 civil penalty and was permanently enjoined from future COPPA violations. The company was also required to destroy all personal information previously collected from children under 13 within 30 days, and to comply with 10-year compliance reporting, recordkeeping, and monitoring obligations.

## Significance

The action illustrates FTC enforcement of COPPA against a major consumer-facing platform for a multi-year lapse in age-gating, resulting in one of the notable COPPA civil penalties of the era and a broad permanent injunction covering all future children's data collection practices.
