## Summary

The FTC charged Tricolor Auto Acceptance, LLC, an auto-loan servicer, with violating the Fair Credit Reporting Act's Furnisher Rule by failing to establish any written accuracy policies for consumer credit information it reported to credit bureaus, and by failing to reasonably investigate direct consumer disputes. The matter was resolved by a consent order entered in the Northern District of Texas requiring a civil penalty and a 20-year compliance regime.

## Developments

- 2015-09-15 — Consent order entered against Tricolor Auto Acceptance, LLC for FCRA Furnisher Rule violations — no written accuracy policies and failure to investigate direct consumer disputes — imposing an $82,777 civil penalty and a 20-year compliance regime. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/142-3073-tricolor-auto-acceptance-llc)

## Background

Tricolor Auto Acceptance services consumer auto loans and regularly furnishes credit information to consumer reporting agencies. It had no written policies or procedures regarding the accuracy and integrity of information furnished, and when consumers disputed account information directly, TAA forwarded the disputes to the credit reporting agency without conducting any investigation of its own. These deficiencies affected 11,635 unique consumer accounts.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged that TAA failed to establish and implement reasonable written policies and procedures regarding the accuracy and integrity of consumer information furnished to credit reporting agencies, as required by the FCRA Furnisher Rule. Count 2 alleged that TAA failed to conduct reasonable investigations of direct consumer disputes, failed to review relevant information provided by consumers, and failed to timely complete investigations and report results.

## Outcome

TAA was permanently enjoined from failing to comply with FCRA furnisher regulations and was required to pay a civil penalty of $82,777 to the United States within 7 days of the order's entry. The order also imposed 20-year compliance reporting and record-keeping obligations, and authorized the FTC to conduct ongoing compliance monitoring.

## Significance

The action illustrates that even smaller auto-loan servicers face civil penalty exposure under the FCRA Furnisher Rule for the foundational failure of having no written accuracy policies, and that forwarding disputes without any internal investigation is itself a cognizable violation — not merely inadequate investigation.
