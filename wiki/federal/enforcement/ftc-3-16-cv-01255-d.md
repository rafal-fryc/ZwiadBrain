## Summary

The FTC charged Credit Protection Association, LP, a Texas-based debt collector, with violating the FCRA Furnisher Rule by failing to maintain adequate written policies for the accuracy of consumer data it reported to credit bureaus and failing to complete dispute investigations within the required 30-day window. The matter was resolved through a consent order entered in federal court. CPA was fined $72,000 and subjected to a 20-year compliance regime.

## Developments

- 2016-05-15 — Consent order entered against Credit Protection Association for FCRA Furnisher Rule violations — failure to maintain written accuracy policies and complete consumer dispute investigations on time — with a $72,000 civil penalty and 20-year compliance obligations. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/142-3142-credit-protection-association)

## Background

Credit Protection Association, LP (CPA) primarily served cable service providers as a third-party debt collector and regularly furnished consumer account information to Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax. Its three-page policies document failed to incorporate required guidelines from Appendix E of the Furnisher Rule, omitting provisions for staff training, recordkeeping, dispute investigations, and periodic self-evaluation. CPA routinely destroyed dispute letters and supporting documents before disputes were resolved, transmitted only minimal coded information to creditor-clients who were then left to investigate on their own, and had no policy requiring it to notify consumers of investigation results within the 30-day FCRA deadline.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged that CPA failed to establish and implement reasonable written policies and procedures regarding the accuracy and integrity of consumer information it furnished to consumer reporting agencies, and failed to consider Appendix E guidelines or periodically review its policies. Count 2 alleged that CPA failed to complete investigations of direct consumer disputes and report results to consumers within the 30-day period required by FCRA Section 611(a)(1).

## Outcome

A consent order was entered in the U.S. District Court (Docket 3:16-cv-01255-D). CPA was permanently enjoined from failing to comply with FCRA furnisher accuracy and dispute-investigation requirements. A civil penalty of $72,000 was imposed, payable in four installments of $18,000 over nine months. CPA is subject to 20 years of recordkeeping, compliance reporting, and monitoring obligations.

## Significance

The action illustrates FTC enforcement of the Furnisher Rule's affirmative accuracy and dispute-resolution obligations on debt collectors that supply data to credit bureaus, underscoring that downstream consumer-reporting accuracy — not just initial data collection — is a cognizable privacy enforcement target.
