## Summary

The FTC charged InMobi Pte Ltd., a mobile advertising network, with secretly tracking users' locations via WiFi network data even when consumers had explicitly disabled location permissions, and with collecting personal information from children across thousands of apps without parental consent in violation of COPPA. The matter was resolved through a consent order requiring a $4 million civil penalty (with $950,000 actually paid), permanent injunctions on location tracking without consent and on COPPA violations, deletion of improperly collected data, and a 20-year comprehensive privacy program with third-party assessments.

## Background

InMobi operated a mobile advertising network and represented to app developers and consumers that it would only track users' locations and serve geo-targeted ads when consumers had granted permission through device location settings. In practice, InMobi collected WiFi network information (BSSIDs) to infer location even when consumers had disabled location permissions. The company also represented that it did not collect personal information from child-directed apps, while in fact collecting such data from thousands of children's apps without parental consent.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged four counts: (1) InMobi falsely represented it tracked location only when the app developer and consumer had granted access via Android and iOS location APIs; (2) InMobi falsely represented it tracked location only with opt-in consumer consent; (3) InMobi falsely represented it did not collect personal information from child-directed applications; and (4) InMobi violated the COPPA Rule by operating an advertising network that collected personal information from child-directed services with actual knowledge of that fact.

## Outcome

A consent order was entered imposing a $4 million civil penalty judgment, of which $950,000 was to be paid in installments with the remainder suspended subject to InMobi's financial representations. InMobi was permanently enjoined from violating COPPA, from collecting location information without affirmative express consent, and from misrepresenting its privacy practices. The company was required to delete all improperly collected children's personal information and location data within 10 days, establish a comprehensive written privacy program, and submit to biennial third-party privacy assessments for 20 years.

## Significance

The action was a notable early enforcement combining COPPA violations with deceptive location-tracking practices in the mobile advertising ecosystem, establishing that covert inference of location through WiFi signals — bypassing device-level permission controls — constitutes an unfair and deceptive practice. The 20-year compliance regime and requirement for third-party privacy assessments set a high-water mark for FTC oversight of mobile ad networks.
