## Summary

The FTC charged RealPage, Inc., a tenant background screening company, with violating the Fair Credit Reporting Act by using overly broad, inaccurate matching criteria that caused criminal records belonging to other individuals to appear in tenant screening reports. These failures potentially led to innocent housing applicants being denied housing. The matter was resolved through a consent order entered in federal district court.

## Developments

- 2018-10-15 — Consent order entered against RealPage requiring $3,000,000 civil penalty and permanent injunction for FCRA violations arising from inaccurate criminal-record matching in tenant screening reports. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/152-3059-realpage-inc)

## Background

RealPage, Inc. operated as a consumer reporting agency providing tenant screening reports. From at least January 1, 2012 through September 19, 2017, it used soft (non-exact) matching on first names, middle names, and dates of birth with minimal filtering, and had no policies to assess the accuracy of results even when reports contained records clearly belonging to multiple different individuals. RealPage continued these practices despite receiving consumer disputes about inaccurate reporting.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that RealPage violated FCRA Section 607(b) by failing to maintain reasonable procedures to assure the maximum possible accuracy of consumer report information. Specifically, the company used overly broad matching criteria, applied only minimal filters, and lacked any policies to assess the accuracy of results before transmitting tenant screening reports to clients, causing wrong individuals' criminal records to be included in reports.

## Outcome

RealPage was permanently enjoined from failing to maintain reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy of consumer report information. The company was also ordered to pay a $3,000,000 civil penalty within 7 days of the order's entry, and was subjected to 10 years of compliance reporting and recordkeeping obligations and ongoing monitoring by the FTC.

## Significance

This action illustrates FCRA accuracy obligations as a privacy enforcement tool: algorithmic or automated matching logic in consumer reporting — even when framed as a technical design choice — must produce accurate results, and companies cannot avoid liability by citing the difficulty of matching. The $3 million civil penalty signals that systemic inaccuracy affecting housing decisions, which is a high-stakes context, will draw substantial monetary consequences.
