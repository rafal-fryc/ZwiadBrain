## Summary

The FTC charged HyperBeard, Inc. and an individual officer with violating COPPA by operating child-directed mobile apps that allowed eleven third-party advertising networks to collect persistent identifiers from children under 13 for behavioral advertising without parental notice or consent. A federal consent order was entered imposing a $4 million civil penalty judgment, with $150,000 actually paid and the remainder suspended, along with permanent injunctive relief and data deletion requirements.

## Background

HyperBeard operated a suite of child-directed mobile apps—including KleptoCats, Chichens, and BunnyBuns—featuring animated characters, simple gameplay, and child-friendly marketing. Until August 2019, when contacted by FTC staff, HyperBeard never informed its eleven advertising network partners that the apps were child-directed, never instructed them to refrain from behavioral advertising, and never posted required privacy notices, provided direct notice to parents, or obtained verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information from children.

## Allegations

Defendants operated online services directed to children and collected—or caused to be collected on their behalf—personal information in the form of persistent identifiers from children under age 13 through third-party advertising networks, in violation of the COPPA Rule (16 C.F.R. Part 312).

## Outcome

A federal consent order was entered requiring HyperBeard and individual defendant Kozachenko to pay a $4,000,000 civil penalty jointly and severally, with $150,000 due within 7 days and the balance suspended subject to conditions. Defendants were permanently enjoined from future COPPA violations and required to delete all pre-order children's personal information within 10 days. Defendants must maintain compliance records for 10 years and submit to Commission monitoring.

## Significance

The action illustrates the FTC's enforcement posture that app developers bear affirmative responsibility under COPPA to notify downstream advertising partners when their platforms are child-directed, and cannot avoid liability by passively allowing third-party data collection without disclosure or parental consent.
