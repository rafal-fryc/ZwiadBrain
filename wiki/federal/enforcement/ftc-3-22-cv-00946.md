## Summary

The FTC charged Kurbo, Inc. and WW International, Inc. with violating COPPA by collecting personal information from children under 13 without adequate parental notice or verifiable consent through their Kurbo by WW weight-management app. The app's age-gate was non-neutral and effectively invited underage self-registration, and children's data was retained indefinitely. The matter was resolved via a court-entered consent order imposing a $1.5 million civil penalty and permanent injunctive relief.

## Background

Kurbo, Inc. and WW International, Inc. operated Kurbo by WW, a weight-management app and website marketed to children as young as 8. Since at least 2014, the app collected names, email addresses, phone numbers, height, weight, food intake, activity data, and persistent device identifiers from children under 13. The age-gate was designed in a way that signaled children could register by falsely claiming to be 13 or older; hundreds of users who had lied about their age later corrected it to under 13 yet retained account access. Privacy notices, when eventually added, were buried in hyperlinks and incomplete, and no meaningful parental consent mechanism existed for years. Children's personal information was also retained indefinitely until August 2021.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of violating the COPPA Rule: Defendants operated an online service directed to children and collected personal information from children under 13 — including names, email addresses, phone numbers, height, weight, food intake, activity data, and persistent device identifiers — without providing direct notice to parents and without obtaining verifiable parental consent, in violation of the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act and its implementing rule.

## Outcome

A federal court entered a consent order permanently enjoining Defendants from failing to provide direct parental notice, failing to post required privacy links, failing to obtain verifiable parental consent, and retaining children's data longer than necessary. Defendants were required to destroy previously collected children's data without parental consent, delete affected work product, and maintain a compliant retention schedule. A civil penalty of $1.5 million was entered jointly and severally against both Defendants, payable within seven days. Ten-year recordkeeping, annual compliance reporting, and FTC compliance monitoring obligations were also imposed.

## Significance

The action is a prominent COPPA enforcement example targeting a children's health and wellness app, underscoring that non-neutral age-gates that effectively invite underage self-registration do not satisfy COPPA's parental-consent requirements. It also signals that indefinite retention of children's personal data is independently actionable and that remediation requires affirmative data destruction rather than merely ceasing collection.
