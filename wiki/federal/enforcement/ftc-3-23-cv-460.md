## Summary

The FTC charged GoodRx Holdings with repeatedly promising users it would never share their personal health information with advertisers, then secretly disclosing prescription drug names, health conditions, and personal identifiers to Facebook, Google, and Criteo for targeted advertising purposes. The complaint also alleged GoodRx violated the Health Breach Notification Rule by failing to notify users of unauthorized disclosures. The matter resolved via a consent order requiring a $1.5 million civil penalty, a permanent ban on sharing health data for advertising, mandatory deletion of improperly shared data, and a 20-year comprehensive privacy program.

## Background

GoodRx is a digital healthcare platform offering prescription discount products and telehealth services. From at least 2017 through 2020, GoodRx transmitted sensitive user health data — including prescription medications and health conditions — to advertising platforms without user notice or consent, and exploited that data for targeted health-related advertisements on Facebook and Instagram. GoodRx also failed to implement sufficient policies to prevent improper disclosure or to notify users of breaches.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged eight counts: five privacy misrepresentation counts (falsely claiming no health data would be shared with advertisers; misrepresenting limits on third-party use; falsely claiming compliance with Digital Advertising Alliance principles and HIPAA); two unfairness counts (failure to implement adequate policies preventing unauthorized health data disclosure; collecting and disclosing health data for advertising without notice or consent); and one count of violating the Health Breach Notification Rule by failing to notify users, the FTC, and media outlets after breaches affecting more than 500 consumers.

## Outcome

GoodRx entered a consent order permanently prohibiting disclosure of health information for advertising purposes and all related misrepresentations. The order requires affirmative express consent before any health data sharing, mandatory breach notifications, user notice of past practices, deletion of improperly shared data by third parties, a 20-year comprehensive privacy program with biennial third-party assessments, annual compliance certifications, and a civil penalty of $1,500,000 payable to the United States.

## Significance

This was the FTC's first enforcement action under the Health Breach Notification Rule, establishing that pixel-based transmission of health data to advertising platforms constitutes a reportable breach of security — and that consumer-facing health apps making broad HIPAA-like privacy promises are subject to FTC Act unfairness and deception liability even when not formally HIPAA-covered entities.
