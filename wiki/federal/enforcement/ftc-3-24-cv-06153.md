## Summary

The FTC charged Verkada Inc., a cloud-based building security camera company, with failing to implement reasonable data security practices, making false claims about HIPAA and Privacy Shield compliance, orchestrating undisclosed fake reviews, and repeatedly violating the CAN-SPAM Act. A 2021 breach exposed over 150,000 live customer cameras and gigabytes of sensitive data. Verkada settled via a consent order requiring a $2.95 million civil penalty, a 20-year information security program, biennial third-party assessments, and permanent injunctive relief.

## Developments

- 2024-08-15 — Consent order filed and finalized requiring Verkada to pay a $2.95 million civil penalty and maintain a 20-year security program following a 2021 breach and findings of false HIPAA/Privacy Shield claims and CAN-SPAM violations. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/2123068-verkada-inc-us-v)

## Background

Verkada sold cloud-connected security cameras to over 12,000 businesses, including schools and healthcare facilities. In March 2021, a threat actor obtained Super Admin access to Verkada's network and viewed more than 150,000 live customer cameras while exfiltrating gigabytes of sensitive customer and consumer data. Despite these security failures, Verkada falsely claimed HIPAA and Privacy Shield certification, falsely represented its products used industry-standard and best-in-class security safeguards, and arranged undisclosed positive reviews from its own employees and a venture capital investor on Google Maps.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged eleven counts: unfair information security practices as to both business customers and consumers, information security misrepresentations (including about the Command platform), false HIPAA compliance claims, false Privacy Shield compliance claims, false representations that online reviews reflected impartial customers, deceptive failure to disclose material connections behind reviews, and three CAN-SPAM violations (failure to honor opt-out requests, failure to provide opt-out notice, and failure to include a valid physical postal address in commercial emails).

## Outcome

Verkada agreed to a consent order entered as a civil judgment. The order imposes a $2,950,000 civil penalty payable to the U.S. Treasury, permanently prohibits misrepresentations about privacy and security practices and compliance certifications, requires establishment and maintenance of a comprehensive information security program for 20 years, mandates initial and biennial independent third-party security assessments pre-approved by the FTC, requires annual sworn compliance certifications and incident reports within 10 days of notifying any government entity of a covered incident, and permanently prohibits further CAN-SPAM violations.

## Significance

The action illustrates the FTC's willingness to pursue civil penalties under both the FTC Act and CAN-SPAM simultaneously against a B2B security technology vendor, and establishes that false compliance certifications (HIPAA, Privacy Shield) and inadequate security practices at a surveillance infrastructure provider warrant a 20-year oversight regime with mandatory independent assessments.
