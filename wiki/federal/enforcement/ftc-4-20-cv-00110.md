## Summary

The FTC charged California mortgage broker Mortgage Solutions FCS, Inc. and its owner Ramon Walker with publicly disclosing customers' sensitive financial and health information — including credit scores and medical conditions — in Yelp responses to negative reviews. The complaint also alleged failures to provide accurate privacy notices, impermissible use of consumer report data, and a years-long absence of any information security program. The matter resolved as a consent order carrying a $120,000 civil penalty and permanent injunctive relief.

## Background

Mortgage Solutions FCS, Inc., doing business as Mount Diablo Lending, is a California mortgage broker. Between approximately June 2015 and August 2016, owner Ramon Walker responded to negative Yelp reviews by publicly posting details drawn from customers' loan files and credit reports, including credit scores, debt-to-income ratios, family health conditions, and credit history. The company also distributed an inaccurate privacy notice from around October 2012 through April 2018, and operated without any information security program until September 2017; the program eventually adopted contained no mechanism for regularly testing its own effectiveness.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged seven counts: (1) failure to provide clear, conspicuous, and accurate privacy notices under Regulation P; (2) impermissible public disclosure of nonpublic personal information via Yelp responses without notice or opt-out opportunity; (3) impermissible use of consumer report information for purposes unrelated to any credit decision, in violation of the FCRA; (4) failure to develop and implement any information security program until September 2017; (5) adopting an inadequate security program with no testing or assessment provisions; (6) deceptive misrepresentation under FTC Act Section 5 that customer financial information would not be publicly disclosed; and (7) unfair public disclosure of sensitive financial information in response to critical online reviews.

## Outcome

The consent order permanently prohibits Mortgage Solutions and Walker from misrepresenting privacy and data security practices, using consumer reports for non-permissible purposes, and disclosing nonpublic personal information without proper notice or consent. It mandates a comprehensive information security program, biennial third-party assessments for ten years, annual compliance certifications, and prompt reporting of covered incidents. A civil penalty of $120,000 was entered jointly and severally against the defendants, payable to the U.S. Treasury within seven days of order entry.

## Significance

This action illustrates that publicly weaponizing customers' own credit and health data — in retaliation for negative reviews — constitutes both FCRA misuse and an unfair practice under the FTC Act, and that a mortgage broker's failure to maintain any security program for years is independently actionable under the GLBA Safeguards Rule. It also signals that small financial-services firms are subject to the same data-security assessment and testing obligations as larger institutions.
