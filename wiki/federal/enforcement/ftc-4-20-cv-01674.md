## Summary

The FTC charged Midwest Recovery Systems and its co-owner officers with collecting debts consumers did not owe and 'parking' over $98 million in unsubstantiated debts on consumer credit reports without first notifying those consumers. The complaint alleged violations of the FTC Act, FDCPA, and FCRA, including furnishing inaccurate information to credit bureaus and failing to investigate disputes. A consent order entered November 2020 imposed a $24.3 million judgment and permanent injunctions against the unlawful practices.

## Background

Since at least 2015, Midwest Recovery Systems pursued consumers for phantom payday loans, counterfeit debts, and unverifiable medical debts. Each month, the company determined 80–97% of disputed debts to be inaccurate or invalid yet continued collecting on them, generating an estimated $24.3 million in gross revenues. The firm reported debts to consumer reporting agencies before ever contacting the consumers those debts concerned, causing credit score drops and jeopardizing home purchases and employment.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged seven counts: (1) making false or unsubstantiated claims that consumers owed debts; (2) using false or misleading representations in collection under FDCPA § 807; (3) unfair debt parking — furnishing debt information to CRAs before communicating with consumers — under FDCPA § 808; (4) failing to provide required validation notices under FDCPA § 809(a); (5) furnishing inaccurate information to CRAs under FCRA § 623(a)(1)(A); (6) failing to conduct reasonable investigations of direct disputes under FCRA § 623(a)(8)(E)(i); and (7) failing to report investigation results to consumers under FCRA § 623(a)(8)(E)(iii).

## Outcome

A $24.3 million judgment was entered jointly and severally against all defendants, with a portion suspended upon completion of specified asset transfers and cash payments. Defendants were permanently enjoined from debt parking, furnishing inaccurate credit information, using deceptive collection tactics, and violating the FDCPA and FCRA Furnisher Rule. The Corporate Defendant was required to request deletion of all previously reported debts from consumer credit files within 10 days.

## Significance

This action marked the FTC's first litigated case explicitly targeting 'debt parking' as an unfair practice under the FDCPA, establishing that furnishing debts to CRAs before notifying consumers is independently actionable and that $98 million in credit-report entries can constitute consumer harm warranting full disgorgement.
