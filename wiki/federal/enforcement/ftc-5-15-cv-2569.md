## Summary

The FTC charged Retro Dreamer, a California mobile app developer, with violating COPPA by allowing third-party ad networks to collect persistent identifiers from child users of its kids' apps for targeted advertising without parental notice or consent. The company continued this practice even after being notified by an ad network that its apps appeared child-directed. The matter was resolved through a federal court consent order imposing a $300,000 civil penalty and a permanent injunction against future COPPA violations.

## Background

Retro Dreamer operated numerous children's apps — including Ice Cream Jump, Happy Pudding Jump, Ice Cream Drop, Sneezies, Wash the Dishes, Cat Basket, and Tappy Pop — directed to children under age 13. Despite being notified in November 2013 by at least one ad network that certain apps appeared child-directed, Defendants continued allowing other ad networks to collect personal information from child users without required notices or verifiable parental consent.

## Allegations

Retro Dreamer violated the COPPA Rule by operating child-directed apps that allowed third-party advertising networks to collect persistent identifiers from children under 13 for targeted advertising, while failing to post required privacy notices, failing to provide direct notice to parents, and failing to obtain verifiable parental consent prior to collection.

## Outcome

A federal court consent order was entered imposing a $300,000 civil penalty payable within 7 days, a permanent injunction prohibiting future COPPA Rule violations, and ongoing compliance obligations including recordkeeping for 10 years, annual compliance reporting for one year post-order, and 14-day notice requirements for structural changes for 10 years.

## Significance

This action illustrates the FTC's enforcement posture that knowledge of child-directed app status — even informal notice from a business partner — triggers COPPA obligations, and that continued non-compliance after such notice warrants civil penalties alongside permanent injunctive relief.
