## Summary

The FTC charged UNIXIZ, Inc. (operating as i-Dressup.com) and its officers with violating COPPA by collecting personal information from over 245,000 children without verifiable parental consent and without adequate notice. The defendants also maintained grossly inadequate data security practices, which allowed a hacker to access the personal information of approximately 2.1 million users in August 2016. The matter resolved in a consent order requiring a $35,000 civil penalty, a permanent injunction against COPPA violations, and a mandated information security program with third-party assessments for ten years.

## Background

UNIXIZ, Inc. operated i-Dressup.com, a children's dress-up gaming and social website. Defendants failed to provide legally sufficient privacy notices, failed to obtain verifiable parental consent, and retained parental contact information indefinitely. Their data security practices were grossly inadequate — passwords and personal data were stored in clear text, no SQL injection vulnerability assessments were conducted, and no intrusion detection systems were in place. A hacker exploited these weaknesses in August 2016 and accessed personal information of approximately 2,125,000 users, including 245,000 children. Defendants did not discover the breach until the hacker sent the stolen data to journalists.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of COPPA Rule violations: defendants collected, used, and disclosed personal information from children under 13 through a website directed to children while purporting to comply with COPPA requirements for notice, verifiable parental consent, data deletion, and data security obligations.

## Outcome

The consent order entered a $35,000 civil penalty judgment against defendants jointly and severally. Defendants were permanently enjoined from violating COPPA and required to establish a comprehensive information security program, obtain biennial third-party security assessments by a qualified assessor for ten years, submit annual compliance certifications, and maintain detailed recordkeeping for ten years.

## Significance

The action illustrates the FTC's dual enforcement focus under COPPA — inadequate parental consent procedures and deficient data security practices are treated as interrelated violations. The case also highlights that a data breach discovered only because the attacker contacted journalists can serve as direct evidence of grossly inadequate security, strengthening the agency's liability case.
