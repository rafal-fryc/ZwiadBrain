## Summary

The FTC charged Epic Games, developer of Fortnite, with violating COPPA by collecting personal information from child players without parental notice or consent, and with causing unfair harm by enabling on-by-default voice and text chat that exposed children and teens to bullying, harassment, and grooming. Epic agreed to a consent order requiring deletion of previously collected children's data, opt-in privacy defaults for minors, a 20-year privacy program with third-party audits, and a $275 million civil penalty. The order was finalized in February 2023.

## Background

Epic Games launched Fortnite in 2017 and, despite internal survey data, support tickets, and merchandising programs confirming large numbers of child players, took no COPPA compliance steps for over two years. Employees repeatedly flagged harms from default-on chat features but leadership resisted meaningful reforms. Limited age-gating introduced in September 2019 was found insufficient to cure the violations.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged Epic violated the COPPA Rule by collecting, using, and disclosing personal information from children under 13 without required parental notice, verifiable parental consent, or a mechanism for parents to review or delete their children's data. Count 2 alleged Epic acted unfairly by operating Fortnite with on-by-default voice and text chat and public display name broadcasting for all users, including children and teens, without adequate safeguards against bullying, sexual harassment, predatory grooming, and psychologically harmful content.

## Outcome

Epic entered a consent order permanently enjoining COPPA violations, requiring deletion of previously collected children's personal information, mandating opt-in affirmative consent before disclosing children's or teens' information to other users or enabling chat, and imposing a comprehensive written privacy program with biennial third-party assessments for 20 years. A civil penalty of $275,000,000 was entered against Epic, payable to the U.S. Treasury within seven days of order entry.

## Significance

At $275 million, this was the largest COPPA civil penalty in FTC history at the time of issuance. The order's prohibition on on-by-default chat for minors and its requirement of affirmative express consent before enabling child-to-user communications extended COPPA enforcement beyond data collection into product design defaults, signaling FTC scrutiny of dark patterns and harmful default settings in children's products.
