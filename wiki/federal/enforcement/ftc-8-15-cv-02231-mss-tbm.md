## Summary

The FTC sued Roca Labs for falsely claiming its dietary supplement had a scientifically proven 90% weight-loss success rate comparable to gastric bypass surgery, suppressing negative customer reviews through contractual non-disparagement clauses, operating a fake independent review website, failing to disclose paid testimonials, and misrepresenting that consumer health information would be kept confidential. A consent order entered a $25,246,000 monetary judgment against the corporate defendants and individual Don Juravin, and imposed a 20-year injunction covering false health claims, review suppression, undisclosed endorsements, and privacy misrepresentations.

## Background

Roca Labs marketed dietary supplement powders as a non-surgical gastric bypass alternative, claiming a scientifically proven 90% success rate and weight loss of up to 100 pounds. The company paid consumers to submit positive testimonials without disclosure, ran GastricBypass.me as a covertly owned fake-independent review site, embedded non-disparagement gag clauses in sales contracts, and sued or threatened customers who posted truthful negative reviews. Roca Labs also promised to keep purchaser health information confidential but disclosed it in public court filings and to payment processors.

## Allegations

The FTC charged seven counts: (1) deceptive weight-loss efficacy claims; (2) false establishment claim of a scientifically proven 90% success rate; (3) unfair use of non-disparagement contract provisions and legal threats to silence customers; (4) misrepresenting GastricBypass.me as an independent resource; (5) failure to disclose material connections between paid endorsers and the company; (6) deceptive privacy claim that consumer health information would be kept confidential; and (7) a deceptive discount clause threatening customers with a higher 'full price' if they posted negative reviews.

## Outcome

A consent order entered a $25,246,000 monetary judgment jointly and severally against the corporate defendants and individual defendant Don Juravin. Defendants are permanently enjoined from making unsubstantiated weight-loss or health claims, using non-disparagement clauses or threatening customers over reviews, misrepresenting the independence of any publication, failing to disclose material connections with endorsers, and misrepresenting consumer information confidentiality practices. The order carries 20-year compliance reporting and recordkeeping obligations.

## Significance

The case combines two distinct enforcement threads: a deceptive-health-claims action and a privacy misrepresentation action, the latter arising from Roca Labs disclosing consumer health information in court filings after promising confidentiality. The permanent prohibition on non-disparagement clauses and its linkage to the Consumer Review Fairness Act framework set a precedent for treating review-suppression contracts as an unfair practice under Section 5.
