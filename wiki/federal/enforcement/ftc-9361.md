## Summary

The FTC charged Jerk, LLC and John Fanning with deceiving consumers by misrepresenting that profiles on Jerk.com were created by other users and that a paid membership fee would give profiled individuals meaningful ability to dispute content. A Final Order was issued on March 13, 2015, and a subsequent amended order in 2018 revised the compliance monitoring provision applicable to Fanning. The matter involved the online collection and use of consumer data linked to specific individuals.

## Developments

- 2017-11-02 — Amended Final Order issued revising compliance monitoring provision for John Fanning following First Circuit remand in deceptive-profile-data case against Jerk.com. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/122-3141-jerk-llc-dba-jerkcom-matter)

## Background

Jerk.com displayed profiles containing names and photographs of individuals, which respondents represented were created by the site's users and reflected those users' opinions. Respondents also charged consumers a $30 membership fee with the promise of dispute rights over posted content. The underlying case went through FTC adjudication and a First Circuit Court of Appeals decision before returning to the Commission on remand.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged that respondents misrepresented that profile content on Jerk.com — including names and photographs harvested from Facebook — was created by users and reflected their views. Count 2 alleged that respondents misrepresented that consumers who paid a $30 membership fee would receive meaningful benefits, including the ability to dispute information posted about them on the site.

## Outcome

A Final Order was issued on March 13, 2015. In 2018, the Commission amended Section VI of that order on remand, replacing the compliance monitoring provision to require John Fanning to notify the FTC for five years whenever he affiliates with any new business involving electronic commerce, social media, or the online collection or use of consumer data linked to a specific consumer, computer, or device. All other provisions of the original Final Order remained in effect.

## Significance

The case illustrates FTC enforcement against platforms that harvest and display personal data about individuals under false pretenses, including misrepresentations about user-generated content and consumer dispute rights. The amended monitoring provision targeting Fanning's future business activity reflects the FTC's use of individual-level compliance obligations in data-related deception cases.
