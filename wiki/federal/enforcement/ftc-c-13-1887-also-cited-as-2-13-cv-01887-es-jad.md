## Summary

The FTC charged Wyndham Worldwide Corporation and its hotel subsidiaries with deceptive and unfair data security practices after three separate breaches between 2008 and 2010 compromised more than 619,000 consumer payment card numbers and caused over $10.6 million in fraud losses. The FTC alleged that Wyndham falsely represented on its website that it used industry-standard, commercially reasonable security practices while in fact failing to maintain adequate safeguards. The matter was litigated through the Third Circuit, which in 2015 upheld the FTC's Section 5 unfairness authority over corporate data security, and was resolved by a consent order requiring a 20-year information security program.

## Background

Between April 2008 and January 2010, intruders breached Wyndham's networks on three separate occasions, each time gaining entry through the Phoenix, Arizona data center. The attackers exported hundreds of thousands of payment card numbers to a domain registered in Russia. Despite the first and second breaches, Wyndham failed to take adequate remedial action, allowing subsequent intrusions using the same malware and techniques. Wyndham operated a network of approximately ninety branded hotel properties whose cardholder data environments were at issue.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged deceptive data security misrepresentation: Wyndham publicly represented that it employed reasonable and appropriate measures to protect consumers' personal information when it had not done so. Count 2 alleged unfair data security practices: Wyndham failed to employ reasonable and appropriate security measures to protect personal information against unauthorized access, resulting in three breaches and over $10.6 million in consumer fraud losses.

## Outcome

A consent order was entered on December 15, 2015. Wyndham Hotels and Resorts was required to establish and maintain a comprehensive information security program for 20 years, obtain annual compliance assessments against an approved security standard, commission a forensic investigator report following any large breach, maintain records for Commission review, and submit ongoing compliance reports. Parent entities Wyndham Worldwide Corporation and Wyndham Hotel Group LLC were obligated to ensure the subsidiary's compliance for as long as it remains their subsidiary. The court retained jurisdiction for modification and enforcement.

## Significance

This consent order capped the landmark Wyndham litigation in which the Third Circuit (2015) became the first federal appellate court to affirm the FTC's authority under Section 5's unfairness prong to bring data security enforcement actions against private companies, establishing that inadequate corporate data security can constitute an unfair act or practice under the FTC Act.
