## Summary

The FTC charged GoDaddy with operating a fundamentally deficient data security program while publicly marketing itself as a secure, award-winning hosting provider. Despite certifying compliance with EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield Frameworks, the company failed to implement basic controls such as multi-factor authentication, patch management, and network segmentation, leading to multiple major data compromises between 2019 and 2022. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order requiring a comprehensive information security program and biennial third-party assessments.

- 2025-05-15 — FTC issued a 20-year consent order against GoDaddy for chronic data security failures and misrepresentations about its hosting security practices and Privacy Shield compliance. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/2023133-godaddy-inc-et-al-matter)

## Background

GoDaddy is one of the world's largest website hosting companies. Since at least 2018, it failed to implement basic security controls across its Shared Hosting environment. Threat actors repeatedly accessed customers' websites, credentials, encryption keys, and payment card data in incidents spanning 2019 through December 2022. GoDaddy also certified compliance with the Security Principle of the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield Frameworks while failing to maintain required data protection measures.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts: (1) unfair data security practices — failing to employ reasonable and appropriate measures to protect the Shared Hosting environment from unauthorized access; (2) data security misrepresentations — falsely representing that it used reasonable and appropriate security measures; and (3) Privacy Shield misrepresentations — falsely claiming adherence to the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield Principles, including the Security Principle.

## Outcome

GoDaddy agreed to a 20-year consent order prohibiting misrepresentations about its security practices, requiring establishment of a comprehensive information security program within 90 days, mandating biennial independent third-party security assessments for 20 years, requiring annual senior-executive compliance certifications, and imposing incident reporting, recordkeeping, and FTC monitoring obligations.

## Significance

The action underscores the FTC's willingness to pursue major consumer internet infrastructure providers for security theater — marketing robust security while operating deficient programs. The 20-year order with mandatory third-party assessments and Privacy Shield-specific counts signals that cross-framework compliance certifications carry enforceable obligations under Section 5.
