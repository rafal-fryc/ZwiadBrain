## Summary

The FTC charged Upromise, Inc. with violating a 2012 consent order by burying required disclosures about its RewardU toolbar's data collection in small, hard-to-read text and by submitting sham third-party compliance assessments that failed to properly evaluate the toolbar. The action resulted in a $500,000 civil penalty and a new order requiring corrective measures. The consent order was finalized in 2017.

## Developments

- 2017-03-15 — FTC obtained a $500,000 civil penalty consent order against Upromise for violating its 2012 order by burying RewardU toolbar data-collection disclosures and submitting sham third-party compliance assessments. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/102-3116-c-4351-upromise-inc)

## Background

Upromise distributed the RewardU toolbar from March 2013 to January 2016, which collected detailed information about consumers' online activities and search queries. A prior FTC order (Docket No. C-4351, March 2012) required Upromise to clearly and prominently disclose its data collection practices prior to download on a separate screen, and to obtain compliant third-party assessments covering any targeting tool.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged two violations of the 2012 order: (1) Upromise buried its RewardU data collection disclosures two full screens below the fold in small, light-gray, footnote-style text that was difficult to notice or understand, and the disclosure was further undermined by conflicting privacy policies from third-party vendor Rakuten; and (2) the Second and Third Fishnet Assessments Upromise submitted to satisfy the order's third-party assessment requirement mentioned RewardU only superficially and failed to certify adequate protection of consumer data as required.

## Outcome

A civil penalty judgment of $500,000 was entered against Upromise. The company was permanently enjoined from violating the 2012 FTC order, required to expire all RewardU-related cookies and notify affected consumers to uninstall the toolbar within 30 days, required to obtain FTC-approved third-party evaluations before making any targeting tool available, and subjected to 20 years of compliance reporting and recordkeeping obligations.

## Significance

This action illustrates the FTC's willingness to pursue civil penalties for order violations involving inadequate privacy disclosures, establishing that burying required disclosures in fine print or obtaining superficial compliance assessments does not satisfy consent order obligations. It reinforces that prior FTC orders carry real enforcement weight and that companies cannot satisfy assessment requirements through pro forma reviews.
