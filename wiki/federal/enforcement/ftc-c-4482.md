## Summary

The FTC charged GMR Transcription Services with falsely claiming it implemented reasonable security measures and HIPAA-compliant safeguards for medical and non-medical transcription files. In reality, thousands of medical transcripts containing sensitive personal information were stored in plain text on a publicly accessible FTP server managed by a third-party provider, Fedtrans. The matter resolved in a 20-year consent order requiring a comprehensive information security program and biennial third-party assessments.

## Background

GMR Transcription Services provided transcription services for medical and non-medical audio files, claiming reasonable security and HIPAA compliance. The company failed to implement basic security safeguards and did not adequately oversee its service provider, Fedtrans, resulting in thousands of medical transcript files being stored and transmitted in clear readable text on an unsecured FTP server publicly accessible via search engines.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts: (1) misrepresentation that GMR implemented reasonable and appropriate security measures to prevent unauthorized access to personal information; (2) misrepresentation that GMR took reasonable measures to oversee its service providers' security practices; and (3) unfair security practices for failing to employ reasonable and appropriate measures to prevent unauthorized access to personal information in audio and transcript files.

## Outcome

A 20-year consent order (Docket C-4482) was issued requiring GMR to cease misrepresentations about privacy and security, establish and maintain a comprehensive information security program, obtain biennial third-party security assessments for 20 years, maintain compliance records, and notify the FTC of material business changes. Individual respondents Prasad and Srivastava must notify the Commission of personal status changes for 10 years. The order terminates August 14, 2034.

## Significance

This action illustrates the FTC's application of Section 5 unfairness and deception theories to medical transcription service providers that outsource data handling without adequate vendor oversight, and reinforces that publicly accessible plain-text storage of sensitive health information — even by a downstream contractor — exposes the primary service provider to FTC enforcement.
