## Summary

The FTC charged Snapchat with six counts of deceptive practices, alleging the company falsely claimed messages disappeared permanently, that senders received screenshot notifications, and that it did not collect location data, while also failing to secure user information. A database breach exposed 4.6 million usernames and phone numbers. Snapchat settled via a 20-year consent order requiring a comprehensive privacy program and biennial third-party assessments.

## Developments

- 2014-12-15 — FTC issued 20-year consent order against Snapchat for six counts of deceptive privacy and security practices, including false claims about ephemeral messaging and a data breach exposing 4.6 million users. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/132-3078-snapchat-inc-matter)

## Background

Snapchat marketed its mobile application as an ephemeral messaging service where photo and video messages would disappear forever after a user-set timer expired. Recipients could easily save messages using publicly available tools and third-party applications. Snapchat also falsely claimed it did not collect location information, misrepresented data collected through its Find Friends feature, and failed to implement reasonable security measures, leading to a breach exposing 4.6 million usernames and phone numbers.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged six counts of deceptive practices under FTC Act Section 5: (1) false claims that messages disappear permanently after a set timer; (2) false claims that senders are notified when recipients take screenshots; (3) false claims that Snapchat does not collect location data; (4) deceptive Find Friends user interface misrepresenting what personal information was collected; (5) deceptive privacy policy regarding Find Friends data collection; and (6) false security claims and failure to implement reasonable security measures to protect personal information.

## Outcome

Snapchat entered into a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4501) prohibiting misrepresentations about privacy and security, requiring establishment of a comprehensive written privacy program with designated privacy coordinators and risk assessments, mandating biennial independent third-party privacy assessments, and imposing recordkeeping, reporting, and corporate-change notification obligations. The order terminates December 23, 2034.

## Significance

This action established that ephemeral-messaging services are fully subject to FTC Act Section 5 when their core privacy claims are deceptive, and it set a precedent for imposing long-term (20-year) privacy program requirements on social media companies that misrepresent both data practices and security posture to consumers.
