## Summary

The FTC charged PaymentsMD with deceiving consumers by secretly using their registration for a free medical billing portal to collect comprehensive health information from pharmacies and health plans for a separate fee-based service. The company failed to adequately disclose this third-party data collection and misrepresented that the authorizations obtained were solely for the free billing service. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order requiring disclosure, consent, and data deletion obligations.

## Background

PaymentsMD operated a free Patient Portal allowing consumers to view their medical billing history. When consumers registered for this free service, the company covertly attempted to collect sensitive health information from third-party sources — pharmacies, health plans, and labs — for a distinct fee-based Patient Health Report service, without adequately disclosing this practice to consumers.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged two counts of deception under FTC Act Section 5: (1) deceptive omission by failing to disclose that registration for the free Patient Portal would be used to collect comprehensive health information from third parties; and (2) deceptive misrepresentation by implying that consumer authorizations were to be used exclusively for the free billing history service.

## Outcome

PaymentsMD entered into a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4505) requiring it to clearly and prominently disclose health information collection practices and obtain affirmative express consent before collecting health information from third parties, prohibiting misrepresentations about privacy and security, restricting use of previously collected data to bill-payment services, and requiring deletion of all other covered information within 60 days.

## Significance

This case illustrates the FTC's use of Section 5 deception authority to police covert secondary uses of sensitive health data collected through consumer-facing portals, establishing that bundling consent for separate services without adequate disclosure is an unfair or deceptive practice even outside HIPAA-covered entities.
