## Summary

The FTC charged TES Franchising, LLC, a business coaching services company, with falsely claiming active participation in the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks and the TRUSTe Privacy Program when none of those certifications were current. The company's Safe Harbor certification had lapsed in March 2013, yet it continued to display participation claims through February 2015. A consent order was finalized on May 15, 2015, imposing a 20-year prohibition on misrepresentations about privacy and security program membership.

## Background

TES Franchising, LLC displayed Safe Harbor participation claims on its website from March 2011 until February 2015, even though its certification lapsed in March 2013. The company also misrepresented the dispute resolution mechanism available to EU and Swiss consumers and falsely claimed to be a current licensee of the TRUSTe Privacy Program.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts: (1) that TES Franchising falsely represented current participation in the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks after its certification had lapsed; (2) that it misrepresented the dispute resolution mechanism available to Safe Harbor-covered consumers, including false claims about arbitration administered by the American Arbitration Association, hearing location in Connecticut, and equal cost-sharing; and (3) that it falsely claimed to be a current licensee of the TRUSTe Privacy Program.

## Outcome

TES Franchising agreed to a consent order (Docket C-4525) prohibiting it from misrepresenting participation in any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program and from misrepresenting participation in any alternative dispute resolution process. The order also imposes recordkeeping, acknowledgment, corporate change notification, and compliance reporting obligations. The order runs for 20 years, terminating May 20, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's sustained enforcement campaign against companies that falsely claim Safe Harbor or TRUSTe certification, reinforcing that lapsed or fictitious privacy seal representations constitute deceptive acts under Section 5.
