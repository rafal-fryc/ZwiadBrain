## Summary

The FTC charged American International Mailing, Inc. with falsely claiming active participation in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework for approximately five years after its certification lapsed in May 2010. The company had originally self-certified in 2006 but failed to renew, yet continued displaying Safe Harbor representations on its website. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Background

American International Mailing, Inc. self-certified to the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework in May 2006, enabling lawful personal data transfers from the EU to the US. In May 2010, the company failed to renew its certification and the Department of Commerce updated its status to 'not current.' Despite this lapse, the company continued to represent on its website that it abides by the Safe Harbor Principles.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that the company falsely represented it was a 'current' participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework after its certification had lapsed, constituting a deceptive act or practice in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

A final consent order (Docket C-4526) was issued, prohibiting the company from misrepresenting its membership in or compliance with any government-sponsored or self-regulatory privacy or security program. The order also imposed recordkeeping, acknowledgment, notification, and compliance reporting obligations, and carries a 20-year duration terminating May 20, 2035.

## Significance

This action is one of a series of FTC enforcement cases targeting lapsed Safe Harbor certifications, reinforcing that failure to renew a certification while continuing to display Safe Harbor claims constitutes a deceptive practice under Section 5.
