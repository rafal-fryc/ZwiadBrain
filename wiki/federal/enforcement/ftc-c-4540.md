## Summary

The FTC charged Golf Connect, LLC with falsely claiming current participation in the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks on an acquired website. The company inherited the golfhub.com site from GolfSwitch, Inc. in April 2014, but GolfSwitch had already let its Safe Harbor certification lapse in May 2013, and Golf Connect never obtained its own certification. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy or security program participation.

## Background

Golf Connect acquired the assets of GolfSwitch, Inc. in April 2014, including the website www.golfhub.com. GolfSwitch had failed to renew its Safe Harbor certification in May 2013, and Golf Connect never self-certified. Despite this, Golf Connect continued displaying privacy statements on golfhub.com claiming current U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor participation from April 2014 through April 2015.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that Golf Connect falsely represented itself as a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, when in fact neither the predecessor company's certification nor any Golf Connect certification was valid during the relevant period.

## Outcome

Golf Connect agreed to a consent order prohibiting misrepresentations about membership in or adherence to any privacy or security program, including the Safe Harbor Frameworks. The order also imposes 5-year recordkeeping requirements, personnel acknowledgment obligations, corporate-change notification duties, and a compliance reporting requirement. The order runs for 20 years, terminating September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action illustrates that Safe Harbor misrepresentation liability can arise through acquisition — a company that inherits website privacy claims from a predecessor without verifying or renewing the underlying certification faces FTC enforcement even if it never affirmatively made the original representation.
