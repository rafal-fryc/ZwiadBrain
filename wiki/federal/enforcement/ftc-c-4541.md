## Summary

The FTC charged Contract Logix, LLC with falsely claiming participation in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework on its website for nearly three years after the program certification had lapsed. The company inherited a predecessor's privacy policy representations when it acquired assets in August 2012, even though the underlying certification had expired in July 2012. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy program participation.

## Background

Contract Logix, LLC acquired the assets of Contract Logix, Inc. in August 2012, including its website. The predecessor company had self-certified to the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework in July 2010 but failed to renew its certification in July 2012. From August 2012 until May 2015, Contract Logix, LLC continued to display privacy policies on its website falsely claiming current Safe Harbor participation.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of misrepresentation: that Contract Logix represented itself as a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework when its certification had lapsed, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

The FTC issued a consent order (Docket C-4541) prohibiting Contract Logix from misrepresenting its membership in or adherence to any privacy or security program, including the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks. The order also imposes recordkeeping, acknowledgment, and compliance reporting obligations for 20 years, terminating on September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's sustained enforcement campaign against companies that falsely claim Safe Harbor certification, reinforcing that lapsed or inherited certifications do not excuse continued privacy representations and that acquirers of business assets bear responsibility for the accuracy of inherited website disclosures.
