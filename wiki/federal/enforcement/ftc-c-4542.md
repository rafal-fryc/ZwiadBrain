## Summary

The FTC charged IOActive, Inc., a security consulting company, with falsely representing on its website that it was a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework for approximately three years after its certification lapsed in May 2012. The company continued displaying the Safe Harbor Certification Mark and related claims through May 2015 despite Commerce updating its status to 'not current.' The matter was resolved by a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Background

IOActive, Inc. originally obtained U.S.-EU Safe Harbor self-certification in May 2009 but failed to renew it in May 2012, at which point the Department of Commerce updated its status to 'not current.' IOActive nonetheless continued to display the Safe Harbor Certification Mark and claim current participation on its website for approximately three years, from May 2012 through May 2015.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that IOActive misrepresented, expressly or by implication, that it was a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act. The misrepresentation persisted for roughly three years after the company's certification had lapsed.

## Outcome

IOActive entered a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4542) prohibiting it from misrepresenting its membership, certification, or participation in any government-sponsored or self-regulatory privacy or security program. The order also imposed recordkeeping, acknowledgment, notification, and compliance-reporting requirements, with a termination date of September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of a series of FTC enforcement matters targeting companies that falsely claimed active U.S.-EU Safe Harbor certification. Notably the respondent is itself a prominent security consulting firm, underscoring that the FTC applies certification-accuracy rules to the security industry itself.
