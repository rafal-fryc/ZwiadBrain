## Summary

The FTC charged Jhayrmaine Daniels, operating as California Skate-Line, with falsely claiming on its website to participate in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework and to adhere to Safe Harbor Privacy Principles, when in fact the company had never been a participant. The matter was resolved by a consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy program membership. The order carries a 20-year duration.

## Background

California Skate-Line, operated by Jhayrmaine Daniels, posted claims on its website representing that it adhered to U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Privacy Principles and was a participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework. The company was never a participant in the framework at any time.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that the respondent falsely represented to consumers that it was a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

A consent order (Docket C-4543) was finalized on October 15, 2015, prohibiting the respondent from misrepresenting membership in or adherence to any government- or self-regulatory-sponsored privacy or security program, including both the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks. The order also imposed recordkeeping, acknowledgment, compliance-reporting, and notification requirements, and runs for 20 years terminating no earlier than September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of a series of FTC enforcement actions against companies that falsely claimed Safe Harbor participation, demonstrating the Commission's use of Section 5 deception authority to police cross-border privacy certification misrepresentations even by small operators.
