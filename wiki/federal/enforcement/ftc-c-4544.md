## Summary

The FTC charged One Industries Corp., a motocross gear seller, with falsely claiming on its website that it adhered to the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Privacy Framework when the company had never self-certified or participated in the program. The FTC alleged this constituted a deceptive practice under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting further misrepresentations about privacy or security program participation.

## Background

One Industries Corp. is a Delaware corporation and worldwide seller of motocross-related gear. Its website contained a privacy policy claiming current participation in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework, which requires companies to self-certify to the U.S. Department of Commerce that they comply with seven privacy principles. One Industries had never self-certified and was never a participant in the framework.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that One Industries expressly or by implication represented to consumers that it was a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework and adhered to Safe Harbor Privacy Principles with respect to personally identifiable information received from customers and employees in the European Union, when in fact it had never self-certified or participated in the program.

## Outcome

One Industries agreed to a consent order (Docket C-4544) prohibiting it from misrepresenting participation in any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, including the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks. The order also imposed recordkeeping, order delivery, compliance reporting, and corporate change notification obligations. The order runs for 20 years, terminating September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's broader campaign against false Safe Harbor certification claims, establishing that even companies that never formally enrolled in the Safe Harbor program can face enforcement action if they publicly claim participation, putting all retailers handling EU consumer data on notice that Safe Harbor representations are subject to FTC scrutiny.
