## Summary

The FTC charged Dale Jarrett Racing Adventure, a race car driving school, with falsely claiming participation in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework on its website when the company was never a certified participant. The matter was resolved through a consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy or security program membership. The order carries a 20-year duration.

## Background

Dale Jarrett Racing Adventure, Inc. operated a race car driving school and falsely represented on its website that it was a participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework and registered with the U.S. Department of Commerce's Safe Harbor Program, when in fact it was never a participant.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that the respondent falsely represented current participation in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

The FTC issued a consent order (Docket C-4545) prohibiting the respondent from misrepresenting membership in any government- or self-regulatory-sponsored privacy or security program, including the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks. The order also imposes recordkeeping, acknowledgment, compliance reporting, and corporate-change notification requirements, and terminates September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is one of a series of FTC enforcement matters targeting companies that falsely claim Safe Harbor certification, reinforcing that unverified privacy-framework representations constitute deceptive practices under Section 5 of the FTC Act regardless of company size or industry.
