## Summary

The FTC charged Inbox Group, LLC, a Texas-based marketing agency, with falsely claiming on its website that it was certified under the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework when it had never been a participant. The FTC alleged this constituted a deceptive practice under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act. The matter was resolved through a consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy program membership and imposing recordkeeping and reporting obligations.

## Background

Inbox Group, LLC is a Texas-based marketing agency that falsely represented on its website that it had certified its compliance with the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework, a program governing data transfers between the European Union and the United States. The company was not and had never been a participant in the framework.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that Inbox Group falsely represented to consumers that it was a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework, making its public representations deceptive in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

A consent order was issued prohibiting Inbox Group from misrepresenting its membership in or participation in any privacy or security program, including the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks. The order also imposed five-year recordkeeping requirements, mandatory delivery of the order to relevant personnel, notification obligations for corporate changes, compliance reporting to the FTC, and a 20-year order duration terminating on September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's ongoing enforcement campaign against companies that falsely claim Safe Harbor certification, treating such misrepresentations as deceptive practices under Section 5. It reinforces that unverified or fabricated claims of participation in cross-border data transfer frameworks carry legal risk even for smaller firms.
