## Summary

The FTC charged Just Bagels Manufacturing, Inc., a wholesale bagel manufacturer, with falsely claiming on its website that it participated in the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks for cross-border personal data transfers. The company had never been enrolled in either framework. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy program participation.

## Background

Just Bagels Manufacturing published a privacy policy on its website asserting compliance with both the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework and the U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Framework, programs governing the transfer of personal data from Europe and Switzerland to the United States. In fact, the company was not and had never been a participant in either framework, rendering its public claims false and misleading.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged a single count: that Just Bagels Manufacturing falsely represented itself as a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework and the U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Framework, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, which prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices.

## Outcome

Just Bagels Manufacturing agreed to a consent order (Docket C-4547) prohibiting it from misrepresenting participation in any privacy or security program. The order also imposed recordkeeping, acknowledgment, compliance reporting, and corporate-change notification requirements. The order runs for 20 years, terminating no earlier than September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's series of Safe Harbor misrepresentation cases, which targeted companies that posted Safe Harbor compliance claims without actually enrolling in the framework. It demonstrates that the FTC applied Section 5 deception enforcement to data-transfer privacy representations even for small, non-technology businesses.
