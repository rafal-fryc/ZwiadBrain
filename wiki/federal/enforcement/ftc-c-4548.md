## Summary

The FTC charged NAICS Association, LLC with falsely representing that it was a current participant in the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks for more than a year after its certification lapsed. The company had self-certified in February 2013 but failed to renew in February 2014, yet continued displaying Safe Harbor compliance claims on its website through April 2015. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy or security program participation.

## Background

NAICS Association, LLC self-certified to the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks in February 2013. In February 2014, the company failed to renew its certification and the Department of Commerce updated its status to 'not current.' Despite this, the company continued to claim Safe Harbor compliance on its website from February 2014 through April 2015.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of Safe Harbor Framework Participation Misrepresentation under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, charging that NAICS Association falsely represented itself as a 'current' participant in both the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks after its certification had expired.

## Outcome

NAICS Association, LLC entered into a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4548) prohibiting it from misrepresenting its membership in or adherence to any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, including Safe Harbor frameworks. The order also imposed recordkeeping, acknowledgment, reporting, and corporate-change notification requirements, terminating on September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's sustained enforcement campaign against companies that falsely claim Safe Harbor certification, establishing that allowing a lapsed certification to remain publicly displayed constitutes a deceptive practice under Section 5 of the FTC Act.
