## Summary

The FTC charged Jubilant Clinsys, a clinical research organization serving pharmaceutical and biotech companies, with falsely claiming participation in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework for over two years after its certification lapsed. The company's website continued to represent active Safe Harbor compliance and annual self-certification from November 2012 through April 2015, despite its certification becoming 'not current' in November 2012. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy or security program participation.

## Background

Jubilant Clinsys self-certified to the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework in November 2007, a program enabling lawful transfer of personal data from the EU to the US. In November 2012, the company failed to renew its certification and the Department of Commerce updated its status to 'not current.' Despite the lapse, the company's public website continued for over two years to represent active Safe Harbor compliance and annual self-certification.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged a single count of false Safe Harbor participation claims: that Jubilant Clinsys represented itself as a 'current' participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework when its certification had lapsed, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

The matter was resolved by a consent order (Docket C-4549) prohibiting Jubilant Clinsys from misrepresenting membership in or adherence to any privacy or security program, including the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks. The order also imposes recordkeeping, acknowledgment, compliance reporting, and corporate-change notification requirements, and runs for 20 years terminating no earlier than September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's sustained enforcement campaign against companies that falsely claim Safe Harbor certification, treating lapsed or fabricated participation claims as deceptive practices under Section 5. It reinforces that maintaining accurate privacy-program representations is an ongoing obligation, not a one-time step.
