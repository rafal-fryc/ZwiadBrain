## Summary

The FTC charged Pinger, Inc., a mobile app developer, with falsely claiming on its website that it was a current, certified participant in the U.S.-EU and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Frameworks after its annual self-certification lapsed in March 2014. The deceptive representations persisted for over a year until Pinger renewed its certification in May 2015. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Background

Pinger originally self-certified compliance with both Safe Harbor Frameworks in March 2011 and published corresponding privacy policy statements on its website. When Pinger failed to renew its annual certification in March 2014, the Department of Commerce updated its status to 'not current,' but Pinger continued displaying language claiming it 'complies with' and 'has certified' adherence to the frameworks from March 2014 through approximately April 2015.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of deceptive acts or practices under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act: that Pinger falsely represented it was a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework and U.S.-Swiss Safe Harbor Framework, including that it complied with those frameworks and had certified adherence to their privacy principles, when in fact its certification had lapsed.

## Outcome

Pinger entered into a consent order (Docket C-4550) carrying a 20-year term expiring September 29, 2035. The order prohibits Pinger from misrepresenting participation in any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, and imposes recordkeeping, order-distribution, acknowledgment, compliance-reporting, and corporate-change-notification obligations.

## Significance

The action is part of the FTC's sustained enforcement campaign against false Safe Harbor certification claims, reinforcing that allowing an annual self-certification to lapse while continuing to display Safe Harbor seals or statements constitutes a deceptive practice actionable under Section 5 of the FTC Act.
