## Summary

The FTC charged Forensics Consulting Solutions, an electronic discovery consulting firm, with falsely claiming participation in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework for nearly three years after its certification lapsed in 2012. The company continued displaying Safe Harbor representations on its website until May 2015. The matter was resolved through a finalized consent order with a 20-year duration.

## Developments

- 2015-10-15 — Consent order (C-4551) issued against Forensics Consulting Solutions for falsely claiming U.S.-EU Safe Harbor participation for nearly three years after its certification lapsed. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/152-3185-forensics-consulting-solutions-llc-matter)

## Background

Forensics Consulting Solutions self-certified to the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework in 2009 but failed to renew its certification in 2012. Despite losing its Safe Harbor status, the company continued to represent on its website from August 2012 until May 2015 that it was a current Safe Harbor participant.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that the respondent falsely represented it was a current participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework when its certification had in fact lapsed, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

A finalized consent order (Docket C-4551) was issued prohibiting the respondent from misrepresenting its membership or participation in any privacy or security program sponsored by government or self-regulatory organizations. The order also imposed recordkeeping, acknowledgment, notification, and compliance reporting obligations, and runs for 20 years terminating on September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's series of enforcement actions against companies that continued to claim U.S.-EU Safe Harbor compliance after their certifications had lapsed, reinforcing that stale or false privacy certification claims constitute deceptive practices under Section 5 of the FTC Act.
