## Summary

The FTC charged SteriMed Medical Waste Solutions with falsely claiming on its website to be a registered participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework when the company had never self-certified with the U.S. Department of Commerce. The charge was brought under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act as a deceptive trade practice. The matter was resolved through a consent order prohibiting further misrepresentations of privacy or security program participation.

## Background

SteriMed Medical Waste Solutions is a Delaware corporation that develops and manufactures on-site chemical-based medical waste processors. Its website privacy policy stated that it adhered to Safe Harbor Privacy Principles and was registered with the U.S. Department of Commerce's Safe Harbor Program — representations that were false, as the company had never self-certified to Commerce and was never a Framework participant.

## Allegations

Since at least January 2015, SteriMed represented on its website that it adhered to the U.S. Safe Harbor Privacy Principles and was a registered participant in the U.S.-EU Safe Harbor Framework. The FTC alleged this constituted a deceptive practice under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act because the company had never self-certified and was never enrolled in the Framework.

## Outcome

SteriMed entered into a consent order (Docket C-4552, issued October 15, 2015) prohibiting it from misrepresenting participation in or certification by any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program. The order also imposed standard recordkeeping, compliance reporting, order-delivery, and corporate-change-notification obligations. The order runs for 20 years, terminating no earlier than September 29, 2035.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's systematic enforcement sweep against companies that falsely claimed U.S.-EU Safe Harbor certification. False Safe Harbor claims are treated as deceptive practices because they mislead consumers and businesses in the EU about the privacy protections governing their personal data transferred to the U.S.
