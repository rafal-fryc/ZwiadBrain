## Summary

The FTC charged Craig Brittain with operating a nonconsensual intimate-image website (isanybodydown.com) that posted private photographs of over 1,000 individuals without their consent and ran a sham paid-removal service he secretly controlled. The agency alleged two counts under FTC Act Section 5: unfair dissemination of intimate images for commercial gain and deceptive solicitation of photographs under false pretenses. A 20-year consent order, finalized January 15, 2016, permanently bars Brittain from posting intimate images without affirmative written consent and from making material misrepresentations online.

## Background

Brittain operated isanybodydown.com from November 2011 to April 2013, obtaining intimate photographs through anonymous third-party submissions, by posing as a woman on Craigslist to solicit images under false pretenses, and through a paid bounty system. Victims seeking removal were directed to fictitious services ('Takedown Hammer' and 'Takedown Lawyer') secretly run by Brittain, who charged $200–$500 to remove the very images he had posted, earning approximately $12,000 from these operations.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged that Brittain unfairly disseminated intimate photographs and personal information of individuals through his website for commercial gain without their knowledge or consent, knowing those individuals had a reasonable expectation the images would not be so shared. Count 2 alleged that he deceptively represented to Craigslist users that intimate photographs he solicited would be used solely for his personal private use.

## Outcome

The finalized consent order permanently prohibits Brittain from disseminating intimate images online without affirmative express written consent and from making material misrepresentations through any website or online service, including about his own identity and data practices. He was required to destroy all personal information collected through the covered websites within 30 days of the order's entry and to notify the FTC of any new business affiliations for ten years. The order runs for 20 years, terminating December 28, 2035.

## Significance

This was one of the first FTC enforcement actions directly targeting a 'revenge porn' operator, establishing that nonconsensual publication of intimate images for commercial gain is an unfair practice under FTC Act Section 5 and that sham removal services extracting fees from victims compound the harm. Together with the later EMP Media/MyEx.com action, it forms the FTC's pre-TAKE IT DOWN Act enforcement lineage on nonconsensual intimate imagery.
