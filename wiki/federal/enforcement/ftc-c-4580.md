## Summary

The FTC charged Very Incognito Technologies, Inc. (doing business as Vipvape) with falsely claiming in its privacy policy that it participated in the APEC Cross-Border Privacy Rules (CBPR) system, when in fact the company had never obtained the required certification. The FTC alleged this constituted a deceptive act or practice under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act. The matter was resolved through a consent order imposing a 20-year prohibition on misrepresenting participation in any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program.

## Background

Vipvape published a privacy policy on its website asserting it abided by the APEC CBPR system. Certification requires review by an APEC-recognized accountability agent and passing annual reviews; Vipvape completed neither step and was never certified.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that Vipvape falsely represented, expressly or by implication, that it was certified to participate in the APEC CBPR system, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

Vipvape agreed to a consent order (Docket C-4580) effective 2016-06-29 and terminating June 21, 2036. The order prohibits the company from misrepresenting membership in, adherence to, certification by, or participation in any privacy or security program sponsored by a government or self-regulatory organization. It also imposes acknowledgment, compliance reporting, recordkeeping, and monitoring obligations for 20 years.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's enforcement campaign against false APEC CBPR certification claims, signaling that even small e-commerce operators face federal action for misrepresenting participation in cross-border privacy frameworks without undergoing the required vetting process.
