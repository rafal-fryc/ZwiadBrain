## Summary

The FTC charged Turn Inc., a digital advertising company, with deceiving consumers by claiming that deleting cookies or using its opt-out mechanism would stop targeted tracking, while secretly using Verizon's unkillable X-UIDH tracking headers to continue surveillance. Turn synced those headers with cookies and device advertising IDs, allowing it to re-identify consumers even after they reset their privacy controls. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order requiring Turn to stop misrepresentations, provide a genuine opt-out mechanism, and honor mobile OS-level advertising opt-out signals.

## Background

Turn Inc. participated beginning in 2013 in a Verizon Wireless program that injected unique X-UIDH tracking headers into all unencrypted mobile web traffic. Consumers had no ability to opt out of these headers at the carrier level. Turn secretly synced the headers with cookies and device advertising identifiers so that when consumers deleted cookies or reset their device advertising IDs, Turn could re-link them to the same tracking profile using the persistent header.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged two counts of misrepresentation under FTC Act Section 5: (1) Turn falsely told consumers that blocking or limiting cookies would restrict its ability to track them, when in fact it used Verizon's X-UIDH headers to continue tracking regardless; and (2) Turn falsely represented that consumers could opt out of tailored advertising in mobile applications through its opt-out page, when the mechanism only applied to mobile browsers and did not block in-app targeted ads.

## Outcome

Turn agreed to a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4612, effective April 2017, terminating April 6, 2037) prohibiting misrepresentations about data collection and consumer controls. Turn was required within 30 days to post a clear opt-out hyperlink on its homepage, disclose all technologies used for targeted advertising, honor mobile OS advertising opt-out signals, maintain specified records for 10 years, and submit sworn compliance reports to the FTC.

## Significance

The Turn order was an early FTC action targeting the use of carrier-injected 'supercookies' — persistent tracking identifiers that consumers could not delete — highlighting how advertising technology could silently circumvent standard privacy controls. It established that misrepresenting the effectiveness of opt-out tools, not just the collection of data itself, constitutes a deceptive practice under Section 5.
