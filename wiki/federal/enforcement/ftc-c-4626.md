## Summary

The FTC charged TaxSlayer, LLC with violating the GLBA Privacy Rule and Safeguards Rule in connection with its online tax preparation service. The company failed to provide customers with a clear privacy notice, operated without a written information security program, and never conducted a risk assessment. These gaps enabled a credential-stuffing attack in late 2015 that compromised nearly 8,900 customer accounts and led to fraudulent tax filings and refund diversion. A 20-year consent order was finalized in November 2017 requiring permanent compliance with the GLB rules, biennial third-party security assessments, and extensive recordkeeping.

## Background

TaxSlayer operated TaxSlayer Online, used by more than 950,000 individuals in 2016 to prepare and file tax returns, collecting highly sensitive data including Social Security numbers, bank account numbers, and income information. As a GLBA-covered financial institution, it was subject to both the Privacy Rule and the Safeguards Rule. It had no written security program until November 2015, had never conducted a risk assessment, and buried its privacy notice inside a lengthy license agreement with no mechanism to ensure customer receipt.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged that TaxSlayer violated the GLBA Privacy Rule by providing its privacy notice buried at the end of a License Agreement without ensuring customers could reasonably receive actual notice and without requiring acknowledgment as a condition of service. Count 2 alleged that TaxSlayer violated the GLBA Safeguards Rule by operating without a written comprehensive information security program until November 2015, never conducting risk assessments, and failing to implement safeguards — such as strong password requirements, risk-based authentication, and account-change notifications — to protect against credential-based account takeover attacks. Beginning October 10, 2015, attackers exploited these failures in a list validation attack that compromised 8,882 accounts before TaxSlayer discovered the breach on January 11, 2016.

## Outcome

TaxSlayer entered into a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4626) finalized November 15, 2017. The order permanently enjoins violations of the GLBA Privacy Rule and Safeguards Rule, requires biennial third-party security assessments for ten years, mandates acknowledgment and delivery of the order to key personnel, imposes compliance reporting and notice obligations, requires 20 years of recordkeeping, and authorizes ongoing FTC compliance monitoring. The order terminates October 20, 2037.

## Significance

This action illustrates the FTC's enforcement of GLBA security and privacy obligations against online tax preparers handling highly sensitive financial data. The case highlights that burying a privacy notice in a license agreement is insufficient under the Privacy Rule, and that the absence of a written security program and risk assessments — basic Safeguards Rule requirements — directly enabled a credential-stuffing attack resulting in large-scale tax identity theft. The 20-year order duration reflects the seriousness of the failures.
