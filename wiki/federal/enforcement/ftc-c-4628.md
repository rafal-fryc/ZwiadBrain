## Summary

The FTC charged Tru Communication, Inc. (doing business as TCPrinting.net), a California printing services company, with falsely claiming participation in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework when the company had never completed the certification process. The FTC alleged this constituted a deceptive trade practice under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Background

Tru Communication operated a website under the name TCPrinting.net and published privacy policies falsely asserting it would remain 'compliant and current with Privacy Shield at all times.' Privacy Shield is the mechanism permitting U.S. companies to lawfully transfer personal data from the EU to the United States by self-certifying compliance with Privacy Shield Principles to the Department of Commerce. Although Tru Communication initiated a certification application, it never completed the required steps to actually participate in the framework.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of false representation of EU-U.S. Privacy Shield participation: that Respondent expressly or by implication represented itself as a current participant in the Privacy Shield framework despite having never completed the self-certification process, deceiving consumers and businesses about its data protection practices in violation of FTC Act Section 5(a).

## Outcome

The consent order (Docket C-4628) prohibits Tru Communication from misrepresenting its participation in any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, including the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks. The order imposes recordkeeping obligations for five years of specified business records, compliance reporting, and FTC monitoring authority, and runs for 20 years terminating November 20, 2037.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's systematic enforcement sweep against companies that falsely claimed EU-U.S. Privacy Shield certification — a deceptive practice that undermines the legal mechanism for transatlantic personal data transfers and misleads EU individuals and partner businesses about applicable data protections.
