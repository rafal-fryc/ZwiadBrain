## Summary

The FTC charged Md7, LLC, a Delaware company assisting wireless operators with real estate management, with falsely claiming in its website privacy policy that it was certified under the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield Framework when it had only begun but never completed the certification application. The FTC alleged this constituted a deceptive act in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Background

Md7, LLC assists wireless operators with real estate management. It published a privacy policy on its website claiming compliance with the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework and certification under its principles, when in fact it had only initiated the application process with the U.S. Department of Commerce and never completed it.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged a single count: that Md7 falsely represented through its website privacy policy that it complies with the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield Framework, has certified adherence to Privacy Shield principles, and is a participant in the framework — when in fact the company was never certified. These false claims constituted deceptive acts or practices in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

Md7 agreed to a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4629) prohibiting it from misrepresenting participation in any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, including the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks. The order also imposes acknowledgment, compliance reporting, recordkeeping, and monitoring obligations, and terminates November 20, 2037.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's sustained enforcement campaign against companies that falsely claim Privacy Shield certification. It demonstrates that even companies with no consumer-facing data product — here, a B2B real estate management firm — face FTC scrutiny when their privacy policies make false framework-participation claims.
