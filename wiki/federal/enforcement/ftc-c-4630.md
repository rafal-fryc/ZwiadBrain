## Summary

The FTC charged Decusoft, LLC, a New Jersey human resources software company, with falsely claiming on its website to be certified under both the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield Frameworks when it had never completed the certification process. The Commission alleged this constituted a deceptive act or practice under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Background

Decusoft posted privacy policies claiming certified compliance with both Privacy Shield frameworks despite only initiating — and never completing — the U.S. Department of Commerce certification application. Businesses and consumers relied on Privacy Shield certification as assurance of a lawful personal-data transfer mechanism from the EU and Switzerland to the United States.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that Decusoft falsely represented, expressly or by implication, that it was a certified participant in both the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework and the Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield framework, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

Decusoft agreed to a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4630) prohibiting misrepresentations about membership in or compliance with any government- or self-regulatory-sponsored privacy or security program, including Privacy Shield. The order also requires acknowledgment and delivery of its terms to relevant personnel, sworn compliance reporting within 60 days, 20-year recordkeeping, and ongoing FTC monitoring. The order terminates November 20, 2037.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's series of Privacy Shield enforcement cases, reinforcing that companies may not claim certified status under EU-U.S. or Swiss-U.S. data-transfer frameworks without completing the actual certification process. It signals that even initiating — but not finishing — an application provides no cover for certification claims.
