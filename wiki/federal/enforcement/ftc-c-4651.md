## Summary

The FTC charged PayPal, Inc. (operating as Venmo) with six counts of deceptive and unlawful conduct relating to its peer-to-peer payment service. The agency alleged that Venmo misled consumers about fund availability, the effectiveness of privacy settings, and its claimed bank-grade security, while also violating the GLBA Privacy Rule and Safeguards Rule. A 20-year consent order was finalized on May 15, 2018, requiring corrective disclosures, prohibition on misrepresentations, and biennial third-party security assessments.

## Background

PayPal, Inc. operated Venmo, a peer-to-peer payment and social networking service. Venmo represented to consumers that transferred funds were credited and available for bank transfer, that privacy settings controlled transaction visibility, and that it employed bank-grade security systems and data encryption. In practice, transactions were subject to review and reversal without adequate disclosure, a separate Transaction Sharing Setting could override user-configured privacy preferences, and Venmo lacked a written information security program and adequate security safeguards. Venmo also failed to deliver clear and accurate privacy notices as required under GLBA Regulation P.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged six counts: (1) deceptive representations about fund transfers and overnight bank availability; (2) deceptive claims about the Default Audience privacy setting's ability to restrict transaction visibility; (3) deceptive claims about the Individual Audience privacy setting; (4) false claims of bank-grade security systems and data encryption; (5) violation of the GLBA Privacy Rule (Reg. P) through inadequate privacy notice presentation and inaccurate disclosure of information-sharing practices; and (6) violation of the GLBA Safeguards Rule by failing to maintain a written information security program or assess foreseeable security risks.

## Outcome

PayPal agreed to a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4651) requiring it to cease misrepresentations about payment services and privacy/security protections; provide clear disclosures near fund-transfer representations about transaction review and potential fund freezing; issue notice to all users about review practices; deliver privacy disclosures at account opening and at each transaction; comply permanently with the GLBA Privacy Rule and Safeguards Rule; and obtain initial and biennial third-party security assessments for 10 years. The order terminates May 23, 2038.

## Significance

This action established that peer-to-peer payment apps with social features face both FTC Act deception liability and GLBA compliance obligations, and set a precedent requiring fintech companies to provide transaction-level privacy disclosures and undergo recurring third-party security assessments under a long-duration (20-year) consent order.
