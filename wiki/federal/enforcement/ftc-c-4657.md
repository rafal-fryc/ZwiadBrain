## Summary

The FTC charged BLU Products and its principal with deception after the company sold smartphones preinstalled with ADUPS software that secretly transmitted users' text messages, real-time location data, call logs, contact lists, and app lists to servers in China. BLU's privacy policy falsely claimed third-party data sharing was limited to what was necessary for contracted services and that appropriate security procedures were in place. The matter resolved via a 20-year consent order requiring a comprehensive information security program, prior consent before data collection, and biennial third-party security assessments.

## Background

BLU Products sold mobile devices preinstalled with firmware software from ADUPS Technology Co., a China-based company engaged only for firmware-over-the-air update services. Without consumer knowledge or consent, ADUPS software collected and transmitted sensitive personal information — including full text message contents, real-time location data, call logs, contact lists, and installed app lists — to ADUPS servers. BLU failed to conduct due diligence on ADUPS, had no written data security standards for service providers, and imposed no contractual security requirements on ADUPS, leaving devices vulnerable to command injection attacks.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged BLU deceptively represented that it limited disclosure of user information to third-party service providers only to the extent necessary to perform services on BLU's behalf. Count 2 alleged BLU deceptively represented that it implemented appropriate physical, electronic, and managerial security procedures to protect consumers' personal information. Both counts arose under FTC Act Section 5's prohibition on unfair or deceptive acts or practices.

## Outcome

A 20-year consent order (Docket C-4657, effective September 15, 2018, terminating September 6, 2038) prohibits misrepresentations about privacy and security practices, mandates a comprehensive written information security program, requires biennial independent third-party security assessments, and obligates BLU to obtain affirmative express consumer consent before collecting or disclosing covered information. Respondents must also maintain records for 20 years and submit annual compliance reports.

## Significance

The action highlights supply-chain privacy and security risks in consumer hardware: a U.S. smartphone seller was held responsible for covert data collection conducted by a foreign firmware vendor embedded in its devices. The requirement for affirmative express consent before any data collection or disclosure, combined with a 20-year order duration, signals the FTC's intent to impose lasting structural obligations on device makers that rely on third-party preinstalled software.
