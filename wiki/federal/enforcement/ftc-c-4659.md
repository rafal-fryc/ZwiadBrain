## Summary

The FTC charged ReadyTech Corporation with deceptively claiming on its website that it was actively certifying compliance with the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework, when in fact it never completed certification. The company had initiated an application with the Department of Commerce in October 2016 but never finished the process. A consent order was finalized requiring ReadyTech to stop misrepresenting participation in privacy programs and imposing 20 years of compliance obligations.

## Background

ReadyTech Corporation, an online and instructor-led training company, published privacy policy statements claiming it was actively certifying Privacy Shield compliance and committed to related dispute resolution procedures. It initiated a Privacy Shield application with the U.S. Department of Commerce in October 2016 but never completed the steps required to actually participate in the framework.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of Privacy Shield misrepresentation under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, asserting that ReadyTech falsely represented, directly or by implication, that it was actively in the process of certifying compliance with the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework.

## Outcome

A finalized consent order (Docket C-4659) prohibits ReadyTech from misrepresenting its membership in, compliance with, or participation in any government- or self-regulatory-sponsored privacy or security program, including the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks. The order imposes acknowledgment, compliance reporting, recordkeeping, and monitoring obligations for 20 years, terminating October 17, 2038.

## Significance

The action is part of the FTC's ongoing enforcement sweep against companies that falsely claim Privacy Shield certification, reinforcing that even incomplete or abandoned certification attempts cannot be publicly represented as active compliance efforts.
