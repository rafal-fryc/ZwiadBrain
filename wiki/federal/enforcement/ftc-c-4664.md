## Summary

The FTC charged VenPath, Inc., a data analytics company, with falsely claiming active participation in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework after its certification lapsed in October 2017, and with failing to affirm to the Department of Commerce that it would continue protecting EU consumer personal data. The company maintained deceptive representations on its website through at least June 2018. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting further misrepresentations and imposing affirmative privacy obligations.

## Background

VenPath obtained EU-U.S. Privacy Shield certification in October 2016 but failed to renew when it expired in October 2017. It nonetheless continued displaying a privacy policy falsely claiming active Privacy Shield participation and never fulfilled its continuing obligation to affirm data protection commitments to the U.S. Department of Commerce.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged two counts: (1) that VenPath misrepresented it was a current participant in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework when its certification had lapsed; and (2) that VenPath falsely represented it would abide by Privacy Shield principles, including the requirement to affirm continuing data-protection obligations to the Department of Commerce upon ceasing participation.

## Outcome

VenPath was bound by a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4664) prohibiting misrepresentations about participation in any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program. The order required VenPath to either affirm continuing Privacy Shield obligations to the Department of Commerce within 10 days or delete all personal data received during its participation period, along with recordkeeping, compliance reporting, and FTC monitoring obligations through November 15, 2038.

## Significance

The action is part of the FTC's systematic enforcement of Privacy Shield misrepresentation cases, reinforcing that companies cannot claim framework participation after certification expires and must fulfill post-participation data-protection obligations regardless of whether they renew.
