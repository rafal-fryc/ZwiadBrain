## Summary

The FTC charged IDmission LLC with falsely claiming on its website that it was a certified participant in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework when it had never completed the required certification steps. The Department of Commerce had warned the company to remove the claims before certification was complete, but IDmission continued displaying them at least through June 2018. The matter was resolved by a 20-year consent order prohibiting further misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Background

IDmission LLC, a cloud-based technology platform provider, initiated a Privacy Shield certification application with the Department of Commerce in October 2017 but never completed the required steps. Despite a Commerce warning to remove its Privacy Shield claims until certification was finalized, IDmission continued displaying those claims on its website at least through June 2018.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of Privacy Shield misrepresentation under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, asserting that IDmission falsely represented, directly or by implication, that it was a certified participant in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework, thereby deceiving consumers and business clients about the legal basis for its handling of EU personal data.

## Outcome

IDmission was subject to a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4665) prohibiting it from misrepresenting its participation in, certification by, or compliance with any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, including the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks. The order also required compliance reporting, recordkeeping for 20 years, delivery of the order to relevant personnel and business successors, and ongoing FTC monitoring. The order terminates November 15, 2038.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's sustained enforcement campaign against companies that falsely claim Privacy Shield certification, reinforcing that mere initiation of a certification application — without completion — does not permit a company to hold itself out as a certified participant. The 20-year order duration signals the seriousness with which the FTC treats cross-border data-transfer misrepresentations.
