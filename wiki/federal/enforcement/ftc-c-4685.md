## Summary

The FTC charged SecurTest, Inc., a Florida background screening company, with falsely claiming on its website that it had self-certified to both the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks when it had not completed the required certification steps. SecurTest displayed these false representations from September 2017 until July 2018. The matter resulted in a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Background

SecurTest initiated a Privacy Shield certification application with the U.S. Department of Commerce in September 2017 but failed to complete the required steps within Commerce's established timeframes. Despite this, it continued to claim certified participant status on its website until July 2018. SecurTest only completed the process and received official certification on August 31, 2018, after the FTC contacted the company.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of Privacy Shield Misrepresentation under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, charging that SecurTest falsely represented, directly or by implication, that it was a certified participant in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks during a period when it had not completed the required certification.

## Outcome

SecurTest agreed to a consent order (Docket C-4685) effective August 15, 2019, and terminating August 12, 2039. The order prohibits misrepresentations about participation in any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, imposes 10-year recordkeeping requirements, mandates compliance reporting, and authorizes ongoing FTC monitoring.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's enforcement series targeting false Privacy Shield certification claims, signaling that even companies that eventually obtain legitimate certification can face liability for misrepresenting their status during the application period before certification is complete.
