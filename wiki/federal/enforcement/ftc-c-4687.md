## Summary

The FTC charged LightYear Dealer Technologies (doing business as DealerBuilt) with failing to protect the personal information of over 14 million consumers and 39,000 employees stored in its automotive dealer management software platform. The company stored sensitive data — including Social Security numbers and financial account data — in clear text without access controls, vulnerability scanning, or a written security program. A resulting 2016 breach exposed data belonging to 69,283 consumers. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order requiring a comprehensive information security program and biennial third-party assessments.

## Background

DealerBuilt provided dealer management software to approximately 180 automotive dealerships. It stored vast amounts of sensitive personal information in clear text without access controls or authentication protections. In April 2015, an employee attached an unsecured storage device to the backup network, opening a port that went undetected for 18 months due to the absence of any vulnerability scanning, penetration testing, or device inventory. In late October 2016, a hacker exploited the open port and downloaded personal data for 69,283 consumers; DealerBuilt only learned of the breach when a customer reported that data was publicly accessible on the Internet.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged two counts: (1) unfair data security practices under FTC Act Section 5(a) for failing to employ reasonable measures to protect personal information of millions of consumers and thousands of employees; and (2) violation of the GLB Safeguards Rule for failing as a financial institution to develop and maintain a comprehensive written information security program with administrative, technical, and physical safeguards.

## Outcome

DealerBuilt agreed to a 20-year consent order requiring it to establish and maintain a comprehensive information security program, obtain biennial independent third-party security assessments, submit annual compliance certifications from senior management, report covered incidents to the FTC within 10 days, and comply permanently with the GLB Safeguards Rule. The order terminates September 3, 2039.

## Significance

The action illustrates FTC enforcement of the GLB Safeguards Rule against a non-bank technology vendor handling sensitive consumer financial data, and highlights that storing data in clear text without basic access controls or vulnerability scanning constitutes an unfair practice. The 20-year order duration and mandatory third-party assessment regime reflect the FTC's heightened expectations for data security programs at companies holding large volumes of sensitive personal information.
