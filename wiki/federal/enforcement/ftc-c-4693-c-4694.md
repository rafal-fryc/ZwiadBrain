## Summary

The FTC issued consent orders against Cambridge Analytica app developer Aleksandr Kogan and CEO Alexander Nix for deceiving Facebook users about data collection through the GSRApp. The app falsely promised not to collect identifiable information while harvesting profile data from roughly 250,000–270,000 direct users and up to 65 million of their Facebook friends. The harvested data was matched with voter records to build psychographic profiles for political and commercial clients. Both 20-year orders became final on December 15, 2019.

## Background

Kogan and Nix, along with Cambridge Analytica, developed and operated the GSRApp (also known as 'thisisyourdigitallife') on Facebook to harvest personal profile data including User IDs, names, genders, birthdates, locations, friends lists, and public page likes. The app told users it would not collect identifiable information. The harvested data was matched with U.S. voter records and used to generate psychographic personality profiles for Cambridge Analytica's political and commercial clients.

## Allegations

The FTC charged one count of deceptive claim concerning the collection of personally identifiable information: the GSRApp represented to users that it would not collect their name or any other identifiable information, when in fact it collected extensive personal profile data from both direct users and their Facebook friends without consent.

## Outcome

Final consent orders (C-4693 and C-4694) issued against Kogan and Nix individually, each lasting 20 years. Both respondents were prohibited from misrepresenting privacy practices, required to delete all GSRApp-collected data and derived work products with sworn confirmation, and subjected to ongoing compliance reporting, recordkeeping, and monitoring obligations.

## Significance

These individual orders, companion to the order against Cambridge Analytica LLC itself, hold the specific developer and executive personally liable under FTC Act Section 5 for deceptive data-collection practices — establishing that individuals directing privacy violations can face decade-long consent orders requiring data deletion and compliance monitoring.
