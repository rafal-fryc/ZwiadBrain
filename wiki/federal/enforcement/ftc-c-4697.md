## Summary

The FTC charged Medable, Inc., a technology solutions provider serving pharmaceutical and research industries, with falsely claiming EU-U.S. Privacy Shield certification on its website despite never completing the certification process. Medable initiated a certification application in December 2017 but continued publishing false certification claims until October 2018. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting further misrepresentations about participation in privacy programs.

## Background

Medable, Inc. initiated a Privacy Shield certification application with the U.S. Department of Commerce in December 2017 but never completed the necessary steps. Despite this, it published claims on its website that it was 'EU/US Privacy Shield certified' and had 'certified to the Department of Commerce that it adheres to the Privacy Shield Principles' until October 2018.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of Privacy Shield Misrepresentation under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act: from December 2017 to October 2018, Medable falsely represented, directly or by implication, that it was a participant in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework, constituting a deceptive act or practice.

## Outcome

Medable entered into a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4697) prohibiting it from misrepresenting its membership in, compliance with, or participation in any privacy or security program, including the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks. The order also imposes compliance reporting, recordkeeping for ten years, and ongoing FTC monitoring.

## Significance

This action reinforces FTC enforcement against false Privacy Shield certification claims, particularly in the health technology sector. It signals that initiating but not completing a certification process does not permit a company to claim certified status, and that healthcare and research technology firms are subject to the same deceptive-practices standards as other industries.
