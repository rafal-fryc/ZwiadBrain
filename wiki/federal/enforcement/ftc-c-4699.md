## Summary

The FTC charged 214 Technologies, Inc. (doing business as Trueface.ai), a facial recognition and digital identity company, with falsely claiming it had self-certified to the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework when it had never completed the certification process. The misrepresentation appeared in the company's privacy policy from approximately March 2018 to October 2018. The matter was resolved by a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Developments

- 2020-01-15 — Consent order issued against 214 Technologies (Trueface.ai) for falsely claiming EU-U.S. Privacy Shield certification on its facial recognition platform. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/1923019-openx-technologies-inc)

## Background

214 Technologies began a Privacy Shield certification application with the Department of Commerce in January 2018 but never completed the steps required to obtain certification. Despite this, it displayed Privacy Shield compliance statements in its privacy policy, deceiving consumers and EU individuals who relied on those representations for the lawful transfer and protection of their personal data.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of Privacy Shield misrepresentation: that Respondent represented, directly or indirectly and expressly or by implication, that it was a participant in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

A 20-year consent order (Docket C-4699) was issued, prohibiting Respondent from misrepresenting participation in or compliance with any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program. The order also requires acknowledgments, compliance reporting, 10-year recordkeeping, and ongoing compliance monitoring, and terminates January 13, 2040.

## Significance

This action illustrates the FTC's willingness to take enforcement action against AI and biometric technology companies for deceptive privacy certification claims, even absent a data breach or misuse of data. It signals that false Privacy Shield representations by emerging AI companies carry real regulatory risk.
