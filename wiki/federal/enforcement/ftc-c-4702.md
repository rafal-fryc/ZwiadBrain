## Summary

The FTC charged Thru, Inc. with deceptively claiming participation in both the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks when the company had never completed certification under either program. Although Thru initiated a certification application in June 2017, it continued displaying Privacy Shield compliance statements on its website without ever becoming certified. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting further misrepresentations about privacy program participation or certification.

## Background

Thru, Inc. is a Delaware corporation that provides cloud-based file transfer software. It posted a privacy policy claiming compliance with both the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks despite having only initiated — and never completed — a certification application with the U.S. Department of Commerce in June 2017.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of Privacy Shield misrepresentation under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, charging that Thru falsely represented, directly or by implication, that it was a certified participant in the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks when it had never obtained that certification.

## Outcome

Thru, Inc. agreed to a consent order (Docket C-4702) prohibiting it from misrepresenting participation in, certification by, or compliance with any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, including the Privacy Shield frameworks. The order imposes 20-year recordkeeping obligations and compliance monitoring, and runs until January 13, 2040.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's ongoing enforcement of Privacy Shield misrepresentation claims, reinforcing that companies must complete — not merely initiate — the certification process before publicly claiming framework compliance. It underscores that initiating an application without finalizing it affords no safe harbor against deception charges.
