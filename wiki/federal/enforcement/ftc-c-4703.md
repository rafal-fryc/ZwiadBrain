## Summary

The FTC charged Incentive Services, Inc. with falsely claiming on its website that it participated in the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks, when it had never completed either certification process. The matter resulted in a consent order prohibiting the company from misrepresenting its participation in any government or self-regulatory privacy program. The order carries a 20-year duration.

## Background

Incentive Services, Inc., a Minnesota corporation, displayed privacy policy statements on its website claiming adherence to the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks. The company initiated but never completed the self-certification process required by the U.S. Department of Commerce for either framework, yet continued to publish the false compliance claims.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count: that Incentive Services misrepresented, directly or by implication, that it was a certified participant in the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

A consent order (Docket C-4703) was finalized on January 15, 2020, prohibiting Incentive Services from misrepresenting its membership in or compliance with any government- or self-regulatory-organization-sponsored privacy or security program, including Privacy Shield and APEC CBPR. The order also imposes acknowledgment, compliance reporting, recordkeeping, and monitoring obligations for 20 years, terminating January 23, 2040.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's ongoing enforcement sweep against companies that falsely claim Privacy Shield certification, reinforcing that even incomplete or abandoned certification attempts do not permit companies to display Privacy Shield compliance language to consumers.
