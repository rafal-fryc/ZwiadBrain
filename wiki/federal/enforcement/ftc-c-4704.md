## Summary

The FTC charged TDARX, Inc. with falsely claiming active participation in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework after its certification lapsed in 2018, despite warnings from the Department of Commerce to remove those claims. The company also failed to obtain required annual attested verification while certified and did not affirm continuing data-protection obligations after lapsing. A final consent order was issued on January 15, 2020, prohibiting future misrepresentations and imposing a 20-year compliance regime.

## Background

TDARX, Inc. provided IT management and security services via its nocdoc.com website and obtained EU-U.S. Privacy Shield certification in 2017. That certification lapsed in 2018. The Department of Commerce warned the company to remove Privacy Shield claims from its website, but TDARX continued to represent itself as a current participant. While certified, it had also failed to obtain the required annual attested verification statement confirming its Privacy Shield practices were accurate and implemented.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts under FTC Act Section 5: (1) misrepresenting current participation in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield after certification had lapsed; (2) misrepresenting compliance with Privacy Shield verification requirements by failing to obtain the required annual attested verification statement; and (3) misrepresenting compliance with Privacy Shield continuing obligations by failing to affirm to the Department of Commerce that it would continue to protect personal data received during participation after ceasing to be certified.

## Outcome

A final consent order (Docket C-4704) was issued on January 15, 2020, effective upon publication on the FTC website and terminating January 23, 2040. The order prohibits TDARX from misrepresenting membership, adherence, certification, or participation in any government- or self-regulatory-sponsored privacy or security program, including Privacy Shield and APEC CBPR. It requires the company to either affirm continuing Privacy Shield data-protection obligations or delete covered data within 10 days, and imposes 10-year recordkeeping, compliance reporting, and monitoring requirements.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's sustained enforcement campaign against Privacy Shield misrepresentations, reinforcing that companies must promptly remove Privacy Shield claims when certification lapses and must fully satisfy the framework's annual verification and continuing-obligations requirements. It signals that even small technology firms offering security services are subject to FTC scrutiny when they misrepresent cross-border data-transfer compliance status.
