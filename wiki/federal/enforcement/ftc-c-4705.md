## Summary

The FTC charged Click Labs, Inc. with falsely claiming on its website that it had self-certified to the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks when it had never completed the certification process. The company operated jungleworks.com and displayed these false certification claims in its privacy policy. The matter was resolved through a consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about participation in privacy or security programs.

## Background

Click Labs, Inc. operated the website jungleworks.com and publicly represented in its privacy policy that it had self-certified to the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks, which would permit lawful personal data transfers from the EU and Switzerland to the United States. In reality, the company initiated but never completed the Department of Commerce certification application for either framework, yet continued displaying the false claims.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of Privacy Shield misrepresentation: that Click Labs falsely represented, directly or indirectly, that it was a participant in the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks, in violation of Section 5(a) of the FTC Act.

## Outcome

Click Labs entered into a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4705) prohibiting it from misrepresenting participation in or compliance with any government- or self-regulatory-sponsored privacy or security program, including EU-U.S. Privacy Shield, Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield, and APEC Cross-Border Privacy Rules. The order also imposes acknowledgment, compliance reporting, recordkeeping, and monitoring obligations, terminating January 23, 2040.

## Significance

This action is part of the FTC's ongoing enforcement against companies that falsely claim Privacy Shield certification — a deceptive practice that misleads consumers and EU counterparties about the legal basis for cross-border data transfers.
