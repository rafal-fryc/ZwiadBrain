## Summary

The FTC charged Global Data Vault, LLC, a Texas-based data storage and recovery company, with falsely claiming active participation in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework after its certification had expired, and with misrepresenting compliance with Privacy Shield verification and continuing-obligations requirements. The company was warned by the Department of Commerce to remove its participation claims but continued to make them. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy program membership and requiring the company to fulfill its Privacy Shield continuing obligations.

## Developments

- 2020-01-15 — FTC issued 20-year consent order against Global Data Vault for falsely claiming active EU-U.S. Privacy Shield participation after certification expired and misrepresenting compliance with verification and continuing-obligations requirements. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/192-3093-global-data-vault-llc-matter)

## Background

Global Data Vault held a Privacy Shield certification from 2017 to 2018 but failed to renew it. Despite warnings from the Department of Commerce, it continued to claim active participation on its website from July 2018 through May 2019. During the period it was certified, the company never obtained the required annual verification statement, and after its certification lapsed it failed to affirm to Commerce that it would continue protecting personal information received during participation.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts: (1) misrepresenting that Global Data Vault was a current EU-U.S. Privacy Shield participant when its certification had expired; (2) misrepresenting compliance with Privacy Shield Principles, including the annual verification requirement via self-assessment or outside review; and (3) misrepresenting compliance with Privacy Shield continuing-obligations requirements, specifically the duty to affirm to Commerce that Privacy Shield principles would continue to apply to personal data received during participation.

## Outcome

The FTC issued a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4706) prohibiting Global Data Vault from misrepresenting membership in or compliance with any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, including EU-U.S. Privacy Shield, Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield, and APEC CBPR. The order also required the company to either affirm continuing Privacy Shield obligations to the Department of Commerce or return/delete covered personal information within ten days, along with standard recordkeeping, compliance reporting, and monitoring provisions.

## Significance

The action is part of the FTC's sustained enforcement of Privacy Shield representation accuracy, reinforcing that companies must not claim active framework participation after certification lapses and must fulfill all annual verification and post-departure obligations while certified or upon leaving the program.
