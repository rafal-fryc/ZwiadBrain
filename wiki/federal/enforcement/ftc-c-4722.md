## Summary

The FTC charged Miniclip S.A., a Swiss mobile and online game developer, with falsely claiming to be a certified participant in the CARU COPPA safe harbor program for roughly four years after its certified status was terminated in July 2015. The false representations appeared on Miniclip's website and apps through at least June–July 2019. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting Miniclip from misrepresenting its participation in or compliance with any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program.

## Developments

- 2020-07-15 — Consent order issued prohibiting Miniclip from falsely claiming certified participation in the CARU COPPA safe harbor program after its status had been terminated since 2015. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/192-3129-miniclip-matter)

## Background

Miniclip joined the CARU COPPA safe harbor program in July 2009. CARU terminated Miniclip's participation on July 6, 2015. Despite losing its certified status, Miniclip continued to display statements on its Small Print page, Terms and Conditions page, and Facebook Games Privacy Policy page claiming it remained a certified, current participant through at least June–July 2019.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of COPPA Safe Harbor Misrepresentations under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, charging that Miniclip represented, directly or indirectly, expressly or by implication, that it was a current participant in the CARU COPPA safe harbor program when in fact its certified status had been terminated years earlier.

## Outcome

Miniclip entered into a consent order (Docket C-4722) effective July 15, 2020, and terminating June 29, 2040. The order prohibits Miniclip from misrepresenting participation in or compliance with any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program, imposes 10-year recordkeeping and 5-year retention requirements, mandates compliance reporting within 60 days and ongoing sworn notices, and subjects Miniclip to 20 years of FTC compliance monitoring.

## Significance

The action reinforces that falsely claiming membership in a recognized COPPA safe harbor program is itself a deceptive practice actionable under FTC Act Section 5, even absent a separate substantive COPPA violation, signaling that self-regulatory privacy seals carry legal weight and that lapsed certifications must be promptly removed from all consumer-facing materials.
