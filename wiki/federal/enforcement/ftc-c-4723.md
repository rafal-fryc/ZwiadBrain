## Summary

The FTC charged Ortho-Clinical Diagnostics with falsely claiming active participation in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework after its certification lapsed in 2018, even after the Department of Commerce warned it to remove those claims. The company also never obtained the required annual attested verification statement while certified, and failed to commit to continuing Privacy Shield protections for data collected during participation. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting future misrepresentations about privacy program membership.

## Background

Ortho-Clinical Diagnostics obtained EU-U.S. Privacy Shield certification in April 2017 but allowed it to lapse in 2018 without renewal. It continued to publish privacy policy statements claiming active compliance with the framework despite the lapse, and ignored an August 2018 Commerce Department warning to remove those claims. While certified, it never obtained the required annual attested verification statement confirming its Privacy Shield practices were implemented.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts under FTC Act Section 5: (1) falsely representing current participation in the EU-U.S. Privacy Shield framework after certification lapsed; (2) misrepresenting compliance with Privacy Shield principles by failing to obtain required annual verification; and (3) misrepresenting compliance with continuing obligations by failing to affirm to Commerce that it would protect personal data collected during program participation.

## Outcome

A 20-year consent order (Docket C-4723) was issued, prohibiting Ortho-Clinical Diagnostics from misrepresenting its membership in or compliance with any government or self-regulatory privacy or security program. The order also required the company to either affirm continuing Privacy Shield obligations to Commerce or return/delete personal data collected under the framework within ten days, along with standard compliance reporting and recordkeeping requirements for 10 years.

## Significance

The action reinforces FTC enforcement of Privacy Shield certification accuracy, making clear that allowing a certification to lapse while continuing to display compliance claims — even after a government warning — constitutes an unfair or deceptive practice under Section 5. It also highlights the ongoing obligations companies bear toward data collected during a privacy framework participation period, even after that participation ends.
