## Summary

The FTC charged Zoom with making deceptive claims about the security of its videoconferencing platform, including falsely asserting it offered end-to-end encryption and AES 256-bit encryption, and with secretly installing software on Mac computers that bypassed Apple's Safari privacy safeguards. The consent order requires Zoom to establish a comprehensive information security program, submit to biennial third-party assessments, and prohibits further misrepresentations about its data security practices. The order carries a 20-year duration.

## Background

Zoom falsely represented that its videoconferencing service used end-to-end encryption, when in fact Zoom's own servers retained the cryptographic keys. It also misrepresented its use of AES 256-bit encryption when it actually employed the weaker AES 128-bit encryption in ECB mode, and falsely claimed recorded meetings were stored encrypted immediately after sessions ended when recordings sat unencrypted on Zoom servers for up to 60 days. Additionally, Zoom secretly installed the ZoomOpener web server on Mac users' computers without adequate notice or consent, circumventing a Safari browser privacy safeguard and exposing approximately 3.8 million users to remote code execution and denial-of-service vulnerabilities.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged five counts: (1) deceptive representation regarding end-to-end encryption; (2) deceptive representation regarding the level of encryption (256-bit vs. actual 128-bit ECB); (3) deceptive representation regarding secured cloud storage for recorded meetings; (4) unfair circumvention of a third-party privacy and security safeguard through secret installation of ZoomOpener; and (5) deceptive failure to disclose the true nature of a Mac app update that actually installed the ZoomOpener web server.

## Outcome

Zoom entered into a consent order (Docket C-4731) prohibiting misrepresentations about its data collection, use, security, and disclosure practices. Zoom was required to implement a comprehensive information security program within 60 days, obtain initial and biennial independent third-party assessments, submit annual compliance certifications from senior management, report covered security incidents to the FTC within 30 days, and maintain five years of specified records. The order is effective for 20 years.

## Significance

This action established that deceptive security claims — including misrepresenting encryption strength and secretly installing software to circumvent platform-level privacy controls — constitute unfair or deceptive acts under FTC Act Section 5. It also set a high-profile precedent for mandatory comprehensive security programs and independent third-party assessments for major videoconferencing platforms, reinforcing that security marketing claims carry legal accountability.
