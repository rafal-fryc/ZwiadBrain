## Summary

The FTC charged Everalbum with two misrepresentations: enabling facial recognition by default without user consent while implying users had to opt in, and retaining users' photos and videos after account deactivation despite promising permanent deletion. The company also used facial images from users' photos to train its commercial face recognition technology (Paravision) without adequate disclosure. The matter resolved through a 20-year consent order requiring deletion of biometric data, affirmative express consent before future AI training, and prohibitions on misrepresentation.

## Background

Everalbum operated the Ever photo storage app. It enabled face recognition by default for all mobile users without an opt-out, contrary to representations suggesting the feature required affirmative user action. It used facial images from users' photos to train proprietary face recognition technology later commercialized as Paravision. When users deactivated accounts, Everalbum represented that photos and videos would be permanently deleted, but until at least October 2019 retained that content indefinitely.

## Allegations

Count 1 alleged misrepresentation regarding users' ability to control the Ever app's face recognition feature — Everalbum represented it was not using face recognition unless the user enabled it, when in fact it was on by default. Count 2 alleged misrepresentation regarding deletion of photos upon account deactivation — Everalbum represented it would delete photos and videos when accounts were deactivated but did not do so.

## Outcome

The FTC issued a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4743) prohibiting misrepresentations about data collection, use, and deletion. Everalbum must obtain affirmative express consent before using biometric information to create face embeddings or train face recognition models, and must delete photos, videos, face embeddings, and related work product from affected users. The order includes recordkeeping, compliance reporting, and FTC monitoring provisions. No monetary penalty is stated in the text.

## Significance

This is a landmark FTC action establishing that using consumer photos to train commercial AI models without adequate disclosure and consent — and failing to honor deletion promises — constitutes an unfair or deceptive practice under FTC Act Section 5. It is among the earliest FTC enforcement actions directly targeting biometric data and AI training data practices, and it required affirmative deletion of AI-derived work product (face embeddings), not just source data.
