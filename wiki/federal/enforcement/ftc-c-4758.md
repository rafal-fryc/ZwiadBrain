## Summary

The FTC charged Ascension Data & Analytics, an analytics firm providing mortgage-related services, with violating the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Safeguards Rule by transferring sensitive mortgage documents covering roughly 60,593 consumers to a vendor it never security-vetted. The vendor misconfigured its cloud storage, leaving names, Social Security numbers, credit account numbers, and financial data openly accessible on the internet for approximately one year. Ascension also failed to require adequate contractual safeguards from its service providers. The matter resolved as a 20-year consent order.

## Developments

- 2021-12-15 — FTC issued 20-year consent order against Ascension Data & Analytics for GLB Safeguards Rule violations stemming from failure to vet a mortgage-document vendor whose cloud misconfiguration exposed sensitive data of 60,593 consumers. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/192-3126-ascension-data-analytics-llc-matter)

## Background

Ascension contracted with a third-party vendor, OpticsML, to perform OCR scanning on mortgage documents containing sensitive consumer personal information. Despite having a written policy requiring security vetting of service providers, Ascension conducted no assessment of OpticsML's security practices before transferring the data. OpticsML misconfigured its cloud storage, leaving the data publicly accessible without a password from approximately January 2018 to January 2019. During that period roughly 52 unauthorized IP addresses accessed the exposed data, including from Russia and China.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged a single count: violation of the GLB Safeguards Rule (16 C.F.R. Part 314). Specifically, Ascension failed to take reasonable steps to select and retain service providers capable of maintaining appropriate safeguards, failed to require service providers by contract to implement such safeguards, and failed to identify reasonably foreseeable risks to the security, confidentiality, and integrity of customer information or to assess the sufficiency of safeguards in place to control those risks.

## Outcome

The matter resolved through a consent order (Docket C-4758) issued December 15, 2021, effective for 20 years through December 22, 2041. The order prohibits future GLB Safeguards Rule violations, mandates a comprehensive data security program for covered businesses, requires initial and biennial independent third-party security assessments, mandates annual senior-officer compliance certifications, requires prompt reporting of covered incidents to the FTC, and imposes detailed recordkeeping and compliance monitoring obligations.

## Significance

This action underscores that companies sharing sensitive consumer financial data with third-party vendors bear direct regulatory responsibility for vetting those vendors' security practices and imposing contractual safeguards — even when the actual misconfiguration is caused by the vendor. It is one of the first FTC enforcement actions under the GLB Safeguards Rule to center on third-party vendor oversight failures rather than the company's own internal security deficiencies.
