## Summary

The FTC alleged that CafePress, operated by Residual Pumpkin Entity, LLC, failed to implement reasonable data security measures, resulting in a 2019 breach that exposed over 20 million email addresses, Social Security numbers, and payment card data belonging to consumers and shopkeepers. The company also misled consumers about the breach, falsely claimed Privacy Shield compliance, ignored data deletion requests from EU and Swiss residents, and withheld commissions from shopkeepers whose accounts were closed after the breach. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order requiring a $500,000 payment, a comprehensive information security program, and biennial third-party assessments.

## Background

CafePress collected extensive personal information from consumers and shopkeepers but failed to protect it against well-known attack vectors including SQL injection and cross-site scripting. In February 2019, a hacker exploited these vulnerabilities and exfiltrated over 20 million unencrypted email addresses, millions of names and addresses, more than 180,000 Social Security numbers, and payment card data. Despite early warnings, CafePress delayed consumer notification, implemented an inadequate password reset procedure, sent unsolicited marketing emails, failed to honor EU/Swiss data deletion requests, withheld shopkeeper commissions after breach-related account closures, and falsely represented compliance with EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks.

## Allegations

The FTC charged seven counts: (1) misrepresenting that reasonable security measures protected personal information; (2) misrepresenting that appropriate steps were taken after the security incident; (3) unfair data security practices for failing to employ reasonable safeguards; (4) misrepresenting that email addresses would only be used for order notifications and receipts; (5) falsely claiming adherence to the EU-U.S. and Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield frameworks; (6) misrepresenting that it honored data erasure and direct-marketing restriction requests from EEA and Swiss residents; and (7) unfairly withholding payable commissions from shopkeepers whose accounts were closed after the breach, including charging a $25 closure fee against hacked accounts.

## Outcome

Residual Pumpkin Entity, LLC entered into a 20-year consent order requiring payment of $500,000, establishment of a comprehensive information security program within 60 days, biennial independent third-party assessments of that program, annual sworn compliance certifications, covered incident reporting to the FTC within 30 days of discovery, provision of customer information to facilitate shopkeeper commission redress, and 20 years of recordkeeping.

## Significance

The CafePress order illustrates the FTC's use of Section 5 to address the full lifecycle of a data breach — inadequate pre-breach security, deceptive post-breach response, and retaliatory financial harm to affected parties. The simultaneous pursuit of Privacy Shield misrepresentation and EU/Swiss deletion-request failures alongside domestic security failures signals FTC willingness to enforce cross-border privacy commitments even under Section 5 alone, without a separate privacy statute.
