## Summary

The FTC charged Drizly, LLC and its CEO with unfair and deceptive data security practices after a 2020 breach exposed the personal information of approximately 2.5 million consumers. The company stored sensitive credentials in public GitHub repositories and failed to enforce basic access controls or multifactor authentication. A final consent order requires Drizly to delete unnecessary consumer data, implement a comprehensive information security program, and submit to independent third-party security assessments for 20 years. CEO James Cory Rellas is personally bound to maintain adequate security programs at future companies he leads.

## Background

Drizly, LLC operated an alcohol e-commerce platform and publicly represented that it used industry-standard security measures. Despite these representations, the company stored AWS and database credentials insecurely in GitHub repositories, failed to enforce strong passwords or multifactor authentication, and lacked monitoring or access controls. In July 2020, a malicious actor exploited these weaknesses to exfiltrate personal data — including names, email addresses, and passwords — on approximately 2.5 million consumers, with the stolen data subsequently offered for sale on dark web forums. CEO James Cory Rellas was found personally responsible for the failures, having neglected to hire a senior security executive or properly delegate information security oversight.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged two counts: (1) unfair information security practices — Drizly and its CEO failed to employ reasonable security measures to protect consumers' personal information; and (2) deceptive security statements — Drizly represented, expressly or by implication, that it used appropriate safeguards to protect consumers' personal information, when it did not.

## Outcome

A 20-year consent order (Docket C-4780) was finalized on January 15, 2023. Drizly must delete all consumer data not needed for customer services, maintain a documented data retention schedule, and implement a comprehensive information security program subject to biennial independent third-party assessments. CEO Rellas is personally required for 10 years to ensure any company he leads as a majority owner or senior information security officer establishes an adequate security program within 180 days. Annual compliance certifications and prompt reporting of future covered incidents to the FTC are also required.

## Significance

The order is notable for imposing personal, forward-looking security obligations on the CEO individually — not just the corporate respondent — establishing a precedent that executives who neglect cybersecurity oversight may face direct, long-term regulatory constraints that follow them to future employers.
