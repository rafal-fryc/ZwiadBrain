## Summary

The FTC charged BetterHelp, an online mental health counseling platform, with secretly sharing consumers' sensitive mental health information with Facebook, Snapchat, Pinterest, and Criteo for targeted advertising purposes despite repeatedly promising that such information would remain strictly private. The company also falsely implied HIPAA certification through seals displayed on its websites. The matter resolved via a finalized consent order requiring a $7.8 million payment, mandatory deletion of improperly shared data, and a 20-year privacy program with independent assessments.

## Background

From 2013 to December 2020, BetterHelp collected sensitive health data through intake questionnaires — including therapy history, medication status, and mental health responses — and disclosed that data to third-party advertising platforms without consumer consent, allowing those platforms to use the data for their own research and product development. BetterHelp displayed HIPAA compliance seals on every page of its websites and had customer service representatives tell consumers it was 'HIPAA certified,' despite never having been reviewed for HIPAA compliance by any government or third-party authority. The practices ended in December 2020 following investigative journalism and an FTC inquiry.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged eight counts against BetterHelp: unfair privacy practices for failing to implement reasonable safeguards including written standards and employee training; failure to obtain affirmative express consent before sharing health data; multiple deceptive misrepresentations that health information would not be shared with third parties for advertising or third parties' own uses; a misrepresentation through the November 2018 privacy policy that health information would not be used for advertising; a misrepresentation via intake questionnaire banners that health data would remain private between consumers and their counselors; and a deceptive implied claim of HIPAA certification through the display of HIPAA seals and customer service representations.

## Outcome

BetterHelp agreed to a finalized consent order (Docket C-4796) requiring payment of $7,800,000 to the FTC for consumer redress administered by an independent third-party Redress Administrator; a permanent prohibition on disclosing covered health information to any third party for advertising purposes; mandatory notification and deletion of improperly shared data from all recipient third parties; a comprehensive privacy program established within 60 days; biennial independent third-party privacy assessments for 20 years; annual senior-officer compliance certifications for 10 years; covered incident reporting within 30 days of discovery; and 20 years of recordkeeping obligations.

## Significance

This action marked the FTC's first use of its Health Breach Notification Rule authority in a case involving a non-HIPAA-covered mental health platform sharing sensitive data for advertising, signaling that the Commission would apply consumer protection principles aggressively to digital health services. The $7.8 million penalty and 20-year oversight period, combined with mandatory data deletion from advertising partners, established a benchmark for enforcement against health-tech companies that monetize sensitive data in violation of their privacy promises.
