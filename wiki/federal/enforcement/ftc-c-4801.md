## Summary

The FTC charged Global Tel*Link Corporation (GTL), Telmate, and TouchPay Holdings with unfair data security practices after they copied 649,500 incarcerated individuals' personal data to an unprotected AWS test environment in August 2020, exposing it to the internet for at least two days and allowing roughly 44 billion bytes of data to be exfiltrated. Respondents then misled consumers and correctional facilities about the breach's scope, delayed consumer notification by approximately nine months, and continued to misrepresent in bid responses that they had never suffered a significant data breach. The matter resolved as a finalized 20-year consent order issued February 15, 2024.

## Background

GTL, Telmate, and TouchPay provided communications and payment services to incarcerated individuals and their contacts, collecting highly sensitive personal information. In August 2020, a vendor technician changed security settings in an unprotected AWS test environment—lacking encryption, monitoring, firewalls, or access controls—that contained real consumer data, exposing approximately 649,500 individuals' records to the internet for at least two days.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged six counts: (1) unfair data security practices for storing consumer data in an unprotected test environment; (2) unfair failure to timely notify affected consumers of the breach; (3) misrepresentations about the adequacy of their data security measures; (4) misrepresentations to individual users claiming no sensitive information was affected; (5) misrepresentations to users about timely notification; and (6) deceptive representations to correctional facilities in RFP responses that respondents had never experienced a significant data breach.

## Outcome

A 20-year consent order (Docket C-4801) requires respondents to implement a comprehensive information security program within 60 days, undergo biennial independent third-party security assessments for 20 years, provide two years of FTC-approved credit monitoring and identity theft protection to affected consumers, notify affected consumers and facilities of future covered incidents within 30 days, post breach-notice banners on all websites and apps for one year, send direct notices to affected consumers who never received written notice, and submit annual senior-manager compliance certifications to the FTC.

## Significance

The action is notable for its focus on a vulnerable population—incarcerated individuals with limited ability to protect themselves or monitor for identity theft—and for the FTC charging both the data security failures and the subsequent deceptive cover-up, including misrepresentations in government procurement responses. The order's credit-monitoring provision was specifically tailored to ensure accessibility for consumers who are incarcerated.
