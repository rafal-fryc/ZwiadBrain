## Summary

The FTC charged X-Mode Social, a location data broker, with unfairly collecting precise consumer location data through SDKs embedded in over 300 mobile apps and selling it — including data tied to visits to sensitive locations such as medical facilities, houses of worship, and domestic abuse shelters — to government contractors without adequate disclosure or consumer consent. X-Mode also ignored Android opt-out signals and created audience segments based on sensitive health-related characteristics. The matter resolved through a 20-year consent order imposing sweeping prohibitions and affirmative obligations on data handling.

## Background

X-Mode Social (later Outlogic) embedded SDKs in over 300 mobile apps to harvest precise location data, which it sold to hundreds of clients including private government contractors. From June 2018 to July 2020, it collected and sold data from consumers who had opted out of personalized advertising on Android devices. Its own apps — Drunk Mode and Walk Against Humanity — and the sample disclosures it supplied to third-party app publishers failed to inform consumers that their location data would be sold for national security purposes. X-Mode also built and sold custom audience segments categorizing consumers by visits to cardiology, endocrinology, and gastroenterology offices.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged seven counts under FTC Act Section 5: (1) unfair sale of sensitive location data revealing visits to medical, reproductive-health, religious, mental-health, shelter, and addiction-recovery facilities; (2) unfair failure to honor consumer opt-out privacy choices on Android; (3) unfair collection and use of location data through X-Mode's own apps without informed consent; (4) unfair collection through third-party SDK apps without consent verification; (5) unfair categorization of consumers based on sensitive health characteristics for marketing; (6) deceptive failure to disclose that location data would be used for government and national-security purposes; and (7) furnishing third-party publishers with misleading sample disclosure language as a means and instrumentality of deception.

## Outcome

X-Mode agreed to a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4802) that prohibits selling or disclosing sensitive location data, requires deletion of historic location data and derived data products within 60–90 days, mandates a Sensitive Location Data Program and a comprehensive privacy program within 60–180 days, obligates a Supplier Assessment Program to verify third-party consent, requires consumer-facing controls for withdrawing consent and requesting deletion, and imposes ongoing compliance reporting, recordkeeping, and FTC monitoring.

## Significance

This order is one of the most detailed FTC actions against a location data broker and establishes a baseline for how companies that monetize sensitive location data must obtain consent, honor opt-outs, screen downstream recipients, and delete data — including data tied to visits to health, religious, and shelter locations. The 20-year duration and requirement to notify downstream customers of required deletions signal that the FTC will hold brokers accountable for the full data supply chain.
