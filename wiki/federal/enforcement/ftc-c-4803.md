## Summary

The FTC charged InMarket Media, LLC with deceptively and unfairly collecting precise location data from approximately 100 million devices annually through its own apps and a third-party SDK, while falsely telling consumers the data was used only for app functionality such as earning reward points or list reminders. In reality, InMarket used the data to build detailed consumer profiles for targeted advertising and retained it for up to five years. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting the sale or licensing of location data and imposing comprehensive data governance obligations.

## Developments

- 2024-05-15 — FTC issued a 20-year consent order against InMarket Media prohibiting the sale or licensing of location data and requiring deletion of historic location data collected without affirmative express consent, resolving charges of deceptive and unfair location-data collection through its apps and third-party SDK. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/2023088-inmarket-media-llc)

## Background

InMarket Media operated the CheckPoints and ListEase consumer apps and supplied an SDK embedded in over 300 third-party apps. Consent screens in InMarket's own apps framed location access as necessary for core features while omitting that the data fed advertising-profile pipelines. InMarket also failed to verify whether third-party app developers obtained informed consent before the SDK collected location data, and it retained sensitive movement data — capable of revealing home, workplace, place of worship, medical facility, or political activity — for five years with no adequate business justification.

## Allegations

The FTC brought four counts under FTC Act Section 5: (1) unfair collection and use of location data through InMarket's own apps without disclosing the advertising-profiling purpose; (2) unfair collection of location data through third-party SDK apps without taking reasonable steps to verify consumer notice; (3) unfair retention of detailed, sensitive location data for five years; and (4) deceptive failure to disclose that location data collected via CheckPoints and ListEase would be used beyond the stated in-app functionality purposes.

## Outcome

The FTC issued a 20-year consent order (Docket C-4803, effective 2024-05-15) that permanently prohibits InMarket from selling or licensing location data, from offering products or services that categorize or target consumers based on sensitive location data, and from misrepresenting its data practices. InMarket must delete historic location data collected without documented affirmative express consent, establish a Sensitive Location Data Program and an SDK Supplier Assessment Program within 90 days, implement a comprehensive privacy program within 60 days, publish a data retention schedule, provide consumers a simple deletion-request mechanism, and notify affected consumers. Annual compliance reporting and 20-year recordkeeping obligations apply.

## Significance

This order is a significant FTC enforcement benchmark for the location-data brokerage and mobile advertising industries, establishing that SDK-based collection of location data without meaningful consumer consent is an unfair practice even when the direct consumer relationship is held by a third-party app developer. The outright prohibition on selling or licensing location data — rather than a consent-based carve-out — signals the Commission's willingness to impose structural remedies on data brokers that traffic in sensitive movement information.
