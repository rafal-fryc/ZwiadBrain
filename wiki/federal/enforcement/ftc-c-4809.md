## Summary

The FTC charged IntelliVision Technologies Corp. with making false and unsubstantiated claims about its AI-based facial recognition software, including assertions of zero racial or gender bias, top-tier accuracy, and anti-spoofing capabilities that photos and videos could not defeat. NIST testing contradicted the bias and accuracy claims, and the company lacked any testing to support them. The matter was resolved through a 20-year consent order prohibiting misrepresentations and requiring substantiation before making performance or bias claims.

## Developments

- 2025-01-15 — Consent order issued requiring IntelliVision to cease unsubstantiated facial recognition bias, accuracy, and anti-spoofing claims and to substantiate any future performance representations with competent testing. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/232-3023-intellivision-matter)

## Background

IntelliVision marketed its facial recognition software to OEMs, integrators, and developers claiming the system operated without racial or gender bias, ranked among the highest in accuracy on the market, and used anti-spoofing technology that could not be fooled by photos or videos. NIST testing found the algorithms produced differing error rates across demographic groups and were not among the top 100 best-performing algorithms. The company had no testing to support its bias, accuracy, or anti-spoofing representations. Additionally, contrary to its claim of training on millions of faces, the software was trained on approximately 100,000 unique individuals with artificially generated variants.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts under FTC Act Section 5: (1) misrepresentations about the software's accuracy and training data volume; (2) false or unsubstantiated claims that the software detected faces of all ethnicities with zero racial or gender bias; and (3) false or unsubstantiated claims that its anti-spoofing technology could not be defeated by photo or video images.

## Outcome

IntelliVision entered a 20-year consent order prohibiting express or implied misrepresentations about the accuracy, bias performance, or liveness detection of its facial recognition technology. The order also bars any accuracy or bias claim unless the company first possesses competent and reliable testing that substantiates it. Provisions require acknowledgment delivery to personnel, annual compliance reporting, 15-year recordkeeping, and ongoing FTC monitoring.

## Significance

This action is an early FTC enforcement matter squarely targeting algorithmic bias and AI performance claims, establishing that vendors of facial recognition systems must have substantiated, test-backed evidence before making accuracy or fairness representations — and that demographic disparity in error rates directly contradicts zero-bias marketing language.
