## Summary

The FTC charged Gravy Analytics and its subsidiary Venntel with unfairly collecting and selling precise mobile geolocation data from roughly one billion devices daily without verifying consumer consent. The data exposed sensitive characteristics including health decisions, religious practices, political activity, and LGBTQ+ identification. Gravy Analytics also derived and sold audience segments categorizing consumers by these sensitive traits. The matter resolved in a 20-year consent order imposing broad data-handling restrictions and deletion obligations.

## Developments

- 2024-12-15 — FTC issued a 20-year consent order against Gravy Analytics and Venntel for selling precise mobile location data revealing consumers' sensitive characteristics without verifying consent, requiring deletion of historic data and broad privacy-program obligations. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/212-3035-gravy-analytics-inc-matter)

## Background

Gravy Analytics and Venntel collected precise mobile location data — accurate to approximately one meter — and sold it to commercial and government customers. Neither company took adequate steps to confirm consumer consent, and in documented instances they continued using data after learning consent was absent or deficient. Gravy Analytics further built and sold audience segments tied to sensitive characteristics derived from that location data.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged three counts under FTC Act Section 5(a): (1) unfair sale of sensitive location data revealing visits to medical facilities, religious sites, LGBTQ+ locations, domestic abuse shelters, and political events; (2) unfair collection and use of location data without reasonable steps to verify informed consumer consent for commercial and government purposes; and (3) unfair sale of sensitive inferences — audience segments categorizing consumers by health conditions, political activities, and religious beliefs — derived from location data.

## Outcome

A 20-year consent order prohibits respondents from selling or using sensitive location data tied to designated sensitive locations, requires deletion of all historic location data within 60 days and all derived data products within 90 days, mandates a Supplier Assessment Program to verify consent from data sources, requires a comprehensive privacy program with board-level reporting, and gives consumers rights to withdraw consent, request deletion, and identify recipients of their data.

## Significance

This action is one of the FTC's most expansive location-data enforcement orders, establishing that selling precise geolocation data revealing sensitive characteristics without consent verification is an unfair practice under Section 5. The deletion mandate for historic data and derived products, combined with 20-year oversight, sets a high baseline for the commercial location-data industry and signals FTC scrutiny of downstream inference products built on location data.
