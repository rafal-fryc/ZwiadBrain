## Summary

The FTC charged Mobilewalla, a data broker, with unfairly collecting and selling consumers' precise location data — including data revealing visits to medical clinics, places of worship, and domestic abuse shelters — without meaningful consumer consent. Mobilewalla also harvested bid-request data from real-time bidding exchanges even when it lost auctions, in violation of exchange terms, and retained vast repositories of sensitive location data indefinitely. A finalized consent order imposes a 20-year ban on selling sensitive location data, mandates deletion of historic location data, and requires a comprehensive privacy program.

## Developments

- 2025-01-15 — FTC consent order finalised against Mobilewalla for unfairly collecting and selling sensitive consumer location data via RTB exchanges without meaningful consent, imposing a 20-year prohibition on selling sensitive location data and requiring deletion of all historic location data. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/202-3196-mobilewalla-inc-matter)

## Background

Mobilewalla collected massive amounts of consumer location data primarily from real-time bidding (RTB) exchanges and third-party data brokers without consumers' knowledge or meaningful consent, retaining bid request data even when it did not win ad auctions and despite exchange terms expressly prohibiting such collection. It sold raw, non-anonymized location data and audience segments categorizing consumers by sensitive characteristics including medical conditions, religion, sexual orientation, and political activity, covering billions of devices over years of historical movement data.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged five counts of unfair practices under FTC Act Section 5: (1) selling sensitive location data revealing visits to medical facilities, religious sites, LGBTQ+ venues, domestic abuse shelters, and welfare shelters; (2) targeting consumers into audience segments based on sensitive characteristics derived from location data and selling those segments to third parties; (3) collecting personal and location data from RTB exchanges even without a winning bid in violation of exchange terms; (4) collecting location data without taking reasonable steps to verify consumer consent, relying only on vague supplier assurances and minimal spot checks; and (5) indefinitely retaining detailed sensitive movement data covering billions of devices.

## Outcome

Mobilewalla agreed to a consent order prohibiting it from selling or using sensitive location data tied to sensitive locations, banning collection of data from advertising auctions for non-auction purposes, prohibiting disclosure of private residence location data, and requiring deletion of all historic location data and derived products. The order mandates a Sensitive Location Data Program, a Supplier Assessment Program, consumer consent-withdrawal and deletion mechanisms, a data retention schedule with defined deletion timeframes, and a comprehensive privacy program — all subject to 20 years of FTC oversight.

## Significance

The action is a significant FTC enforcement milestone against data brokers operating in the real-time bidding ecosystem, establishing that scraping bid-stream location data beyond auction use violates FTC Act Section 5 and that selling location data tied to sensitive venues without verified consent is an unfair practice. The order's affirmative obligations — including consumer-facing deletion and consent-withdrawal tools and mandatory supplier vetting — set a template for data broker privacy compliance.
