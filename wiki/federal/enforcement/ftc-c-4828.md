## Summary

The FTC alleged that General Motors and OnStar collected precise geolocation and driving behavior data from millions of vehicles every three seconds and sold it to consumer reporting agencies without consumers' meaningful informed consent, while falsely representing the Smart Driver feature as a personal self-assessment tool. The consent order, docketed as C-4828, imposes a 20-year compliance regime including a five-year ban on selling covered driver data to consumer reporting agencies, mandatory affirmative express consent requirements, data minimization, and deletion obligations.

## Background

General Motors and OnStar enrolled consumers in the Smart Driver feature through an enrollment flow that bundled opt-in with unrelated notification features and omitted disclosures about third-party data sales. Collected data — including precise geolocation sampled every three seconds — was sold to Verisk Analytics and LexisNexis, which used it to compile insurance-related consumer reports. Consumers consequently faced loss of auto insurance coverage and unexpected premium increases.

## Allegations

Count 1 charged unfair collection and sale of sensitive geolocation and driver behavior data to third parties, including consumer reporting agencies, without affirmative express consent prior to collection. Count 2 charged deceptive failure to disclose that Smart Driver data would be shared with and sold to third parties, while representing to consumers it was solely a personal driving self-assessment tool.

## Outcome

The FTC issued a final consent order requiring: a five-year prohibition on disclosing covered driver data to consumer reporting agencies; affirmative express consent before any collection, use, or third-party disclosure of covered driver data (within 180 days); data minimization and documented retention schedules; deletion of all previously retained covered driver data (within 180 days) with a 90-day window to seek retroactive consent; consumer rights to obtain copies of or delete their data; mandatory deletion requests to prior data-sharing partners; and a 20-year compliance, recordkeeping, and monitoring regime.

## Significance

This action establishes that bundling a sensitive data collection opt-in with unrelated product features — without clear disclosure of third-party sales to consumer reporting agencies — constitutes both an unfair practice and a deceptive omission under FTC Act Section 5. It signals FTC scrutiny of automakers' connected-vehicle data ecosystems and sets a precedent for requiring affirmative express consent before monetizing granular mobility data.
