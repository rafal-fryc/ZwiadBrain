## Summary

The FTC charged Ruby Corp., operator of AshleyMadison.com, with deceiving consumers through fake female profiles, false security representations including a fabricated 'Trusted Security Award,' and a paid account-deletion service that secretly retained user data. The charges followed a July 2015 breach that exposed personal and billing information of over 36 million members. Ruby Corp. settled via a consent order requiring an $8.75 million judgment, a 20-year data security program, and biennial third-party security assessments.

## Background

Ruby Corp. and its subsidiaries operated AshleyMadison.com, a dating site for people seeking extramarital affairs. The company created thousands of fake 'engager profiles' staffed by its own employees to lure male users into paid memberships, falsely claimed the site held a 'Trusted Security Award' that never existed, and charged $19 for a 'Full Delete' service that retained personal data for up to 12 months. In July 2015 hackers published the personal and billing information of more than 36 million members, exposing the company's security failures.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged five counts under FTC Act Section 5(a): (1) misrepresenting network security through trust badges and executive claims that the site was '100% secure' and 'completely anonymous'; (2) misrepresenting that messages to male members came from real women; (3) misrepresenting that the $19 Full Delete option would completely erase all user data; (4) falsely displaying a 'Trusted Security Award' seal; and (5) unfair security practices including failure to maintain a written security policy, implement access controls, train personnel, oversee third-party vendors, or monitor system logs.

## Outcome

Ruby Corp. entered a consent order imposing a $8,750,000 judgment (with $828,500 due within 7 days and the remainder suspended subject to financial conditions), a permanent ban on misrepresenting data practices and profile authenticity, a mandatory comprehensive written information security program, biennial independent third-party security assessments by CISSP/CISA/GIAC-certified professionals for 20 years, and 20-year recordkeeping and compliance-reporting obligations.

## Significance

The Ashley Madison settlement was a high-profile signal that the FTC would pursue companies whose deceptive security claims and fake-profile practices caused serious consumer harm, and that a catastrophic data breach resulting from neglected basic security measures would draw both financial penalties and long-term structural oversight. The 20-year order duration and mandatory third-party assessments reflected the Commission's willingness to impose extended remedial regimes on companies handling sensitive personal information.
