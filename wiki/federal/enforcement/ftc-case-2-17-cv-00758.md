## Summary

The FTC alleged that VIZIO and its subsidiary VIZIO Inscape Services secretly collected second-by-second television viewing data from over 10 million consumers via Automated Content Recognition software enabled by default, without disclosure or consent, and sold that data to third parties for targeted advertising. VIZIO described the feature only as providing 'program offers and suggestions,' which was both misleading and never delivered. The matter was resolved through a consent order requiring affirmative consent before data collection, deletion of previously collected data, a 20-year privacy program, and monetary relief totaling $2.5 million.

## Background

Beginning in February 2014, VIZIO enabled proprietary ACR software by default on its internet-connected televisions to collect granular viewing data on a second-by-second basis, storing it indefinitely and appending demographic information such as age, income, and marital status. The 'Smart Interactivity' setting that controlled this tracking was described only as enabling program offers and suggestions, with no mention of the actual surveillance and data-sharing practices.

## Allegations

The FTC charged three counts: (1) unfair tracking without consumer consent, alleging VIZIO used ACR technology to collect, store, and share television viewing data through a medium consumers would not expect to be used for tracking; (2) deceptive omission, alleging VIZIO misrepresented the Smart Interactivity feature as enabling program suggestions while concealing its true data-collection purpose; and (3) deceptive representation, alleging VIZIO promised program offers and suggestions it never actually delivered. All charges were brought under FTC Act Section 5(a).

## Outcome

Under the consent order, VIZIO was required to obtain affirmative express consent before collecting any viewing data, delete all viewing data collected before March 1, 2016 within 120 days, implement a comprehensive written privacy program, and submit to biennial third-party privacy assessments for 20 years. VIZIO and Inscape Services paid $1,500,000 to the FTC and $1,000,000 to the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs.

## Significance

The action was one of the first FTC enforcement matters targeting covert data collection through consumer electronics beyond computers and smartphones, establishing that smart television manufacturers must obtain affirmative consent before using ACR technology and cannot obscure surveillance practices behind vague feature descriptions. The joint resolution with New Jersey also signaled coordinated state-federal privacy enforcement in the connected-device space. This order is the direct precedent behind the May 2026 Texas lawsuit against Netflix over ACR biometric surveillance noted on the TDPSA wiki page.
