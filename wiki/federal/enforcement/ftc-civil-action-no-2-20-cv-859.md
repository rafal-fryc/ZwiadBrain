## Summary

The FTC charged Kohl's Department Stores with violating FCRA Section 609(e) by refusing identity theft victims access to application and business transaction records tied to fraudulent purchases made in their names. Kohl's implemented progressively more restrictive policies between 2017 and 2019, ultimately limiting record disclosures to law enforcement only, and repeatedly ignored victim complaints. The matter was resolved through a consent order requiring Kohl's to comply with FCRA disclosure obligations and pay a $220,000 civil penalty.

## Developments

- 2020-06-15 — Consent order entered requiring Kohl's to provide FCRA Section 609(e) transaction records to identity theft victims and pay a $220,000 civil penalty after the retailer systematically denied victims access to records of fraudulent purchases made in their names. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/182-3200-kohls-department-stores-inc)

## Background

Beginning in February 2017, Kohl's changed its policy to share identifying transaction information only with law enforcement or a victim's attorney; in August 2018 it further restricted access so only law enforcement could obtain detailed order information. These policies remained in effect through March 2019. Kohl's ignored repeated complaints from identity theft victims — including those who sent the text of the FCRA and FTC guidance — and only changed its policy six months after receiving a Civil Investigative Demand from the FTC. On multiple occasions Kohl's also failed to respond to victims within the required 30-day window.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged two counts: (1) that Kohl's failed to provide required application and business transaction records to identity theft victims upon request, in violation of FCRA Section 609(e); and (2) that on multiple occasions Kohl's refused to provide those records within the required 30-day response window.

## Outcome

A consent order was entered under Civil Action No. 2:20-cv-859. Kohl's was permanently enjoined from failing to timely provide FCRA Section 609(e) records to identity theft victims within 30 days of a valid written request. Kohl's was required to pay a $220,000 civil penalty to the U.S. Treasury within 7 days, post a website notice directing victims on how to request records, send records or notices to previously affected victims, and maintain 10 years of compliance records.

## Significance

The action reinforced that retailers operating credit or transaction accounts have affirmative obligations under FCRA Section 609(e) to furnish records to identity theft victims, and that internal policies restricting such access — even when framed as fraud-control measures — violate federal law. The case also demonstrated FTC willingness to pursue civil penalties against retailers for data-access denials harming fraud victims.
