## Summary

The FTC charged LifeLock with falsely marketing its identity theft protection service as comprehensive when it covered only a narrow subset of identity theft scenarios. The company also made false claims about encrypting and securing customer data, while in reality storing and transmitting sensitive personal information in clear text with inadequate access controls. A $100 million judgment was entered against LifeLock under an amended consent order.

## Developments

- 2015-12-17 — Amended consent order entered imposing $100 million judgment against LifeLock for falsely marketing identity theft protection services and failing to implement adequate data security practices. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/072-3069-x100023-lifelock-inc-corporation)

## Background

LifeLock marketed an identity theft prevention service to over one million consumers at $10/month, primarily based on placing Initial Fraud Alerts with credit bureaus. The service did not protect against misuse of existing accounts, medical identity theft, or employment-related theft. LifeLock collected sensitive personal information including Social Security and credit card numbers while falsely claiming to encrypt and restrict access to that data; in reality the company stored and transmitted personal information in clear readable text, failed to enforce strong password policies, and left paper documents containing personal information in open accessible areas.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged seven counts against LifeLock: (1) false claims of complete identity theft protection; (2) false claims of address change protection; (3) false claims of ongoing credit monitoring; (4) false claims of guaranteed creditor phone calls before new accounts were opened; (5) false claims of reasonable data security practices; (6) false claims of encrypting customer data; and (7) false claims of need-to-know access controls on sensitive customer information.

## Outcome

A $100 million monetary judgment was entered against LifeLock, to be deposited into the Court's Registry within five business days for consumer redress. The original Permanent Injunction was kept in force with amended compliance provisions, including five years of compliance reporting and monitoring, 13-year recordkeeping obligations, and mandatory distribution of the order to employees and successors.

## Significance

The $100 million penalty was one of the largest FTC data-security and false-advertising settlements at the time, arising from LifeLock's violation of a prior consent order. The case established that companies marketing identity theft and data protection services are held to a high standard of accuracy and must actually implement the security practices they advertise to consumers.
