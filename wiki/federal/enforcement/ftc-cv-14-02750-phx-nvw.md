## Summary

The FTC charged Sitesearch Corporation (doing business as LeapLab) and related defendants with unfairly selling consumers' sensitive payday loan applications — including Social Security numbers and bank account numbers — to non-lenders, telemarketers, and fraudulent merchants who used the data to make unauthorized bank account debits. Approximately 95% of applications were sold to parties with no legitimate need for the data, and defendants continued selling to known fraudster Ideal Financial Solutions even after being directly confronted about its illegal activity. A consent order was entered requiring a $4,124,710 judgment (suspended), permanent prohibition on disclosing sensitive personal information, and destruction of previously collected consumer data.

## Background

From at least 2006 to late 2013, Sitesearch Corporation (dba LeapLab), LeapLab LLC, Leads Company LLC, and John Ayers operated as data brokers collecting sensitive consumer payday loan applications and selling them to non-lenders at roughly $0.50 each. After LeapLab's then-CEO confronted founder John Ayers in April 2012 about Ideal Financial Solutions using purchased data to make unauthorized bank account debits, defendants continued selling consumer information to Ideal Financial through October 2012. Using only data supplied by defendants, Ideal Financial made at least $4.12 million in unauthorized debits.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged one count of unfair trade practices under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act: defendants sold consumers' payday loan applications containing Social Security numbers and bank account numbers to non-lenders — including fraudulent online merchants — that had no legitimate need for the information and used it to debit consumer accounts without authorization.

## Outcome

A consent order was entered requiring a $4,124,710 monetary judgment (suspended based on accuracy of financial disclosures), permanent injunction against selling or disclosing consumers' sensitive personal information, prohibition on misrepresentations relating to financial products or services, destruction of all previously collected consumer information within 30 days, and 20 years of compliance reporting and recordkeeping obligations.

## Significance

The case established that data brokers can face FTC liability not only for their own deceptive practices but for knowingly supplying sensitive consumer financial data to third parties who use it to commit fraud — and that continuing to sell data after actual notice of a buyer's illegal activity is an unfair practice under Section 5.
