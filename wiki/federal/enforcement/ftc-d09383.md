## Summary

The FTC brought an action against Cambridge Analytica, LLC (Docket D09383) alleging the company misrepresented its data practices and participation in privacy programs in connection with harvesting personal data from millions of consumers. The matter resulted in a Commission order imposing a 20-year prohibition on misrepresentations and requiring deletion of data collected via the GSRApp, including any derived algorithms. The order also permanently bars Cambridge Analytica from disclosing or benefiting from covered information.

## Developments

- 2019-12-15 — Commission issued 20-year final order against Cambridge Analytica requiring deletion of GSRApp-harvested personal data and derived algorithms and prohibiting misrepresentations about data practices and privacy program participation. — [source](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/182-3107-cambridge-analytica-llc-matter)

## Background

Cambridge Analytica harvested personal data from millions of consumers through the GSRApp and misrepresented both how it handled that data and its participation in privacy frameworks including EU-U.S. Privacy Shield, Swiss-U.S. Privacy Shield, and APEC Cross-Border Privacy Rules.

## Allegations

The FTC alleged that Cambridge Analytica misrepresented the extent to which it protects the privacy and confidentiality of covered information — including how it collects, uses, shares, or sells such information — and falsely claimed membership in or adherence to government and self-regulatory privacy programs.

## Outcome

The Commission issued a final order (Docket D09383) effective 60 days after service and terminating November 25, 2039. The order prohibits misrepresentations about data practices and privacy program participation, requires deletion of all GSRApp-collected covered information and derived work product including algorithms, permanently bars any use or disclosure of covered information, and mandates full document and data access for Commission monitoring.

## Significance

The order is notable for requiring deletion not only of raw personal data but also of algorithms derived from that data, establishing a precedent that algorithmic work product built on unlawfully obtained data is itself subject to disgorgement. It also imposed a 20-year compliance horizon with ongoing monitoring access.
