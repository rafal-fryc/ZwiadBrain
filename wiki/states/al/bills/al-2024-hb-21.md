## Summary

Alabama's HB 21, the Alabama Genetic Data Privacy Act, requires genetic testing companies to prominently disclose their privacy policies and to obtain a consumer's express written consent before using a biological sample or genetic data, retaining it for future testing, or transferring/disclosing it to a third party for purposes beyond fulfilling the consumer's order; separate express written consent is required before disclosing genetic data to insurers or employers, and informed consent under 45 C.F.R. Part 46 is required for research uses. It requires companies to let consumers access their data, delete accounts, and request destruction of samples/data (within 30 days) or return of transferred data after revoking consent (within 60 days), and it binds contractors to the same confidentiality duties via contract. The law exempts HIPAA-covered entities/business associates, noncommercial research by higher-education institutions, and law-enforcement-obtained samples, and it is enforced by the Alabama Attorney General's Consumer Division through civil actions carrying penalties up to $3,000 per violation. The act took effect October 1, 2024.
