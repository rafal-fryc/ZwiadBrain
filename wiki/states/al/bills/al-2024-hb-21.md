## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB 21, the Alabama Genetic Data Privacy Act, was introduced by Rep. Chip Brown and referred to the House Judiciary Committee. The bill cleared the House of Origin on 2024-02-20 by a 102-0 vote (following an earlier 100-0 third-reading tally on an instrument change), reflecting broad bipartisan support for regulating direct-to-consumer genetic-testing companies. In the Senate, the Banking and Insurance Committee took up the engrossed bill and offered an amendment, adopted by Roll Call 1169, before the full Senate passed the amended bill 33-0 on 2024-05-08. The bill was enrolled the same day and delivered to Governor Kay Ivey on 2024-05-09, who signed it into law on 2024-05-16. As enacted, HB 21 requires direct-to-consumer genetic-testing companies to obtain a consumer's express, informed consent before collecting, using, retaining, or disclosing that consumer's genetic data or biological sample, and separately before transferring or disclosing genetic data to third parties such as employers, insurers, or law enforcement. It grants consumers rights to access their genetic data, request its deletion, and revoke consent, and imposes a duty on covered companies to implement reasonable security safeguards protecting the confidentiality and integrity of genetic data. Enforcement rests with the Consumer Protection Division of the Alabama Attorney General's Office. The bill's passage without a single recorded 'no' vote in either chamber underscores its treatment as a consensus consumer-protection measure rather than a contested partisan issue.
