## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

The Alabama Personal Data Protection Act (APDPA), House Bill 351, was introduced by Rep. Mike Shaw and moved through the House first. On February 24, 2026, the House passed HB 351 on Third Reading as amended by a unanimous 103-0 vote, sending it to the Senate. In the upper chamber, Sen. Chris Elliott offered amendments that were adopted before the Senate passed the bill on Third Reading, again unanimously, by a vote of 34-0 on April 7, 2026. Because the Senate had amended the bill, it returned to the House the same day, where Rep. Shaw moved to concur in the Senate amendments and the House agreed, clearing the way for enrollment. The bill was enrolled and delivered to Governor Kay Ivey on April 8, 2026, and she signed it into law on April 17, 2026, making Alabama the 21st U.S. state to enact a comprehensive consumer privacy law. As described in the engrossed bill text and in contemporaneous legal analyses, the APDPA establishes consumer rights to access, correct, delete, and obtain a portable copy of personal data, together with the right to opt out of targeted advertising, the sale of personal data, and profiling in furtherance of decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. It imposes data minimization, purpose-limitation, and reasonable-security duties on controllers and processors, and it is notable for a relatively low applicability threshold of 25,000 Alabama consumers (or deriving more than 25% of gross revenue from data sales). Enforcement is vested exclusively in the Alabama Attorney General, with no private right of action and a cure period before penalties may be imposed. The bill's bipartisan, unanimous votes in both chambers reflected broad legislative consensus on establishing Alabama's first comprehensive data-privacy framework.
