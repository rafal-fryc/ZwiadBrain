## Summary

Alabama SB 63 (2026), enacted, restricts health benefit plan providers' use of artificial intelligence in making prior-authorization and medical-necessity determinations. Insurers using AI for these determinations must base decisions on the enrollee's medical history and individual clinical circumstances rather than group datasets, certify annually that their AI is applied fairly and non-discriminatorily, and ensure that any denial, delay, or modification of a request is ultimately made by a licensed physician or qualified health care professional; providers must also disclose their AI use in policies and periodically review AI outcomes for accuracy. The Alabama Department of Insurance may investigate violations and, for repeat violations amounting to a business pattern, impose fines up to $5,000 or suspend/revoke a provider's certificate of authority. The act takes effect October 1, 2026.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 63, sponsored solely by Sen. Arthur Orr and read first in the Senate on January 13, 2026, was referred to the Senate Healthcare Committee, which reported it out with a first substitute (NRSWP8T-1) on February 11, 2026, and placed it on the calendar the next day. On February 19, 2026, the Senate adopted Orr's motion to substitute (Roll Call 476, 27-0), then passed the bill as amended on third reading (Roll Call 477, 27-0) — the same day it had earlier passed the House of Origin vote 27-0 — and it was engrossed and sent to the House, where it was referred to the House Insurance Committee. That committee reported the bill out with its own substitute (TBV8D95-1) on March 17, 2026. On April 8, 2026, the House adopted the committee substitute (Roll Call 1307, 103-0), adopted a floor amendment offered by Rep. Shaw (Roll Call 1308, 104-0), and passed the bill as amended on third reading (Roll Call 1309, 102-0); the same day, the Senate — on a motion by Sen. Gudger — concurred in the House amendment (Roll Call 1261, 33-0). The bill was ordered enrolled on April 8, 2026, and delivered to the Governor on April 9, 2026, becoming law on April 17, 2026, with an effective date of October 1, 2026. As enrolled, the Act imposes limits on health benefit plan providers' use of artificial intelligence in determinations of coverage: any AI-assisted medical-necessity determination on a prior-authorization request must be based on the enrollee's own medical history, clinical circumstances, and medical record; providers must certify annually to the Alabama Department of Insurance that such AI does not rely on group datasets, is fairly and equitably applied, and does not discriminate; any denial, delay, or modification decision must ultimately be made by a licensed physician or other qualified health care professional; providers must prominently disclose their use of AI in utilization review and periodically review AI outcomes for accuracy; and the Department of Insurance may investigate violations, impose corrective plans, and — for a pattern of repeat violations — levy administrative fines of up to $5,000 and/or suspend or revoke a provider's certificate of authority.
