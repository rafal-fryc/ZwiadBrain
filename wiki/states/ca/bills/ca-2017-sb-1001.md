## Summary

California SB 1001 (2018), the B.O.T. Act, adds Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 17940–17943. It makes it unlawful to use a bot to communicate or interact with a person in California online with intent to mislead them about its artificial identity, for the purpose of knowingly deceiving them about the content of the communication in order to incentivize a purchase or sale of goods or services in a commercial transaction, or to influence a vote in an election. A person is not liable if the bot discloses that it is a bot, clearly and conspicuously enough that a reasonable person would be informed. The duty applies to public-facing internet services with a specified level of monthly United States visitors, and the act expressly does not impose a duty on online platforms including web hosting and internet service providers. This is the earliest of the tracked state bot-disclosure laws and predates the 2019 floor of the state backfill, so no earlier sweep had seen it.
