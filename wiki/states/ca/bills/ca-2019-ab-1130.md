    2019, with sponsorship from Attorney General Xavier Becerra, to close a gap in
    California’s Data Breach Notification Law by adding government-issued identification
    numbers and unique biometric data to the definition of personal information —
    a response prompted in part by the 2018 Marriott-Starwood breach that exposed
    passport numbers for approximately 327 million hotel guests. The bill was heard
    by the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection on March 26, 2019
    (do-pass 7–1), where a coalition led by the California Chamber of Commerce opposed
    the corresponding Section 1798.81.5 security-measures amendment as creating class-action

    bill passed the full Assembly on May 29, 2019 (46–19). In the Senate, the Judiciary
    Committee approved the measure on July 2, 2019 (7–2), Senate Appropriations cleared
    it on August 30, 2019 (5–2), and the full Senate passed it on September 5, 2019
    (26–12). Governor Gavin Newsom signed AB 1130 on October 11, 2019, chaptering
    it as Chapter 750, Statutes of 2019, with an effective date of January 1, 2020.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 1130 was introduced by Assemblymember Marc Levine on February 21, 2019, with sponsorship from Attorney General Xavier Becerra, to close a gap in California’s Data Breach Notification Law by adding government-issued identification numbers and unique biometric data to the definition of personal information — a response prompted in part by the 2018 Marriott-Starwood breach that exposed passport numbers for approximately 327 million hotel guests. The bill was heard by the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection on March 26, 2019 (do-pass 7–1), where a coalition led by the California Chamber of Commerce opposed the corresponding Section 1798.81.5 security-measures amendment as creating class-action litigation risk and challenged the breadth of the biometric data definition; after amendments were adopted in Assembly Appropriations (12–5 on May 16, 2019), the bill passed the full Assembly on May 29, 2019 (46–19). In the Senate, the Judiciary Committee approved the measure on July 2, 2019 (7–2), Senate Appropriations cleared it on August 30, 2019 (5–2), and the full Senate passed it on September 5, 2019 (26–12). Governor Gavin Newsom signed AB 1130 on October 11, 2019, chaptering it as Chapter 750, Statutes of 2019, with an effective date of January 1, 2020.
