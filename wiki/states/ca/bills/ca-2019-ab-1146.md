## Summary

AB 1146 amends the CCPA to add an exemption for vehicle information, limiting consumers' deletion rights and other CCPA rights with respect to certain vehicle-related data shared between car dealers and manufacturers. It was enacted as Chapter 751, Statutes of 2019.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 1146, introduced by Assemblymember Marc Berman on February 21, 2019, and sponsored by the California New Car Dealers Association, sought to address an unintended conflict between the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 and the federal warranty and safety-recall framework governing new motor vehicles. The bill was referred to the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection, which heard it on April 23, 2019, after the author accepted amendments narrowing the exemption to information "retained or shared" strictly in connection with warranty work or a federally mandated recall, while preserving consumers' rights to access, to know what information had been collected, and to a private right of action in the event of a data breach. The committee approved the bill 10–0, and the Assembly Appropriations Committee followed 18–0 on May 8, 2019; the full Assembly passed it 77–0 on May 23, 2019. In the Senate, the bill was referred to the Judiciary Committee, where it received a clarifying amendment in June 2019 before passing the full Senate 40–0 on September 11, 2019. The Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments 79–0 on September 12, 2019, and Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on October 11, 2019, enacting it as Chapter 751, Statutes of 2019.
