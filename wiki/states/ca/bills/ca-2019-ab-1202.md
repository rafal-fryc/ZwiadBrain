## Summary

An act to add Title 1.81.48 (commencing with Section 1798.99.80) to Part 4 of Division 3 of the Civil Code, relating to privacy.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 1202 was introduced by Assemblymember Edwin Chau (D-Arcadia) on February 21, 2019, creating California's first data-broker registry by requiring any business that knowingly sells consumers' personal information without a direct relationship with those consumers to register annually with the Attorney General and pay a fee, with a public registry maintained on the Attorney General's website. The Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection voted 9–2 on March 26, 2019 to recommend passage, and the Assembly Committee on Appropriations cleared the bill 13–4 on May 16, 2019; the full Assembly then approved it 56–13 on third reading on May 28, 2019. In the Senate, the Judiciary Committee — chaired by Senator Hannah-Beth Jackson, who carried the bill on the Senate floor — voted 7–1 on July 2, 2019, and the Senate Appropriations Committee released the bill from suspense 5–2 on August 30, 2019. The Senate passed AB 1202 29–10 on September 13, 2019, and the Assembly concurred in Senate amendments 63–14 on September 14, 2019. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on October 11, 2019, enacting it as Chapter 753, Statutes of 2019 (Civil Code § 1798.99.80 et seq.), effective January 1, 2020.
