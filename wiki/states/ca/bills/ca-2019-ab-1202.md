## Summary

An act to add Title 1.81.48 (commencing with Section 1798.99.80) to Part 4 of Division 3 of the Civil Code, relating to privacy.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 1202 was introduced by Assemblymember Ed Chau on February 21, 2019, as a companion to the California Consumer Privacy Act (AB 375, Stats. 2018), which Chau had also authored the prior year. The bill was referred to the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection, which Chau chaired; the committee heard the bill on March 26, 2019, and passed it 9–2 with a recommendation to re-refer to Appropriations. The Assembly Appropriations Committee approved the bill as amended on May 16, 2019, by a vote of 13–4, and the full Assembly passed it on May 28, 2019, 56–13. In the Senate, the bill was referred to the Senate Judiciary Committee, which passed it as amended 7–1 on July 2, 2019, before the Senate Appropriations Committee cleared it 5–2 on August 30, 2019; the Senate then passed the bill 29–10 on September 13, 2019. The Assembly concurred in the Senate's amendments 63–14 on September 14, 2019, and the enrolled bill was presented to Governor Gavin Newsom, who signed it on October 11, 2019, making it Chapter 753, Statutes of 2019. The new law added Civil Code Title 1.81.48 (§§ 1798.99.80 et seq.), requiring data brokers — businesses that knowingly collect and sell to third parties the personal information of consumers with whom they have no direct relationship — to register annually with the Attorney General and pay a registration fee, with the Attorney General directed to post a publicly accessible registry; non-compliance subjects a broker to civil penalties of $100 per day, plus fees and AG enforcement costs deposited in the Consumer Privacy Fund.
