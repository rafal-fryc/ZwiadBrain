## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 1281 was introduced by Assemblymember Edwin Chau on February 21, 2019, originally as a facial recognition technology disclosure bill requiring businesses to post signs at entrances where FRT was in use. The Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection, chaired by Chau himself, passed the bill (8-3), and the full Assembly passed it on April 25, 2019 (61-13). The bill crossed to the Senate, was referred to the Senate Judiciary and Appropriations Committees, and passed Senate Judiciary in 2019 (7-2) with recommended amendments; it then stalled. The bill was revived on June 25, 2020, when it was gutted and amended into an entirely different measure — a one-year extension of the CCPA employee/personnel and business-to-business (B2B) personal information exemptions, originally enacted in AB 25 (Chau, 2019) with a January 1, 2021 sunset. Chau explained in the Senate Judiciary Committee that COVID-19 had disrupted the stakeholder process intended to produce a permanent framework and that a pending ballot initiative (Proposition 24/CPRA) created further uncertainty; the bill was therefore drafted to become operative only if Proposition 24 failed. The amended bill cleared the Senate Judiciary Committee 9-0 on August 13, 2020 (with a recommendation to the Consent Calendar); Senate Appropriations was bypassed under Senate Rule 28.8, and the bill passed the Senate on August 28, 2020 (39-0), and was concurred in by the Assembly on August 30, 2020 (75-0). Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on September 29, 2020, chaptering it as Chapter 268, Statutes of 2020; because Proposition 24 was approved by voters on November 3, 2020, AB 1281's extended sunset became inoperative and the CPRA's longer exemption period took effect instead.
