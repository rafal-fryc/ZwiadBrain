## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 1355 was introduced on February 22, 2019 by Assembly Member Edwin Chau, Chair of the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection, as an omnibus technical-cleanup measure to correct drafting errors in the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (Civil Code § 1798.100 et seq.) before its January 1, 2020 operative date. The bill was referred to the Assembly Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee and, after amendments on April 12, 2019, was heard and unanimously approved on April 23, 2019 (11–0); it then moved to Assembly Appropriations, which placed it on the consent calendar on May 1, 2019 (18–0), and the full Assembly passed it 76–0 on May 9, 2019. In the Senate, the bill was heard by the Senate Judiciary Committee on July 9, 2019 — one of six CCPA amendment bills that cleared the committee that day — and was approved 8–0 with referral to Senate Appropriations, which also placed it on the consent calendar. Following September 2019 floor amendments to add and refine the business-to-business (B2B) one-year exemption, the Senate passed the amended bill 40–0 on September 12, 2019, and the Assembly concurred in Senate amendments 79–0 on September 13, 2019. Governor Gavin Newsom signed AB 1355 on October 11, 2019, chaptering it as Chapter 757, Statutes of 2019; the key substantive changes included excluding de-identified and aggregate consumer information from the definition of "personal information," inserting "reasonably" into the "capable of being associated with" standard, clarifying the Fair Credit Reporting Act exemption, adjusting the non-discrimination provision to allow price differences reasonably related to the value of a consumer's data, and providing a one-year B2B exemption (expiring January 1, 2021) for personal information collected in the context of due diligence or commercial transactions between businesses.
