## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Assemblymember Marc Berman (D-Palo Alto) introduced AB 1564 on February 22, 2019, to address a practical gap in the newly enacted California Consumer Privacy Act: the CCPA required every business to offer consumers at least two designated request-submission methods including a toll-free telephone number, an obligation that online-only companies argued was anachronistic and costly. The Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection heard the bill on April 23, 2019, and approved it 10-0 as amended, directing it to the Appropriations Committee, which cleared it 18-0 on May 8. The full Assembly passed AB 1564 on May 13, 2019 by a vote of 73-0 and sent it to the Senate. In the Senate, the bill was referred to the Judiciary Committee, where it was heard on July 9, 2019, as part of a marathon hearing on multiple CCPA amendment bills; the committee approved it 8-0 as amended, narrowing the online-only exemption to businesses with a direct consumer relationship and restoring the toll-free-number requirement for businesses with physical storefronts. After clearing the Senate Appropriations Committee in August, the Senate passed the bill 40-0 on September 11, 2019, and the Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments 78-0 on September 12. Governor Gavin Newsom signed AB 1564 on October 11, 2019, enacting it as Chapter 759, Statutes of 2019, amending Civil Code § 1798.130 to allow exclusively online businesses with a direct consumer relationship to satisfy CCPA request-method requirements with an email address alone.
