## Summary

AB 25 amends the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (CCPA), modifying Civil Code sections governing consumer request procedures and exemptions. It was enacted as Chapter 763, Statutes of 2019.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Assemblymember Ed Chau (D-Monterey Park) introduced AB 25 on December 3, 2018, as a targeted cleanup amendment to the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (CCPA), authored by the very chair of the committee that would hear it. The Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection — chaired by Chau himself — approved the bill 10–0 on April 23, 2019; the Assembly Appropriations Committee followed 18–0 on May 1, 2019; and the full Assembly passed it 77–0 on May 29, 2019, sending it to the Senate with coauthors Senators Bill Dodd and Bob Hertzberg. The Senate Judiciary Committee approved an amended version 8–0 on July 9, 2019, after which the bill bypassed a formal Senate Appropriations hearing under Senate Rule 28.8 before the full Senate passed it 40–0 on September 12, 2019, and the Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments 78–0 on September 13, 2019. Governor Gavin Newsom signed AB 25 on October 11, 2019, enacting it as Chapter 763, Statutes of 2019, amending Civil Code §§ 1798.130 and 1798.145. The enacted law created two temporary exemptions — both sunset January 1, 2021 — shielding personal information collected from job applicants, employees, owners, directors, officers, medical-staff members, and contractors in the employment context from most CCPA consumer rights, and separately exempting personal information exchanged in business-to-business due-diligence or commercial transactions; in both cases the CCPA private right of action for data breaches and the right to receive notice of data-collection categories were expressly preserved.
