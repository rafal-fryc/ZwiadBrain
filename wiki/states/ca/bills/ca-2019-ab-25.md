## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 25 was introduced on December 3, 2018, by Assembly Member Edwin Chau — Chair of the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection — with co-authors Senators Bill Dodd and Bob Hertzberg, to address two operational gaps in the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (CCPA): the act's unintended reach over employee and contractor personal information, and data exchanged in business-to-business contexts. After a series of chair amendments in March and April 2019, the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection passed the bill unanimously (10–0) on April 23, 2019; the Assembly Appropriations Committee placed it on consent (18–0, May 1, 2019) before the full Assembly passed it 77–0 on May 29, 2019. In the Senate, the Judiciary Committee — chaired by Senator Hannah-Beth Jackson — substantially rewrote the bill during a marathon July 9, 2019 hearing: rather than fully removing employees from the statutory definition of 'consumer,' the committee's version preserved a disclosure-of-collection-categories requirement for employees while creating a broad, time-limited exemption for employment-context personal information set to expire January 1, 2021. The Senate passed the amended bill 40–0 on September 12, 2019, and the Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments 78–0 the following day. Governor Gavin Newsom signed AB 25 on October 11, 2019 as Chapter 763, Statutes of 2019, enacting amendments to Civil Code §§ 1798.130 and 1798.145.
