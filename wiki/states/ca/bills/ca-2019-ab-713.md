## Summary

AB 713 amends the CCPA by modifying consumer request procedures and adding new Civil Code sections, including provisions related to medical and health research data. Enacted as an urgency statute (Chapter 172, Statutes of 2020), it took effect immediately upon chaptering.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 713 was introduced on February 19, 2019, by Assembly Speaker pro Tempore Kevin Mullin, with coauthors Tasha Boerner Horvath, Tom Daly, and Chad Mayes, to address an emerging conflict between the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (CCPA) and the federal HIPAA framework for de-identified health information. The bill was referred to the Assembly Committee on Health, which passed it unanimously (15-0) on March 27, 2019, followed by a unanimous Assembly Appropriations Committee vote (18-0) on May 16, 2019; the full Assembly passed the bill 74-0 on May 28, 2019. The bill then crossed to the Senate, where it was held in the Senate Committee on Health through the interim recess; the Senate Health Committee passed it unanimously (8-0) on January 8, 2020, and the bill received further Senate amendments in June and July 2020 to broaden the research and business-associate exemptions. The Senate Committee on Appropriations cleared the amended bill (7-0) on August 20, 2020, and the full Senate passed it 37-0 on August 31, 2020; the Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments 74-0 on September 1, 2020. Governor Gavin Newsom signed AB 713 on September 25, 2020, chaptering it as Chapter 172, Statutes of 2020, with an urgency clause making it effective immediately — the Legislature having declared that the CCPA's ambiguous de-identification standard was negatively impacting health-related research and that immediate relief was necessary.
