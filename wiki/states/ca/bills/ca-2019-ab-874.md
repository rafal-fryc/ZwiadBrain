## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 874, authored by Assemblymember Jacqui Irwin, was introduced on February 20, 2019, as a technical correction and clarification bill targeting two provisions of the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018 (Civil Code §1798.140). The bill proposed to: (1) replace 'capable of being associated with' with 'reasonably capable of being associated with' in the definition of 'personal information,' establishing an objective-reasonableness standard; (2) streamline the 'publicly available' information exception so that any information lawfully available from federal, state, or local government records would be excluded from the definition of personal information, removing the prior 'compatible purpose' restriction; and (3) correct a drafting error that had mistakenly placed the deidentified/aggregate exclusion within the 'publicly available' definition rather than the 'personal information' definition. The Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection passed AB 874 unanimously (10-0) on April 23, 2019, and the Assembly Appropriations Committee concurred (18-0) on May 1, 2019, placing the bill on the consent calendar; the full Assembly passed it 76-0 on May 9, 2019. In the Senate, the Committee on Judiciary held a marathon hearing on July 9, 2019, on multiple CCPA amendment bills and passed AB 874 (8-0), after which Senate Appropriations cleared it without a separate floor hearing. The Senate passed AB 874 40-0 on September 11, 2019, and the Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments 79-0 the following day. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on October 11, 2019, chaptering it as Chapter 748, Statutes of 2019.
