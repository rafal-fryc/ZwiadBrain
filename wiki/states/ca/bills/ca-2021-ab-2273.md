## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 2273, the California Age-Appropriate Design Code Act, was introduced on February 16, 2022 by joint authors Assemblymembers Buffy Wicks (D) and Jordan Cunningham (R), with coauthor Cottie Petrie-Norris and Senate coauthors Benjamin Allen, Josh Newman, and Henry Stern; modeled on the United Kingdom's Age Appropriate Design Code and co-sponsored by the 5Rights Foundation and Common Sense, it required covered businesses providing online services likely to be accessed by children under 18 to configure default privacy settings to the highest available level, complete Data Protection Impact Assessments, and refrain from dark patterns and harmful data practices, with enforcement by the Attorney General. The bill advanced unanimously through the Assembly: the Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee approved it 10-0 on April 19, 2022; Appropriations cleared it 16-0 on May 19, 2022; and the full Assembly passed it 72-0 on May 26, 2022. In the Senate, the Judiciary Committee approved an amended version 9-1 on June 28, 2022—after the author agreed to amendments clarifying the 'likely to be accessed by a child' scope through enumerated factors and adding an explicit Attorney General enforcement mechanism in response to industry opposition—and the Appropriations Committee cleared it 5-0 on August 11, 2022. The full Senate passed the bill 33-0 on August 30, 2022, and the Assembly concurred in Senate amendments that same day 75-0. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the measure on September 15, 2022, chaptering it as Chapter 320, Statutes of 2022, with an operative date of July 1, 2024.
