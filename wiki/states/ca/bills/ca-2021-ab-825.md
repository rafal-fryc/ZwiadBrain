## Summary

California AB 825 (Chapter 527, Statutes of 2021) amends California's data-breach notification law by expanding the definition of protected personal information to include genetic data, requiring businesses to notify affected individuals when such data is compromised.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 825, authored by Assemblymember Marc Levine (D-Marin), was introduced on February 16, 2021, as a reintroduction of his pandemic-shelved AB 2301 (2020), which itself built on his AB 1130 (Ch. 750, Stats. 2019). The bill amends Civil Code sections 1798.29, 1798.81.5, and 1798.82 to add “genetic data” — broadly defined to encompass DNA, RNA, SNPs, uninterpreted biological-sample data, and any information extrapolated therefrom — to California’s data-breach notification and reasonable-security requirements. The Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection passed it unanimously 11–0 on April 8, 2021, the full Assembly cleared it 75–0 on May 27, 2021, and the Senate Judiciary Committee approved it 10–1 on June 22, 2021, with Sen. Brian Jones the lone dissent; the Senate Appropriations Committee followed 5–2 on August 26, 2021. The Senate passed the bill 30–0 on September 8, 2021, and Governor Gavin Newsom signed it on October 5, 2021, enacting it as Chapter 527, Statutes of 2021, effective January 1, 2022. Consumer genetic-testing companies (23andMe, Ancestry) and consumer advocates supported the measure, while the California Chamber of Commerce and TechNet opposed it as overbroad, arguing the new “genetic data” definition would create overlap and compliance confusion with existing health and biometric data categories already covered by the statutes.

### Statements

- **Consumer Reports and Electronic Frontier Foundation** (other), 2021-04-08 — "We are strong proponents of public policy that bolsters consumers’ privacy and their individual right to choose who accesses their data and for what purposes. It is within this framework that we support this bill, which would expand the definition of personal information in California’s data breach notification and data security statutes to include genetic data. This bill would require companies to adopt reasonable security measures to protect this data from unauthorized access, and provides companies with strong incentives to comply." — [source](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/sites/privacycp.assembly.ca.gov/files/AB%20825.pdf) ✓
