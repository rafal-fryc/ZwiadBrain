## Summary

AB 1836 amends California Civil Code §3344.1 to prohibit the commercial use of AI-generated digital replicas of deceased personalities in audiovisual works or sound recordings without consent from the deceased's estate. Liability attaches to producers and distributors who use such replicas without authorization. The law extends existing personality rights protections to cover AI-generated likenesses of the dead.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 1836 was introduced on January 16, 2024, by Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan (D-Orinda), who also chaired the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection, and was sponsored by SAG-AFTRA. The bill amended Civil Code Section 3344.1 to carve out from the existing "expressive works" exemption a new cause of action — with damages of the greater of $10,000 or actual damages — against anyone who produces, distributes, or makes available a digital replica of a deceased personality's voice or likeness in an expressive audiovisual work or sound recording without the prior consent of the personality's estate. The bill cleared the Assembly unanimously: the Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee passed it 8-0 on April 16, 2024, with author amendments narrowing the definition of "digital replica" to a layperson-indistinguishable simulation and adding First Amendment exemptions for news, commentary, parody, and documentary uses; Judiciary passed it 9-0 on April 30, 2024; Appropriations cleared it 11-0; and the full Assembly passed it 59-0 on May 20, 2024. In the Senate, the Judiciary Committee (Senator Thomas Umberg, Chair) heard the bill on July 2, 2024, where the author agreed to a further amendment deleting a prefatory "to the extent the use is protected by the First Amendment" clause from the exemptions at the Motion Picture Association's request; the bill then passed Senate Judiciary 11-0, Senate Appropriations 5-1, and the full Senate 33-4 on August 31, 2024. The Assembly concurred in Senate amendments 57-8 on August 31, 2024, and Governor Newsom signed the bill on September 17, 2024, chaptering it as Chapter 258, Statutes of 2024, with an effective date of January 1, 2025.
