## Summary

An act to add Chapter 22.2.3 (commencing with Section 22585.5) to Division 8 of the Business and Professions Code, relating to personal information.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 1971 was introduced by Assemblymember Dawn Addis (D-San Luis Obispo) on January 30, 2024, to close a gap in the Student Online Personal Information Protection Act (SOPIPA) that had allowed non-profit standardized testing organizations — primarily the College Board and ACT — to collect, sell, and share students' personal data outside the law's reach. The bill was double-referred in the Assembly; the Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection approved it unanimously (10-0) on March 12, 2024, and the Committee on Education approved it (7-0) on April 3, 2024, on the consent calendar. The full Assembly passed the bill 75-0 on April 15, 2024. In the Senate, the bill cleared the Standing Committee on Education on June 19, 2024 (6-0), drawing testimony from the California Teachers Association, ACLU California Action, and student advocates in support, and from the College Board and ACT in opposition; the Senate Judiciary Committee heard the bill on July 2, 2024 (9-1). Following Senate amendments, the Senate passed the bill 39-0 on August 28, 2024, and the Assembly concurred 75-0 on August 29, 2024. Governor Gavin Newsom chaptered the measure on September 24, 2024, as Chapter 508, Statutes of 2024, adding Business and Professions Code § 22585.5 and bringing the administration of standardized tests within SOPIPA's student-data protections.
