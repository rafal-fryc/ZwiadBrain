## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Assembly Bill 2013, authored by Assemblymember Jacqui Irwin (D-Thousand Oaks) and introduced on January 31, 2024, requires developers of generative artificial intelligence systems or services made available to Californians on or after January 1, 2022 to post public documentation about their training data — including a high-level summary of datasets used, data types (including whether personal or copyrighted information was used), and synthetic data practices — by January 1, 2026. The bill passed the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection on April 30, 2024 by an 8–1 vote after Irwin accepted amendments, and cleared the full Assembly on May 20, 2024 by a vote of 56–8. The Senate amended the bill in June 2024 to narrow its scope to generative AI specifically and add exemptions for systems developed exclusively for use by affiliates or hospital medical staff, after which the Senate Judiciary Committee approved the measure 10–1 on June 25, 2024. The full Senate passed AB 2013 without opposition on August 26, 2024 (39–0), and the Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments on August 27, 2024 by a unanimous 75–0 vote. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on September 28, 2024, enacting it as Chapter 817, Statutes of 2024 — making California the first legislature in the United States to require public disclosure of basic AI training data information.
