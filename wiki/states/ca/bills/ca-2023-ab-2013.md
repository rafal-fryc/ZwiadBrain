## Summary

California AB 2013 (Chapter 817, Statutes of 2024) adds Civil Code Title 15.2 requiring developers of generative AI systems to publicly disclose documentation about the data used to train those systems. Covered developers must post training-data transparency information so that the public can understand the sources and nature of data underlying their generative AI products.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 2013, authored by Assemblymember Jacqui Irwin (D), was introduced on January 31, 2024 and referred to the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection, chaired by Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan. The committee heard the bill on April 30, 2024 and adopted author's amendments replacing the introduced bill's granular per-dataset disclosure requirements with a 'high-level summary' formulation — a change designed to address trade-secret objections raised by a California Chamber of Commerce-led industry coalition — then reported it favorably 8–1. The full Assembly passed AB 2013 on May 20, 2024 by a vote of 56–8 and transmitted it to the Senate, where it was referred to the Senate Judiciary Committee chaired by Senator Thomas Umberg. The Judiciary Committee heard the bill on June 25, 2024 and voted 10–1 to report it favorably; after a Senate floor amendment on August 19, 2024, the full Senate passed the bill unanimously on August 26, 2024 by 39–0. The Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments the following day, August 27, 2024, on a unanimous vote of 75–0. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on September 28, 2024, chaptering it as Chapter 817, Statutes of 2024, which adds Title 15.2 (commencing with Section 3110) to the Civil Code and requires developers of generative AI systems or services made available to Californians to post high-level training data documentation on their websites before January 1, 2026 and before each subsequent public release or substantial modification.
