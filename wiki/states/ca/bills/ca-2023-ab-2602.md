## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 2602 (Kalra) was introduced on February 14, 2024, to address a practice that grew acute after the 2023 SAG-AFTRA strike: performers unknowingly signing broad contracts authorizing studios to use AI-generated digital replicas of their voice and likeness in perpetuity, without further compensation or specific consent. The bill cleared its first committee, Assembly Labor and Employment, 5-0 on April 3, 2024, then passed the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection 9-0 on April 23 — the committee adopting author-agreed amendments aligning the bill with the SAG-AFTRA collective bargaining agreement and the companion bill AB 1836. The Assembly Appropriations Committee passed it 11-1 on May 16, and the full Assembly approved it 62-0 on May 22, 2024. In the Senate, the bill cleared the Committee on Labor, Public Employment and Retirement 5-0 on June 19, then the Senate Judiciary Committee — where no opposition was filed — on July 2; the Senate floor passed it 37-1 on August 27 and the Assembly concurred in Senate amendments 73-0 on August 28, 2024. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on September 17, 2024, at SAG-AFTRA headquarters alongside AB 1836, enacting Labor Code Section 927 as Chapter 259, Statutes of 2024, effective January 1, 2025.

### Statements

- **Ash Kalra** (sponsor), 2024-04-23 — "Last year, SAG-AFTRA took part in a historic strike, bargaining with major studios on critical topics including the impact of artificial intelligence on the future of workers in the entertainment industry. Artificial intelligence has shown to be capable of reproducing or creating content based on a performer's work, without their permission or compensation. Some contracts in the entertainment industry have included clauses that grant full use of a performer's voice and likeness forever. While the industry explores new opportunities using artificial intelligence, performers must not be exploited or coerced into relinquishing their digital rights." — [source](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2024-04/ab-2602-kalra-apcp-analysis.pdf) ✓
- **Ash Kalra** (sponsor), 2024-04-23 — "AB 2602 strikes a balance that allows the industry to adapt to technological advancements while also protecting performer's rights to their digital self. This bill will require a performer's informed consent and proper representation in executing a contract for any transfer of rights of that individual's likeness or voice." — [source](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2024-04/ab-2602-kalra-apcp-analysis.pdf) ✓
- **California Labor Federation** (other), 2024-04-23 — "Current law prohibits the use of an individual's name or likeness for commercial purposes without the individual's consent. However, in contracts with major studios, artists have unintentionally signed agreements authorizing the on-going use of their voice and likeness through digital replication without additional compensation or proper consent. These contract clauses, signed well before the capabilities of AI technology were ever imagined, would have been disguised as standard copyright or advertising language, but now serve as the studio's right to digitally own a performer's image. The lack of explicit legal protections means that artists will continue to unknowingly have their own voices and likenesses used without proper compensation or control over the most personal of possessions—their image." — [source](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2024-04/ab-2602-kalra-apcp-analysis.pdf) ✓
- **California Labor Federation** (other), 2024-04-23 — "AB 2602 addresses these challenges by requiring performers to have union or legal representation before signing contracts that transfer the likeness or voice of a performer to another person or employer. This bill will address the use of AI in the entertainment industry, but the concept can be applied to other industries where technology exploits human skills and products to the benefit of employers, not workers." — [source](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2024-04/ab-2602-kalra-apcp-analysis.pdf) ✓
- **California Labor Federation** (other), 2024-04-23 — "It is critical that workers and their unions have a voice in the development and use of AI and other technologies in the workplace. Otherwise, technology will rapidly worsen the exploitation of workers and the elimination of jobs. California performers are some of the best in the world and should have the protections they deserve in the digital age. AB 2602 ensures this right is enshrined in state law." — [source](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2024-04/ab-2602-kalra-apcp-analysis.pdf) ✓
- **Oakland Privacy** (other), 2024-04-23 — "It makes sense to us to protect people's literal physical attributes for a while longer until we all have a better understanding of what we are selling and for what purposes. While creative work like songs are deeply personal and not easily replaced if stolen from their creator, our physical likenesses are even more intimate and less replaceable, so it makes sense to be cautious and assist people who had little to no understanding of what they signed on to. Assembly Bill 2602 provides the opportunity for the re-negotiation of contracts with the appropriate guidance to protect each party's rights, which may not have been present when the original contract was signed." — [source](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/system/files/2024-04/ab-2602-kalra-apcp-analysis.pdf) ✓
- **SAG-AFTRA** (other), 2024-07-02 — "Protection of a performer's digital voice and likeness is a critical issue for SAG-AFTRA. It is crucial that performers—and all individuals—control whether and how their digital self is exploited. If they enter into an agreement, it must be with informed consent. This is critical to ensure they can continue to make a living." — [source](https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2024-06/ab-2602-kalra-sjud-analysis.pdf) ✓
- **Ash Kalra** (sponsor), 2024-09-17 — "I am grateful that Governor Newsom has signed AB 2602, a bill that will safeguard a performer's right to their digital self and protect artists' livelihoods. While this bill was informed by negotiations during the historic strike by SAG-AFTRA, AB 2602 shows how California can strike the right balance between AI innovation and protecting workers in the digital age." — [source](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2024/09/17/governor-newsom-signs-bills-to-protect-digital-likeness-of-performers/) ✓
