## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Assemblymember Marc Berman introduced AB 2655 on February 14, 2024, as the 'Defending Democracy from Deepfake Deception Act of 2024,' sponsored by the California Initiative for Technology & Democracy (CITED) and building on Berman's prior AB 730 (Ch. 493, Stats. 2019), which had targeted distributors of election deepfakes but left platform liability unaddressed. AB 2655 shifted the compliance burden to large online platforms (those with at least one million California users), requiring them to block materially deceptive AI-generated content during the 120-day pre-election window and to label a broader category of such content throughout the election year. The bill passed the Assembly Elections Committee 6-1 on April 10, 2024, and the Assembly Judiciary Committee 9-0 on April 23, 2024, with both committees receiving author amendments addressing constitutional concerns; Assembly Appropriations approved it 11-1 on May 16, 2024, and the full Assembly passed the bill 56-1 on May 22, 2024. In the Senate, a joint informational hearing on AI and elections on June 4, 2024, provided background before the Senate Elections and Constitutional Amendments Committee cleared the bill 6-1. The Senate Judiciary Committee, chaired by Sen. Thomas Umberg, approved a further-amended version 9-2 on July 2, 2024, with amendments that removed an attorney's-fees remedy, eliminated the content-retention mandate, and tightened platform liability to actual knowledge or reckless disregard—modifications aimed at reducing Section 230 and First Amendment exposure. After Senate Appropriations cleared the bill in August 2024, the full Senate passed AB 2655 31-9 on August 27, 2024, and the Assembly concurred in Senate amendments 59-8 on August 28, 2024. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on September 17, 2024, chaptering it as Chapter 261, Statutes of 2024, one of five AI-deepfake bills he signed that day; the act faced immediate federal court challenge on First Amendment and Section 230 grounds.
