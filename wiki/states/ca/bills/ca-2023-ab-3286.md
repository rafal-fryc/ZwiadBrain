## Summary

California AB 3286 (Chapter 121, Statutes of 2024) amends more than a dozen Civil Code CCPA provisions to update the monetary thresholds used in the statute — such as those triggering business coverage and civil penalty ranges — and makes related adjustments to grant provisions administered under the law. The changes reflect inflation-based recalibration of the dollar figures embedded throughout the CCPA.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 3286 was introduced on March 18, 2024, by the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection as a technical omnibus measure to update the California Consumer Privacy Act of 2018. The bill transfers biennial CPI-based monetary-threshold adjustment authority from the Attorney General to the California Privacy Protection Agency, eliminates an attendant rulemaking requirement, expands authorized uses of the Consumer Privacy Fund to offset agency operating costs, and delays administration of the fund's grant program until available grant funds exceed $300,000 after required distributions. The Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection heard the bill on April 23, 2024, and voted 11-0 to pass it as amended with a consent-calendar recommendation; the Assembly Appropriations Committee followed on May 8, 2024, also placing it on the consent calendar by a 15-0 vote. The full Assembly passed AB 3286 on May 16, 2024, by a vote of 71-0. In the Senate, the Judiciary Committee voted 11-0 on June 11, 2024, to pass the bill on consent, and the full Senate passed it 40-0 on June 27, 2024. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on July 15, 2024, enacting it as Chapter 121, Statutes of 2024, with the threshold-adjustment provisions taking effect January 1, 2025.
