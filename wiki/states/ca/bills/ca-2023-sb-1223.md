## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 1223, authored by Senator Josh Becker (D-Menlo Park) and sponsored by the Neurorights Foundation, was introduced on February 15, 2024, to amend Civil Code §1798.140 of the California Consumer Privacy Act by adding 'neural data' — information generated by measuring activity of the central or peripheral nervous system that is not inferred from nonneural sources — to the statutory definition of 'sensitive personal information,' filling a gap that left brain signals collected by consumer neurotechnology devices entirely unprotected. The Senate Judiciary Committee approved the bill unanimously 11-0 on April 16, 2024, after receiving expert testimony from neuroscientists and the Neurorights Foundation on the rapid growth of the $21-billion consumer neurotechnology market and from industry opponents (TechNet and the California Chamber of Commerce) who raised concerns about the breadth of the covered-technology definitions; the full Senate passed SB 1223 38-0 on May 21, 2024. In the Assembly, the Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee approved the bill 10-0 on July 2, 2024 (analysis dated June 30), the Appropriations Committee cleared it on consent 14-0 on August 7, 2024, and the bill was amended a final time on August 27, 2024, under a suspended joint rule (63-0). The Assembly passed SB 1223 by 76-0 on August 31, 2024, and the Senate concurred in the Assembly amendments the same day (38-0), completing bicameral passage without a single dissenting vote in either chamber. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on September 28, 2024, enacting it as Chapter 887, Statutes of 2024, effective January 1, 2025 — California's first dedicated neural-data privacy statute and the second in the United States, after Colorado's HB 24-1058 (signed April 17, 2024).
