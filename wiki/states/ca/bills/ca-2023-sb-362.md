  narrative: Senator Josh Becker (D-San Mateo) introduced SB 362 on February 8, 2023,
    building on California's existing data-broker registry law to require the California
    Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) to create a single "accessible deletion mechanism"—later
    branded DROP—enabling consumers to direct all registered data brokers to delete
    their personal information in one step rather than contacting each broker individually.
    The Senate Judiciary Committee passed the bill 9-2 on April 25, 2023 after debate
    over whether CCPA already covered data-broker deletions, and Senate Appropriations
    advanced it through suspense hearings on May 8 and May 18, before the full Senate
    passed SB 362 on third reading 32-8 on May 31, 2023. In the Assembly, the Committee
    on Privacy and Consumer Protection unanimously voted 7-0 on June 27, 2023 after
    hearing testimony on risks posed by health and geolocation data sales; Assembly
    Appropriations cleared a 10-3 hurdle on September 1, 2023, and the Assembly passed
    the bill 52-14 on September 13, 2023. The Senate concurred in Assembly amendments
    31-9 on September 14, 2023—the final day of the regular session—with principal
    co-author Scott Wiener and co-authors Dave Min, Jesse Gabriel, and Gregg Hart
    among supporters. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill as Chapter 709, Statutes
    of 2023, on October 10, 2023, creating the first centralized data-broker deletion
    mechanism of its kind in any jurisdiction; the DROP platform became available
    to consumers on January 1, 2026, with data brokers required to begin processing
    deletion requests by August 1, 2026.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Senator Josh Becker (D-San Mateo) introduced SB 362 on February 8, 2023, building on California's existing data-broker registry law to require the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) to create a single "accessible deletion mechanism"—later branded DROP—enabling consumers to direct all registered data brokers to delete their personal information in one step rather than contacting each broker individually. The Senate Judiciary Committee passed the bill 9-2 on April 25, 2023 after debate over whether CCPA already covered data-broker deletions, and Senate Appropriations advanced it through suspense hearings on May 8 and May 18, before the full Senate passed SB 362 on third reading 32-8 on May 31, 2023. In the Assembly, the Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection unanimously voted 7-0 on June 27, 2023 after hearing testimony on risks posed by health and geolocation data sales; Assembly Appropriations cleared a 10-3 hurdle on September 1, 2023, and the Assembly passed the bill 52-14 on September 13, 2023. The Senate concurred in Assembly amendments 31-9 on September 14, 2023—the final day of the regular session—with principal co-author Scott Wiener and co-authors Dave Min, Jesse Gabriel, and Gregg Hart among supporters. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill as Chapter 709, Statutes of 2023, on October 10, 2023, creating the first centralized data-broker deletion mechanism of its kind in any jurisdiction; the DROP platform became available to consumers on January 1, 2026, with data brokers required to begin processing deletion requests by August 1, 2026.
