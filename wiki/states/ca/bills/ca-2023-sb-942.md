## Summary

The California AI Transparency Act requires providers of generative AI systems with more than one million monthly users to offer a free AI-detection tool and to embed machine-readable disclosures identifying AI-generated content. Covered providers must also give users the option of a visible, manifest disclosure on AI-generated outputs. Civil penalties of $5,000 per day per violation apply, with the law originally set to take effect January 1, 2026.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 942, the California AI Transparency Act, was introduced by Sen. Josh Becker (D-Menlo Park) on January 17, 2024 and referred to the Senate Judiciary Committee, which passed it 10–1 on April 16, 2024 and re-referred it to the Senate Governmental Organization Committee. The Governmental Organization Committee passed the bill 11–1 on April 23, and the Senate Appropriations Committee cleared it 5–0 on May 16 after amendments. The full Senate passed SB 942 on third reading 32–1 on May 21, 2024. In the Assembly, the bill cleared the Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee 8–0 on June 18, the Judiciary Committee 9–1 on July 2, and the Appropriations Committee 11–0 on August 15, with successive amendments refining the bill's scope and delaying the operative date to January 1, 2026. In a significant turn, industry opponents — including TechNet, the California Chamber of Commerce, NetChoice, and the Computer and Communications Industry Association — withdrew all opposition on August 23, 2024, following negotiations with the author over technical feasibility provisions. The Assembly passed the amended bill 73–0 on August 28, and the Senate concurred in the Assembly amendments 35–2 on August 29. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill on September 19, 2024, enacting it as Chapter 291, Statutes of 2024.

- **Josh Becker** (sponsor), 2024-04-16 — "I appreciate that. Again, I appreciate the comments from the opposition. - **Tom Kemp** (witness), 2024-04-16 — "SB 942 addresses this problem. It simply says a provider must label its content and provide a way for a consumer to ask, hey, did you create this?" — [source](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/hearings/257702) ✓
- **Dylan Hoffman** (witness), 2024-04-16 — "Thank you Mr. Chair and members. Dylan Hoffman, on behalf of Technet, were respectfully opposed to SB 942 at this time. We fully agree with the intent of the bill to create greater trust in user generated content online by fostering the adoption of content providence verifications and watermarks, and we look forward to providing much more substantive feedback and working with the author to offer amendments." — [source](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/hearings/257702) ✓
- **Dylan Hoffman** (witness), 2024-04-16 — "SB 942 enacts requirements for technology that is currently rapidly evolving, such as requiring a watermark or content provenance for text generated content, it's our understanding that's not currently possible, and so we think there are some reasonable amendments to sort of tailor this bill to what is and is not capable at this time." — [source](https://calmatters.digitaldemocracy.org/hearings/257702) ✓
