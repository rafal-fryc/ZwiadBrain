## Summary

AB 316 adds Section 1714.46 to the Civil Code barring any defendant who developed, modified, or used an artificial intelligence system from raising, as a defense in a civil lawsuit, the claim that the AI 'autonomously' caused the plaintiff's harm. Defendants may still assert other affirmative defenses (including on causation or foreseeability) and present evidence of other parties' comparative fault. It applies broadly to any civil action alleging AI-caused harm and was chaptered October 13, 2025.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 316, introduced by Assemblymember Maggy Krell on January 24, 2025, would add Civil Code Section 1714.46 to bar a defendant who developed, modified, or used an artificial intelligence system from asserting, as a defense in a civil action, that the AI system autonomously caused the plaintiff's harm, while preserving other defenses such as causation, foreseeability, and comparative fault. The bill cleared the Assembly Judiciary Committee 12-0 on March 25, 2025, and the Assembly Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee 11-2 on May 6, 2025, before passing the full Assembly 70-1 on May 19, 2025. In the Senate, the bill advanced through the Senate Judiciary Committee 12-0 on June 24, 2025, and the Senate Appropriations Committee, which placed it on the suspense file on July 7, 2025, before voting it out 7-0 on August 29, 2025; it was then amended on the Senate floor on September 2, 2025. The Senate passed the bill on its third reading 38-0 on September 8, 2025, and the Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments 78-0 on September 9, 2025. The bill was enrolled and presented to Governor Gavin Newsom on September 16, 2025, and he signed it on October 13, 2025, enacting it as Chapter 672, Statutes of 2025.
