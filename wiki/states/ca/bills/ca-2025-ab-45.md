## Summary

AB 45 rewrites California's family-planning-center privacy law to prohibit any person or entity from collecting, using, disclosing, selling, sharing, or retaining the personal information of someone physically located at or within a precise geolocation (1,850-foot radius) of a family planning center, with an exception only for collection/use necessary to provide the requested services. It separately bans geofencing an in-person health care provider to identify, track, message, or advertise to patients or staff, and bans using or trading personal information obtained through such geofencing, subject to exceptions for the provider's own security/safety use, warrants, subpoenas, and true emergencies (explicitly excluding abortion-related searches from the emergency exception). Violations of the geofencing ban carry a $25,000 civil penalty per violation recovered by the Attorney General and deposited into the state's Reproductive Justice and Freedom Fund, and the act also shields abortion-related research records from out-of-state or foreign subpoenas. Signed by the Governor and chaptered September 26, 2025.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 45, authored by Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan, was pre-filed on December 2, 2024 and referred on March 28, 2025 to the Assembly Committees on Privacy and Consumer Protection and on Judiciary, which reported it out 10-2 (April 23, 2025) and 9-2 (April 30, 2025) respectively after author's amendments. The Assembly Appropriations Committee, after placing the bill on its suspense file, passed it 11-2 on May 23, 2025, and the full Assembly approved it 63-11 on June 3, 2025, sending it to the Senate. In the Senate, the Judiciary Committee reported the bill 11-1 on July 16, 2025, and Appropriations — again via the suspense file — passed it 5-2 on August 29, 2025, after which the bill was amended twice more (September 5 and September 9) before the full Senate passed it 29-9 on September 12, 2025. The Assembly concurred in the Senate amendments by a 61-12 vote on September 13, 2025, and the bill was enrolled and presented to Governor Gavin Newsom on September 24, 2025. Governor Newsom signed AB 45 on September 26, 2025, and it was chaptered the same day as Chapter 134, Statutes of 2025, enacting new Civil Code and Health and Safety Code provisions that bar geofencing and location-based tracking around reproductive health facilities, impose a $25,000 Attorney General-enforced civil penalty per violation deposited into the California Reproductive Justice and Freedom Fund, and shield abortion-related research records from out-of-state and foreign subpoenas.
