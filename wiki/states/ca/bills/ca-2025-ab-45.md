## Summary

AB 45 rewrites California's family-planning-center privacy law to prohibit any person or entity from collecting, using, disclosing, selling, sharing, or retaining the personal information of someone physically located at or within a precise geolocation (1,850-foot radius) of a family planning center, with an exception only for collection/use necessary to provide the requested services. It separately bans geofencing an in-person health care provider to identify, track, message, or advertise to patients or staff, and bans using or trading personal information obtained through such geofencing, subject to exceptions for the provider's own security/safety use, warrants, subpoenas, and true emergencies (explicitly excluding abortion-related searches from the emergency exception). Violations of the geofencing ban carry a $25,000 civil penalty per violation recovered by the Attorney General and deposited into the state's Reproductive Justice and Freedom Fund, and the act also shields abortion-related research records from out-of-state or foreign subpoenas. Signed by the Governor and chaptered September 26, 2025.
