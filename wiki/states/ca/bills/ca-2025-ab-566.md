## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Assemblymember Josh Lowenthal introduced AB 566, the California Opt Me Out Act, on February 12, 2025, with principal coauthor Sen. Henry Stern and coauthor Sen. Aisha Wahab, to add Section 1798.136 to the Civil Code requiring web browsers to include an easily accessible feature for sending a CCPA opt-out preference signal. The bill cleared the Assembly Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee 9-0 on April 7, 2025, and the Assembly Appropriations Committee 11-0 on April 23, 2025, before the full Assembly passed it 53-1 on June 5, 2025, sending it to the Senate. In the Senate, the Judiciary Committee approved the bill 11-0 on July 2, 2025, and after further amendment on September 5, 2025, the full Senate passed it 31-7 on September 10, 2025. The Assembly then concurred in the Senate amendments 57-3 on September 11, 2025, and the bill was enrolled and presented to Governor Gavin Newsom on September 23, 2025. Governor Newsom signed AB 566 into law on October 8, 2025, as Chapter 465, Statutes of 2025, with the browser opt-out-signal mandate and California Privacy Protection Agency rulemaking authority taking effect January 1, 2027.
