## Summary

AB 621 expands California's civil cause of action against creators and distributors of nonconsensual sexually explicit deepfakes to also cover material depicting a minor and to reach anyone who knowingly facilitates or recklessly aids or abets such conduct, including a presumption of liability for services that keep a 'deepfake pornography service' operating once notified and given 30 days to stop. It raises statutory damages to $1,500–$50,000 (up to $250,000 for malicious violations) and authorizes the Attorney General and local prosecutors to sue for injunctive relief and civil penalties of $25,000–$50,000 per violation. Chaptered October 13, 2025.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

AB 621, introduced February 13, 2025 by Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan with bipartisan coauthors, amends Civil Code section 1708.86 to strengthen California's civil remedies against nonconsensual sexually explicit deepfakes, extending the cause of action to material depicting minors, reaching anyone who knowingly facilitates or recklessly aids or abets such conduct, and creating a liability presumption for services that continue operating a "deepfake pornography service" after 30 days' notice, while raising statutory damages (to $50,000, or $250,000 for malicious violations) and authorizing public-attorney injunctive relief and civil penalties. In the Assembly, the bill passed the Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection 15-0 on March 18, 2025, the Committee on Judiciary 12-0 on April 8, 2025, and the Committee on Appropriations 14-0 (with one member not voting) on May 23, 2025, before passing the Assembly floor on Third Reading 78-0 (one not voting) on June 2, 2025. In the Senate, the bill cleared the Committee on Judiciary 13-0 on July 15, 2025, was held on the Appropriations suspense file before passing that committee 7-0 on August 29, 2025, and passed the Senate floor on Third Reading 40-0 on September 9, 2025. The Assembly concurred in the Senate's amendments 79-0 (one not voting) on September 10, 2025, and the bill was enrolled and presented to Governor Gavin Newsom on September 22, 2025. Governor Newsom signed AB 621 on October 13, 2025, enacting it as Chapter 673, Statutes of 2025, with unanimous or near-unanimous support at every recorded vote reflecting broad bipartisan backing for the deepfake-pornography protections.
