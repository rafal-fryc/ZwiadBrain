## Summary

SB 243 regulates 'companion chatbot' platforms (AI systems with human-like, relationship-sustaining interfaces, excluding pure customer-service bots, limited video-game bots, and non-relational voice assistants), requiring operators to clearly disclose when a reasonable user could be misled into thinking the chatbot is human, and to maintain and publish a protocol for detecting and redirecting suicidal-ideation or self-harm content to crisis resources. For users known to be minors, operators must disclose AI use, send break/AI-disclosure reminders at least every three hours, and take reasonable measures to block sexually explicit chatbot output or suggestions. Beginning July 1, 2027, operators must report safety-protocol data annually to the Office of Suicide Prevention, and any person injured by noncompliance may sue for injunctive relief, damages of at least $1,000, and attorney's fees. Chaptered October 13, 2025.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Senator Steve Padilla introduced SB 243, joined by coauthors Josh Becker, Susan Rubio, Henry Stern, Akilah Weber Pierson, Josh Lowenthal, and Gail Pellerin, on January 30, 2025, to regulate operators of AI "companion chatbots" by requiring human-disclosure notices, a self-harm/suicidal-ideation crisis-referral protocol, minor safeguards such as break reminders and a bar on sexually explicit output, annual reporting to the Office of Suicide Prevention, and a private right of action. The bill moved through three Senate policy committees — Judiciary (Do Pass, re-refer to Health, 12-0, April 8, 2025), Health (Do Pass, re-refer to Appropriations, 8-0, April 30, 2025), and Appropriations (suspense-file placement May 12, then Do Pass 5-0 on May 23) — before the full Senate passed it on third reading on June 3, 2025 by a vote of 28-5. In the Assembly, the bill cleared the Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee (11-1, July 8), Judiciary Committee (9-1, July 15), and Appropriations Committee (13-1, August 29) before passing the Assembly floor on September 10, 2025 by a vote of 59-1. The Senate concurred in the Assembly's amendments on September 11, 2025 by a vote of 33-3, and the bill was enrolled and presented to Governor Gavin Newsom on September 22, 2025. Governor Newsom signed SB 243 into law on October 13, 2025, enacting it as Chapter 677, Statutes of 2025, adding Business and Professions Code Chapter 22.6 (Section 22601 et seq.).
