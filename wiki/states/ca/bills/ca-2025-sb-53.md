## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 53 (Wiener, D-San Francisco), the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act, was introduced on January 7, 2025, as a direct successor to SB 1047, which Governor Newsom had vetoed in 2024 while commissioning the Joint California Policy Working Group on AI Frontier Models. Drawing on that Working Group's June 2025 report, Sen. Wiener expanded the bill in committee to require large frontier AI developers to publish safety and security protocols and pre-deployment transparency reports, report critical safety incidents to the Attorney General, and provide enhanced whistleblower protections for employees, while also establishing a CalCompute public cloud computing consortium. The bill cleared the Senate Governmental Organization Committee 13-0 on March 25, 2025, and the Senate Judiciary Committee 13-0 on April 8, 2025, before passing the full Senate floor 37-0 on May 28, 2025. In the Assembly, the bill was strengthened through amendments adopted at the Assembly Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee on July 16, 2025; the Assembly then passed the bill, and the Senate concurred in the Assembly amendments on September 13, 2025. Governor Newsom signed the bill into law on September 29, 2025.

## Commentary & Analysis

<!-- lawbrain:begin slug=regulation-california-sb53-tfaia -->

> Copied verbatim. Law brain authoritative for analysis; official sources authoritative for bill text and legislative history.

### Summary
**SB 53 — Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (TFAIA)** — was enacted in 2025 (Ch. 138, Stats. 2025) as the successor to **SB 1047**, the Safe and Secure Innovation for Frontier Artificial Intelligence Models Act, which Governor Newsom **vetoed on September 29, 2024**. SB 1047 had set covered-model thresholds at **10²⁶ FLOP / $100M** training cost and would have imposed kill-switch, audit, and pre-deployment-testing obligations on developers. SB 53 retains a transparency-focused subset — frontier-model disclosure obligations and CalCompute reporting — without the catastrophic-harm enforcement framework that drove the veto. Comparative-law commentary frames SB 1047 as a **model-centric, catastrophic-harm** approach, contrasted with the EU AI Act's **use-case-based, four-tier risk pyramid**.

### Key points

#### SB 1047 (vetoed Sept. 29, 2024)
- **Covered models:** ≥10²⁶ training-FLOP threshold OR ≥$100M training cost.
- **Obligations:** Kill-switch capability; pre-deployment safety testing; third-party annual audits; whistleblower protections; **catastrophic-harm** liability ($500M+ damage threshold, mass-casualty scenarios).
- **Frontier Model Division:** New state agency under the California Government Operations Agency.
- **Veto rationale (Newsom):** Threshold tied to compute, not capability; would create false sense of security; preferred risk-based approach.
- **Reception:** Author Sen. Wiener (D); broad civil-society support; opposed by leading AI labs and most of the state's tech industry.

#### SB 53 (signed 2025) — TFAIA
- Retains transparency-focused frontier-model obligations from SB 1047.
- Drops the catastrophic-harm liability and kill-switch mandates.
- Establishes **CalCompute** public-cloud-compute resource concept.
- Practitioner read: "SB 1047 minus the parts Newsom vetoed."

#### Comparative law (SB 1047 vs. EU AI Act)
- **SB 1047 (model-centric):** Triggers based on model parameters; targeted at catastrophic harms; binary covered/not-covered.
- **EU AI Act (use-case-based):** Four-tier risk pyramid (unacceptable / high / limited / minimal); applies across the AI value chain (provider, deployer, importer, distributor); systemic-risk GPAI tier added late in negotiations covers frontier models in a parallel track.
- Convergence: GPAI systemic-risk tier (Art. 51) is closest to SB 1047 model-centric thinking; both use compute thresholds (10²⁵ FLOP for GPAI systemic risk under EU AI Act).
