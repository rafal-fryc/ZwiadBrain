## Summary

Concerning restrictions on making public the personal information of government employees whose official duties involve child abuse and neglect cases.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB19-1197, "Protect Social Workers' Personal Information On Internet," was introduced in the Colorado House on February 20, 2019 by Representatives Terri Carver and Jonathan Singer, with Senators Pete Lee and Bob Gardner as Senate sponsors. The bill responded to safety concerns raised by social workers and other public employees whose home addresses and other personal details were discoverable through internet-accessible public records, and it created a process for such employees to request redaction of that information. The House Judiciary Committee heard the bill on March 7, 2019, adopting two amendments (L.005 and L.006) unanimously before referring the amended measure to the Committee of the Whole on a 10-0 vote. The full House passed the bill on Third Reading on March 12, 2019 by a lopsided 61-0-4 vote, reflecting broad, bipartisan support. In the Senate, the Judiciary Committee reviewed the bill on March 20, 2019 and referred it to the Committee of the Whole unamended with a consent-calendar recommendation on a 5-0 vote, and the full Senate concurred on Third Reading on March 26, 2019 by a 34-0-1 vote. With no floor amendments altering the House-passed text, the bill proceeded directly to the Governor. Jared Polis signed HB19-1197 into law on April 11, 2019, and it took effect immediately as Chapter 95 of the 2019 Session Laws of Colorado. Legislative Council Staff issued fiscal notes both at introduction (March 4, 2019) and again after enactment (May 22, 2019), documenting the measure's minimal state fiscal impact throughout its uncontested path from introduction to signature.
