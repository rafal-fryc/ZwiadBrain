## Summary

Concerning restrictions on making public the personal information of certain employees at risk of threats relating to the administration of human services programs.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB20-1052, "Privacy Protections For Human Services Workers," was introduced in the Colorado House on January 8, 2020 by Representatives Terri Carver and Jonathan Singer, with Senators Bob Gardner and Pete Lee as Senate sponsors. The bill extends the redaction protections created for social workers by 2019's HB19-1197 to a broader class of county and state human services workers -- including those handling child abuse/neglect investigations, at-risk adult mistreatment cases, child support enforcement, public benefits fraud investigations, and juvenile detention staff with direct youth contact -- shielding their home addresses and other personal information from disclosure in internet-accessible public records when disclosure would pose an imminent threat to their safety. The House Judiciary Committee heard the bill on January 23, 2020, adopted Amendment L.001, and referred it to the Committee of the Whole on a 9-0 vote; the House passed it on Second Reading January 28 and on Third Reading January 29 by a unanimous 63-0-0 vote. The bill crossed to the Senate on February 3, 2020 and was assigned to Senate Judiciary, which adopted Amendment L.004 and advanced it 5-0 on March 2; the Senate passed it on Second Reading March 5 and Third Reading March 6 by a 30-0-5 vote. The House concurred with Senate amendments and repassed the bill 62-0-3 on March 10, 2020. Both chambers' presiding officers signed the enrolled bill on March 17, it was transmitted to the Governor on March 23, and Governor Jared Polis signed HB20-1052 into law on March 24, 2020, with the act later codified as Chapter 77 of the 2020 Session Laws. The bill drew no recorded opposition at any stage, passing every floor vote unanimously or with only minor abstentions, reflecting broad bipartisan support for extending worker-safety privacy protections already established for social workers to the wider human services workforce.
