## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Senate Bill 21-190 — the Colorado Privacy Act — was introduced on March 19, 2021, by a bipartisan quartet of prime sponsors: Senators Robert Rodriguez (D-Denver) and Paul Lundeen (R-Monument) in the Senate, and Representatives Monica Duran (D-Wheat Ridge) and Terri Carver (R-Colorado Springs) in the House. The bill cleared the Senate Committee on Business, Labor, & Technology on May 5, 2021, with a unanimous 7-0 vote after adopting four amendments (L.001–L.004); it then advanced through Senate Appropriations on May 14 by another 7-0 vote. The full Senate passed the bill on third reading on May 26, 2021. In the House, the bill received two days of Finance Committee consideration (June 1–2, 2021), during which nine amendments were debated and a 10-1 referral issued to House Appropriations, which forwarded the bill on June 3; the House passed the bill on third reading on June 7, 2021, 57-7. The Senate concurred in the House amendments on June 8, 2021, completing bicameral passage. Governor Jared Polis signed the Colorado Privacy Act into law on July 7, 2021 (effective July 1, 2023), making Colorado the third state — after California and Virginia — to enact comprehensive consumer data privacy legislation.
