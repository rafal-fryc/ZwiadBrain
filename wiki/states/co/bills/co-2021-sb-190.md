## Summary

Colorado's Privacy Act (SB21-190) establishes comprehensive consumer data privacy rights for residents, applying to controllers doing business in the state that process personal data of 100,000 or more consumers annually, or 25,000 or more consumers when data sales generate revenue. Consumers gain rights to access, correct, delete, and port their data, plus opt-out of targeted advertising, data sales, and significant-decision profiling — including through a universal opt-out mechanism that controllers must recognize. Controllers must meet duties of transparency, data minimization, purpose limitation, and care, obtain consent before processing sensitive data, execute contracts with processors, and complete data protection assessments for high-risk processing activities. Enforcement rests exclusively with the Attorney General and district attorneys; the law took effect July 1, 2023, with no private right of action.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Colorado Senate Bill 21-190, the Colorado Privacy Act (CPA), was introduced on March 19, 2021, by a bipartisan quartet — Senate prime sponsors Robert Rodriguez (D-Denver) and Paul Lundeen (R-Monument) and House prime sponsors Monica Duran (D-Wheat Ridge) and Terri Carver (R-Colorado Springs) — and referred to the Senate Business, Labor, & Technology Committee, which voted 7–0 on May 5, 2021, to refer the amended bill to Senate Appropriations; Senate Appropriations in turn referred it 7–0 on May 14, 2021. The full Senate passed the bill on second reading with amendments on May 25, 2021, and on third reading 35–0 on May 26, 2021. In the House, the Finance Committee discussed the bill on June 1, 2021, and referred it as amended to House Appropriations 10–1 on June 2, 2021; House Appropriations referred it 11–0 on June 3, 2021, the House passed it on second reading with amendments on June 4, 2021, and on third reading 57–7 on June 7, 2021; the Senate concurred in the House amendments and repassed the bill 34–0 on June 8, 2021. Governor Jared Polis signed SB 21-190 on July 7, 2021, making Colorado the third state in the nation to enact comprehensive consumer data privacy legislation. The CPA, codified at C.R.S. §§ 6-1-1301 et seq. and effective July 1, 2023, grants Colorado consumers the rights to access, correct, delete, and obtain a portable copy of their personal data, and to opt out of the processing of personal data for targeted advertising, the sale of personal data, and profiling in furtherance of decisions producing significant legal or similarly significant effects; it imposes data protection assessment obligations on controllers for high-risk processing activities, requires controllers to honor a universal opt-out mechanism for targeted advertising and data sales, and is enforced exclusively by the Attorney General and district attorneys, with no private right of action.
