## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB22-1041, "Privacy Protections For Protected Persons," was introduced in the Colorado House on January 12, 2022, by Representatives Andrew Boesenecker and Colin Larson, with Senator Joann Ginal carrying the bill in the Senate. The measure builds on Colorado's earlier anti-doxxing statutes (2019's HB19-1197 and 2020's HB20-1052) by expanding the class of "protected persons" who may request that internet-accessible public records redact their personal information, adding groups such as child representatives, code enforcement officers, health-care workers, animal control officers and bureau of animal protection agents, and office of the respondent parents' counsel staff. The bill first went to the House Public & Behavioral Health & Human Services Committee, which amended and advanced it on a bipartisan 11-2 vote on February 4, 2022. It passed the full House on Third Reading on February 14, 2022, by a vote of 52-10. In the Senate, the Health & Human Services Committee amended and referred the bill on a 6-1 vote on February 28, 2022, and the full Senate passed it on Third Reading on March 4, 2022, by a vote of 24-9. Because the Senate amended the bill, it returned to the House, which concurred in the Senate amendments on March 8, 2022, by a vote of 54-9, sending the final act to the Governor. Governor Jared Polis signed HB22-1041 into law on March 24, 2022, enacting it as Chapter 39 of the 2022 Colorado Session Laws.
