## Summary

SB 171 adds educators who face credible threats to Colorado's existing protected-persons privacy scheme. It applies the same restrictions on publicly posting or disclosing their personal information and provides the same removal-on-request mechanisms available to other protected classes under the framework.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB22-171, "Privacy Protections For Educators," was introduced in the Colorado Senate on March 21, 2022 by prime sponsors Senators Jeff Bridges and Kevin Priola, with House sponsors Representatives Cathy Kipp and Barbara McLachlan. The bill extends the state's existing "protected persons" framework — which allows individuals to have personal address and identifying information redacted from internet-accessible public records when disclosure poses an imminent and serious threat — to public-school educators and related school employees, and amends the Colorado Open Records Act's definition of "personnel file" to cover specific dates of an educator's absence. It moved as a companion measure to HB22-1041, which extended similar protections more broadly. The Senate Education Committee heard the bill on April 14, 2022 and referred it to the Committee of the Whole on a 6-1 vote, with sponsors Bridges and Priola presenting; it passed Senate Third Reading on April 21, 2022 by a vote of 25-9-1, after a floor amendment failed on Second Reading (15-19-1) on April 20. In the House, the Education Committee heard testimony from the Colorado Education Association, Douglas County Federation, Colorado PTA, Stand for Children, Poudre Education Association, and an individual witness on April 27, 2022, referring the bill 7-2 (with Representatives Baisley and Geitner opposed); the House passed it on Third Reading April 29, 2022 by a vote of 46-16-3. Following signature by the Senate President (May 9) and House Speaker (May 10), the enrolled act was delivered to Governor Jared Polis, who signed it into law on May 26, 2022 as Chapter 240 of the 2022 Session Laws of Colorado.
