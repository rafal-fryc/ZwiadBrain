## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB24-1104, "Prohibiting Firefighter Personal Information on Internet," was introduced in the Colorado House on January 25, 2024 by Reps. Ryan Armagost and Marc Snyder, with Sen. Tony Exum as the prime Senate sponsor and more than forty co-sponsors across both chambers. The bill extends Colorado's existing anti-doxxing framework for "protected persons" — which already covered judges, law enforcement, and other public servants — to firefighters, allowing them to request that public entities redact their home address and other personal information from internet-accessible public records. The House Judiciary Committee heard the bill on February 7, 2024, with representatives of the Colorado Professional Fire Fighters and the Colorado State Fire Chiefs testifying in support; the committee voted 10-0 to refer it to the Committee of the Whole. The full House passed the bill on third reading on February 12, 2024 by a vote of 64-0-1. In the Senate, Sen. Exum presented the bill to the Judiciary Committee on March 20, 2024, which voted 4-0 to refer it to the Committee of the Whole on the consent calendar, reflecting the measure's broad, non-controversial support; the full Senate passed it on third reading on March 26, 2024 by a vote of 33-0-2. The bill's fiscal note was revised in June 2024 to reflect the final enacted version. Governor Jared Polis signed HB24-1104 into law on April 11, 2024, and it took effect August 7, 2024 as Chapter 64 of the 2024 Session Laws.
