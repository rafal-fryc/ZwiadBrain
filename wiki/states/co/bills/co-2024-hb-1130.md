## Summary

HB 1130 amends the Colorado Privacy Act to impose specific obligations on entities that collect or use biometric identifiers and biometric data, regardless of whether the entity meets the CPA's normal consumer-processing thresholds. Covered duties include obtaining consent before collection, maintaining a written retention and deletion policy, and prohibiting the sale of biometric identifiers; the law also limits employer collection of employees' biometric data.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB24-1130, "Privacy of Biometric Identifiers & Data," was introduced in the Colorado House on January 29, 2024, by prime sponsors Rep. Lindsey Daugherty and Rep. Mike Lynch, with Sen. Chris Hansen and Sen. Paul Lundeen carrying the bill in the Senate; the measure ultimately drew 35 House and 13 Senate co-sponsors, reflecting broad bipartisan support. The bill amends the Colorado Privacy Act to impose new obligations on controllers that collect biometric identifiers and biometric data, including written retention and deletion policies, pre-collection notice and consent requirements, and restrictions on employer use of biometric data as a condition of employment. It was assigned to the House Judiciary Committee, which on February 14, 2024 adopted four amendments and referred the bill to the House Committee of the Whole on an 11-0 vote. The House passed the bill on Second Reading on February 16 with further amendments and gave it final passage on Third Reading on February 20 by a unanimous 60-0 vote. In the Senate, the bill was assigned to the Senate Judiciary Committee, which heard witness testimony and committee discussion on March 27, 2024 before adopting an additional amendment and referring the bill favorably on a 5-0 vote on April 15. The Senate passed the bill on Second Reading on April 18 with amendments and gave final passage on Third Reading on April 19 by a 33-0 vote. Because the Senate had amended the House-passed bill, the House took up the Senate amendments and concurred, repassing the bill 60-2 on April 22, 2024 — accounting for the string of thirteen recorded votes across the bill's multiple committee and floor stops in both chambers. The engrossed bill was signed by the presiding officers of both chambers on May 28, 2024, and Governor Jared Polis signed HB24-1130 into law on May 31, 2024; it takes effect July 1, 2025 as Chapter 313 of the 2024 Session Laws.
