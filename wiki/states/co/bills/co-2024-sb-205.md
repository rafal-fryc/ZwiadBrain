## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Senate Bill 24-205, titled 'Consumer Protections for Artificial Intelligence,' was introduced in the Colorado General Assembly on April 10, 2024, by Senate Majority Leader Robert Rodriguez, Representatives Brianna Titone and Manny Rutinel, and co-sponsored by Senators Lisa Cutter, Steve Fenberg, Dafna Michaelson Jenet, Kevin Priola, and Faith Winter, and Representative Monica Duran. The bill was heard by the Senate Committee on Judiciary on April 24, 2024, where it adopted Amendment L.001 and was referred to the Committee of the Whole on a narrow 3-2 vote, with industry groups and labor largely opposing or seeking amendments and civil-liberties advocates supporting passage. On May 3, 2024, the full Senate passed the bill on third reading 23-11, also adopting Amendment L.005 by a 33-1 margin. The bill then moved to the House, where the State, Civic, Military, & Veterans Affairs Committee heard it on May 4, 2024, adopted Amendments L.006 and L.007, and advanced it 7-4. The House passed the bill on third reading on May 8, 2024 by a vote of 41-22, and the Senate concurred in the House amendments the same day — voting 35-0 on concurrence and repassing 28-7 — sending the enrolled bill to Governor Jared Polis, who signed it on May 17, 2024, making Colorado the first state in the nation to enact a comprehensive high-risk artificial intelligence consumer-protection law.
