## Summary

SB 41 amends the Colorado Privacy Act to establish heightened protections for minors' personal data online. Controllers offering services likely to be accessed by minors must exercise a duty of care and obtain consent before processing minors' data for targeted advertising, sale, or profiling; the law also requires design safeguards including restrictions on features designed to prolong engagement.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB24-041, "Privacy Protections for Children's Online Data," was introduced in the Colorado Senate on January 10, 2024, by prime sponsors Senate Majority Leader Robert Rodriguez and Senate Minority Leader Paul Lundeen, with House sponsors Lisa Frizell and Javier Mabrey, amending the Colorado Privacy Act to impose heightened duties (data minimization, opt-in consent, and a duty of care) on entities processing minors' personal data online. The bill was assigned to the Senate Business, Labor, & Technology Committee, which held a hearing on April 16, 2024, taking testimony from TechNet (seeking amendments), the Colorado Public Interest Research Group and the Department of Law (in support), and the Colorado Catholic Conference (in favor); the committee adopted two amendments (L.001 and L.002) without objection and voted unanimously (7-0) to refer the amended bill to the Committee of the Whole. The bill passed the Senate on Third Reading on April 23, 2024, by a vote of 33-0-2, after further floor amendments (L.003 and L.004) on Second Reading. It then moved to the House, where it was assigned to the House Business Affairs & Labor Committee, which adopted amendment L.005 unanimously on May 1, 2024, and referred the bill to the House Committee of the Whole on an 11-0 vote; the House passed the bill on Third Reading on May 5, 2024, by a vote of 61-0-4. The Senate concurred with the House amendments and repassed the bill on May 6, 2024, by a vote of 34-0-1. Following signature by the Senate President and House Speaker on May 14, 2024, the bill was transmitted to Governor Jared Polis, who signed it into law on May 31, 2024, enacting it as Chapter 296 of the 2024 Colorado Session Laws, with an effective date of October 1, 2025. The measure passed both chambers with overwhelming bipartisan support and no recorded opposition votes.
