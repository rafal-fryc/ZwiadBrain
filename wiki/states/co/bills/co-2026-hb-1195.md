## Summary

Colorado HB 26-1195, enacted June 3, 2026 and effective August 12, 2026, bars licensed, certified, or registered mental-health professionals (including psychologists, counselors, social workers, marriage and family therapists, addiction counselors, credential candidates, and unlicensed psychotherapists) from using AI systems to directly interact with clients in therapeutic communications, to generate treatment plans or recommendations without the professional's review and approval, or to detect clients' emotions or mental states. AI tools may still be used for administrative or supplementary support as long as the professional retains full responsibility for all interactions, outputs, and data handling, and any AI recording or transcription of a therapy session requires the client's prior written informed consent. The law exempts AI used in accredited education or training programs that are not deployed or marketed for use with clients, patients, or the public.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB26-1195, "Psychotherapy Artificial Intelligence Restrictions," was introduced in the Colorado House on February 11, 2026 by prime sponsors Reps. Gretchen Rydin and Javier Mabrey, with Sens. Judy Amabile and Kyle Mullica carrying it in the Senate. The bill was assigned to House Health & Human Services, which adopted three amendments and referred the amended bill to the House Committee of the Whole by a 13-0 vote on March 4, 2026. The full House passed the bill on third reading on April 16, 2026 by a vote of 62-0-3. In the Senate, the bill was introduced and assigned to Senate Health & Human Services on April 29, 2026; that committee adopted three further amendments and referred the bill to the Senate Committee of the Whole by a 5-2 vote on May 6, 2026, reflecting closer, though still decisive, support. The full Senate passed the bill on third reading on May 11, 2026 by a vote of 33-2-0. The House then concurred in the Senate amendments on May 12, 2026 by a vote of 62-2-1 and repassed the bill 64-0-1 the same day, sending it to the Governor on June 2, 2026. Governor Jared Polis signed HB26-1195 into law on June 3, 2026, with the act set to take effect August 12, 2026 — capping a roughly four-month bipartisan-leaning journey through both chambers with no committee or floor vote coming close to defeat.
