## Summary

Colorado HB 26-1263 (the Colorado Chatbot Safety Act), enacted July 1, 2026 and effective January 1, 2027, requires operators of conversational AI chatbot services to estimate or assess a user's age, disclose that users are interacting with AI rather than a human, and protect minors from sexually explicit content and from chatbot behavior simulating emotional dependence or romantic/sexual interest. Operators must also provide privacy and account-management tools for minor users, adopt suicide and self-harm response protocols that refer users to crisis resources, and are barred from representing chatbot outputs as equivalent to services from a licensed professional. The law is described as the first state statute specifically targeting AI chatbots and directs the Colorado Attorney General to conduct rulemaking on chatbot safety and automated decision-making.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB26-1263, the Colorado Chatbot Safety Act, was introduced in the House on February 19, 2026 by Reps. Sean Camacho and Javier Mabrey with Sens. John Carson and Iman Jodeh as Senate prime sponsors, and referred to the House Business Affairs & Labor Committee. That committee amended the bill and referred it to the Committee of the Whole on March 26, 2026 by a vote of 10-3. On the House floor, members took up amendments on April 20, 2026 before passing the bill on Third Reading on April 21, 2026 by a vote of 40 Yes-24 No-1 Other. The bill was introduced in the Senate on May 4, 2026 and assigned to the Senate Business, Labor, & Technology Committee, which amended it and referred it to the Committee of the Whole on May 5, 2026 by a vote of 5-0; the Senate adopted further floor amendments on Second Reading on May 8, 2026 and passed the bill on Third Reading on May 11, 2026 by a vote of 24 Yes-11 No. The House concurred with the Senate amendments and repassed the bill on May 12, 2026, with a concurrence vote of 48 Yes-16 No-1 Other followed by a repass vote of 42 Yes-22 No-1 Excused. Final legislative action was completed on May 28, 2026, and Governor Jared Polis signed the bill into law on May 29, 2026, with the Act's operator requirements — age estimation, AI disclosure, minor safeguards against sexualized or emotionally manipulative content, and mandatory suicide/self-harm response protocols — taking effect January 1, 2027.
