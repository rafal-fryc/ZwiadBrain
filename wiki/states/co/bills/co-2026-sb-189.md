## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB26-189, "Automated Decision-Making Technology," was introduced in the Colorado Senate on May 1, 2026 by prime sponsors Senators Robert Rodriguez and James Coleman, with Representatives Monica Duran and Jennifer Bacon carrying the bill in the House, alongside Representative Mark Baisley and numerous co-sponsors. The bill repeals and reenacts the consumer-protection framework Colorado established in 2024 under SB24-205 (the Colorado AI Act), replacing its broad "high-risk artificial intelligence system" and algorithmic-discrimination structure with a narrower regime governing "automated decision-making technology" (ADMT) that materially influences "consequential decisions" in education, employment, housing, financial and lending services, insurance, health care, and essential government benefits. The bill moved through the Senate Business, Labor, & Technology Committee on May 5, 2026, where members adopted amendments L.001 and L.002 before referring the amended bill to Appropriations on a 5-0 vote; Senate Appropriations adopted amendment J.001 and forwarded the bill to the floor on a 7-0 vote on May 6. The full Senate passed the bill on Third Reading on May 7, 2026 by a vote of 34-1. In the House, the Judiciary Committee advanced the amended bill to Appropriations 11-0 on May 8, and House Appropriations sent it to the floor 10-1 the same day. The House adopted a permanent floor amendment (50-13, with 2 other) and passed the bill on Third Reading on May 9, 2026 by a vote of 57-6 (2 other). The Senate then concurred with the House amendments and repassed the bill on May 12, 2026, each by a vote of 34-1, sending the Final Act to the Governor. Governor Jared Polis signed SB26-189 into law on May 14, 2026 as Chapter 131 of the 2026 Session Laws of Colorado, with the substantive ADMT requirements—developer technical-documentation duties, deployer consumer notices, and rights to correction and human review—taking effect January 1, 2027, alongside a phased Attorney General enforcement mechanism under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act with a cure period through January 1, 2030.
