## Summary

Connecticut's SB 6 (Public Act 22-15) is the Connecticut Data Privacy Act. It gives residents the right to confirm, access, correct, delete and port their personal data and to opt out of targeted advertising, sale of personal data, and profiling used for solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. Controllers must limit collection to what is adequate, relevant and reasonably necessary, get consent before processing sensitive data, and conduct data protection assessments for higher-risk processing. They must also honor opt-out preference signals by January 1, 2025 and may not target-advertise to or sell data of consumers they know (or wilfully disregard) are 13 to 15 without consent. It applies to businesses that handle data of at least 100,000 consumers (excluding payment-only data), or at least 25,000 consumers while deriving more than 25% of gross revenue from data sales, and exempts government bodies, nonprofits, higher-education institutions, GLBA financial institutions and HIPAA covered entities. The Attorney General has exclusive enforcement authority, with a 60-day cure notice mandatory through December 31, 2024 and discretionary after that. There is no private right of action.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Senate Bill 6, "An Act Concerning Personal Data Privacy and Online Monitoring," was referred to the Joint Committee on General Law on February 9, 2022. The committee voted to draft the bill 13-4 on February 17, 2022, held a public hearing on March 3, 2022, and reported a Joint Favorable Substitute by a 14-4 vote on March 15, 2022, under the leadership of co-chair Sen. James Maroney, the bill's principal architect. The Senate referred the substitute bill to the Judiciary Committee, which reported it Joint Favorable 25-14 on April 11, 2022, and then to the Appropriations Committee, which reported it Joint Favorable 48-0 on April 18, 2022. On April 20, 2022, the Senate adopted Amendment Schedule A (LCO 4985), offered by Senate President Pro Tempore Martin Looney, and then passed the bill as amended by Roll Call Vote 147, 35-0. On April 28, 2022, the House of Representatives concurred, adopting the same Senate Amendment Schedule A and passing the bill as amended by Roll Call Vote 137, 144-5. The bill became Public Act 22-15 on May 4, 2022, was transmitted to the Governor on May 5, 2022, and was signed by Governor Ned Lamont on May 10, 2022, making Connecticut the fifth state to enact a comprehensive consumer data privacy law, following California, Virginia, Colorado, and Utah. As chaptered, the Connecticut Data Privacy Act imposes duties on data controllers and processors, grants consumers rights to access, correct, delete, and obtain a portable copy of their personal data, and requires controllers to honor consumer opt-outs of targeted advertising, the sale of personal data, and profiling in furtherance of decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects. The Act is enforced exclusively by the Connecticut Attorney General and took effect July 1, 2023.
