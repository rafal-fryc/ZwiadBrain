## Summary

Florida's SB 262 (2023), the Florida Digital Bill of Rights, creates consumer rights to access, correct, delete, and port personal data and to opt out of targeted advertising, data sales, and significant-effect profiling for large controllers meeting specific revenue/business thresholds (e.g., over $1 billion in global revenue plus criteria like deriving 50% of revenue from online ad sales), enforced solely by the Department of Legal Affairs under the state's deceptive trade practices law with no private right of action. The same act separately bars government entities from directing social media platforms to moderate content, restricts online platforms likely accessed by children from harmful profiling and unnecessary data collection/precise geolocation tracking (enforceable civil penalties up to $50,000 per violation, tripled for known minors), and requires opt-in consent before selling sensitive or biometric data. Most provisions took effect July 1, 2024.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 262, sponsored by Sen. Jennifer Bradley, was filed on March 3, 2023 and moved through the Senate Commerce and Tourism Committee, which reported a committee substitute and passed the bill 9-0 on April 4, 2023. The Senate Rules Committee approved the measure 19-0 on April 24, 2023, and the full Senate passed CS/CS/SB 262 by a vote of 38-0 on April 28, 2023. The House took up the bill and passed it 106-10 on May 3, 2023, having adopted House amendments. On May 4, 2023, the Senate concurred in the House amendments and gave final passage 40-0, while the House gave its own final passage 110-2 the same day. The bill was ordered enrolled, signed by the presiding officers, and presented to the Governor, and Governor Ron DeSantis approved it on June 6, 2023, enacting it as Chapter 2023-201, Laws of Florida, effective July 1, 2024. As enacted, the law — known as the Florida Digital Bill of Rights — imposes consumer-privacy obligations on very large controllers meeting revenue and data-processing thresholds (including controllers with global gross annual revenue exceeding $1 billion), alongside protections for children's online data and transparency obligations for large online platforms and search engines. It grants Florida consumers rights to access, correct, delete, and obtain a portable copy of their personal data, and to opt out of targeted advertising, sale of personal data, and certain profiling, with enforcement vested exclusively in the Florida Department of Legal Affairs (the Attorney General).
