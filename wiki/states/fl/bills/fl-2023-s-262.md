## Summary

Florida's SB 262 (2023), the Florida Digital Bill of Rights, creates consumer rights to access, correct, delete, and port personal data and to opt out of targeted advertising, data sales, and significant-effect profiling for large controllers meeting specific revenue/business thresholds (e.g., over $1 billion in global revenue plus criteria like deriving 50% of revenue from online ad sales), enforced solely by the Department of Legal Affairs under the state's deceptive trade practices law with no private right of action. The same act separately bars government entities from directing social media platforms to moderate content, restricts online platforms likely accessed by children from harmful profiling and unnecessary data collection/precise geolocation tracking (enforceable civil penalties up to $50,000 per violation, tripled for known minors), and requires opt-in consent before selling sensitive or biometric data. Most provisions took effect July 1, 2024.
