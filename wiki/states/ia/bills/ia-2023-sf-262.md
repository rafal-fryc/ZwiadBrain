## Summary

Iowa Senate File 262 enacts the Iowa Consumer Data Protection Act (new Iowa Code chapter 715D), the sixth comprehensive U.S. state consumer-privacy law, applying to businesses that target Iowa residents and annually control or process personal data of at least 100,000 consumers, or of at least 25,000 consumers while deriving over 50% of gross revenue from selling personal data; it exempts state agencies, financial institutions/GLBA-covered data, HIPAA-regulated entities, nonprofits, and institutions of higher education. Consumers get rights to confirm and access their personal data, delete data they provided, obtain a portable copy, and opt out of the sale of personal data, but the Act does not grant a right to opt out of targeted advertising or profiling generally, and sensitive-data processing requires only opt-out notice rather than opt-in consent. Controllers must maintain reasonable data security, honor consumer requests within 90 days (extendable 45 days), provide a privacy notice and an appeals process, and flow down processor obligations via contract; enforcement is exclusively by the Attorney General, who must give a 90-day cure notice before suing and may seek civil penalties of up to $7,500 per violation (paid into the state's consumer education and litigation fund), with no private right of action. Effective January 1, 2025.
