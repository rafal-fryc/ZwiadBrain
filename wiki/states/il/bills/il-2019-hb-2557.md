## Summary

Creates the Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act (820 ILCS 42/). Provides that an employer that asks applicants to record video interviews and uses an artificial intelligence analysis of the applicant-submitted videos must, before asking applicants for positions based in Illinois to submit video interviews: (1) notify each applicant before the interview that artificial intelligence may be used to analyze the applicant's video interview and consider the applicant's fitness for the position; (2) provide each applicant with information before the interview explaining how the artificial intelligence works and what general types of characteristics it uses to evaluate applicants; and (3) obtain, before the interview, the applicant's consent to be evaluated by the artificial intelligence program. Bars an employer from using artificial intelligence to evaluate applicants who have not consented. Limits sharing of applicant videos to persons whose expertise or technology is necessary to evaluate an applicant's fitness for a position (Section 10), and requires an employer, upon an applicant's request, to delete the applicant's videos within 30 days and to instruct anyone who received copies to delete them as well, including all electronically generated backup copies (Section 15).

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB 2557, the Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act, was introduced on February 13, 2019, by Representative Jaime M. Andrade Jr. in the Illinois House of Representatives during the 101st General Assembly, with cosponsors Diane Pappas and Terra Costa Howard. The bill imposed on employers who solicit and AI-analyze applicant video interviews three pre-interview duties: written notification that AI may be used, an explanation of how the system evaluates candidates, and the applicant's consent. The House Labor and Commerce Committee approved it unanimously 25-0-0 on March 6, 2019, and the full House passed it 101-3-4 on March 27, 2019, over a dissent from Representative Mary Flowers who argued the consent-only framework would facilitate discrimination. In the Senate, Senator Iris Y. Martinez carried the bill; the Senate Labor Committee approved it unanimously 15-0-0 on May 1, 2019. The Senate adopted Floor Amendment No. 1—removing the requirement that disclosure and consent be in writing and making video deletion contingent on an applicant request—and passed the amended bill 58-0-0 on May 23, 2019. The House concurred in the Senate amendment 115-0-0 on May 29, 2019. Governor JB Pritzker signed the bill as Public Act 101-0260 on August 9, 2019; it took effect January 1, 2020, making Illinois the first state in the nation to regulate employer use of artificial intelligence in hiring video interviews.
