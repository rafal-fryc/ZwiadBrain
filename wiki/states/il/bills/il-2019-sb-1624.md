## Summary

Amends the Personal Information Protection Act. Provides that a data collector required to report breaches to more than 100 Illinois residents as a result of a single breach must also report to the Attorney General. Provides that the Attorney General shall report to the General Assembly specified information concerning breaches of data security by February 1 of each year.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 1624 was introduced in the Illinois Senate on February 15, 2019, by lead sponsor Sen. Suzanne (Suzy) Glowiak Hilton, with Sen. Jacqueline Collins, Sen. Kimberly Lightford, Sen. Rachelle Crowe, Sen. Julie Morrison, Sen. Laura Murphy, Sen. Cristina Castro, Sen. Christopher Belt, Sen. William Cunningham, Sen. Antonio Munoz, and Sen. Elgie Sims joining as cosponsors, and Rep. Jaime Andrade carrying the bill in the House. The measure amends the Personal Information Protection Act (815 ILCS 530/10) to require that data collectors notify the Illinois Attorney General whenever a breach of security affects more than 500 Illinois residents, and to direct the Attorney General to submit an annual report to the General Assembly, by February 1 of each year, summarizing statewide breach-notification data, including the total number of Illinois residents affected in the preceding calendar year. The bill passed the Senate on Third Reading and crossed over to the House around April 10, 2019, cleared the House, and received final passage around May 27, 2019, with no recorded opposition in available vote summaries. Governor J.B. Pritzker approved the bill on August 9, 2019, and it was certified as Public Act 101-0343, taking effect January 1, 2020. The enactment strengthened Illinois's existing data-breach notification framework by adding a state-level Attorney General reporting and oversight mechanism alongside the pre-existing consumer-notification requirements.
