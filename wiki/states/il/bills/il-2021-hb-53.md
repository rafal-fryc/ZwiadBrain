## Summary

Amends the Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act. Provides that employers that rely solely upon artificial intelligence to determine whether an applicant will qualify for an in-person interview must gather and report certain demographic information to the Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity. Requires the Department to analyze the data and report to the Governor and General Assembly whether the data discloses a racial bias in the use of artificial intelligence.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

House Bill 53, amending Illinois' Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act (820 ILCS 42), was introduced in the Illinois House on January 13, 2021 by lead sponsor Rep. Jaime Andrade, with Rep. Gregory Harris joining as a House sponsor and Sens. John Connor and Jacqueline Collins carrying the bill in the Senate. The bill crossed over to the Senate around April 21, 2021, and cleared both chambers by late May 2021 (final passage May 25, 2021) before being transmitted to the Governor. The measure requires employers that rely solely on artificial intelligence analysis of a video interview to decide whether an applicant advances to an in-person interview to collect and report to the Illinois Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity the race and ethnicity of applicants who are and are not granted interviews, as well as those ultimately hired; the Department in turn analyzes the data annually for evidence of racial bias in AI-driven hiring and reports to the Governor and General Assembly. Governor J.B. Pritzker signed the bill on July 9, 2021, enacting it as Public Act 102-0047, with an effective date of January 1, 2022. The bill moved through committee and both chambers without documented floor opposition, and stands as one of the first state laws requiring bias auditing/reporting for AI-based hiring tools.
