## Summary

Creates the Civil Liability for Doxing Act (740 ILCS 195/). Establishes a private civil cause of action allowing a person to sue anyone who intentionally publishes their personally identifiable information without consent and with intent to harm or harass, where the publication causes economic injury, emotional distress, a reasonable fear of serious bodily injury or death, or a substantial disruption to daily life. Permits recovery of damages (including punitive damages), reasonable attorney's fees and costs, and injunctive or other equitable relief — including a court order prohibiting further publication of the information. Preserves exceptions for reporting to or cooperating with law enforcement and for constitutionally protected speech and activity.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

House Bill 2954, the Civil Liability for Doxing Act, was introduced in the Illinois House on February 16, 2023, by Rep. Jennifer Gong-Gershowitz, with Rep. Bob Morgan and numerous House and Senate cosponsors (including Justin Slaughter, Curtis Tarver, Dan Ugaste, Daniel Didech, Jonathan Carroll, Ann Williams, Terra Costa Howard, Kelly Cassidy, and Kevin Olickal) and lead Senate sponsor Sen. Julie Morrison. The bill creates a new civil cause of action for victims of "doxing" — the intentional, non-consensual publication of a person's personally identifiable information with intent to harm or harass, causing significant economic injury, emotional distress, fear of bodily harm, or substantial life disruption — allowing recovery of damages, attorney's fees, and injunctive relief, while expressly preserving protections for law-enforcement reporting and constitutionally protected speech. The bill was assigned to the House Judiciary - Civil Committee, amended by House Floor Amendment No. 1 (filed by Rep. Gong-Gershowitz on March 14, 2023, adding definitions, exceptions, and remedy provisions), and passed the House before crossing to the Senate Judiciary Committee. It passed both chambers essentially without opposition and was sent to the Governor. Governor J.B. Pritzker approved the bill on August 4, 2023, enacting it as Public Act 103-0439, codified at 740 ILCS 195/, with an effective date of January 1, 2024.
