## Summary

Amends the Biometric Information Privacy Act. Defines "electronic signature" as an electronic sound, symbol, or process attached to or logically associated with a record and executed or adopted by a person with the intent to sign the record. Provides that "written release" includes an electronic signature. Provides that a private entity that more than once collects or discloses a person's biometric identifier or biometric information from the same person in violation of the Act has committed a single violation for which the aggrieved person is entitled to, at most, one recovery. Effective immediately.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 2979 was introduced in the Illinois Senate on January 31, 2024, by Sen. Bill Cunningham (D-Chicago) as a direct legislative response to the Illinois Supreme Court's 2023 decision in Cothron v. White Castle, in which the court held that a separate BIPA violation accrues with each unauthorized biometric scan and expressly invited the General Assembly to clarify its intent on damages. After referral to the Senate Judiciary Committee — which voted 6-3 on March 13, 2024 to advance the bill — the full Senate passed SB 2979 on third reading on April 11, 2024, by a bipartisan vote of 46-13, with Senate Minority Leader John Curran (R-Downers Grove) joining the majority. In the House, Rep. Ann Williams (D-Chicago) carried the bill; the House Judiciary – Civil Committee voted 10-5 on May 1, 2024 (largely along party lines) to recommend passage, and the full House approved it 81-30 on May 16, 2024. The bill was sent to the Governor on June 14, 2024, and Governor JB Pritzker signed it into law on August 2, 2024, as Public Act 103-0769, effective immediately. The enacted law overrides the Cothron per-scan accrual rule by limiting BIPA damages to a single recovery per person per collection method, and adds a statutory definition of "electronic signature" to authorize electronic written releases.
