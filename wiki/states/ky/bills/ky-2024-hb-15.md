## Summary

Creates new sections of KRS Chapter 367 to establish consumer rights relating to personal data; defines terms; exempts certain persons or entities from the statutory provisions of this Act; establishes certain consumer rights relating to personal data, including the rights to confirm whether data is being processed, to correct any inaccuracies in the consumer’s personal data, to delete personal data provided by the consumer, to obtain a copy of the consumer’s personal data that was previously provided, and to opt out of targeted advertising, the sale of data, or profiling of the consumer; sets forth requirements for persons or entities that control and process consumer data; establishes that the Attorney General has exclusive authority to enforce the consumer data privacy rights; creates a consumer privacy fund to be administered by the Office of the Attorney General; and provides that the Act may be cited as the Kentucky Consumer Data Protection Act. EFFECTIVE January 1, 2026.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

House Bill 15, the Kentucky Consumer Data Protection Act, was introduced by Rep. Joshua Branscum with cosponsors Reps. Steven Rudy, Josh Bray, Beverly Chester-Burton, Daniel Elliott, and Deanna Frazier Gordon, and referred to the House Committee on Committees on February 2, 2024. The House passed the bill 92-0 on February 20, 2024, incorporating House Floor Amendment 1 (Rep. Steven Rudy), which added exemptions for utility and utility-affiliate data and for data collected under the federal Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act of 2005. The bill was then referred to the Senate Committee on Committees on February 21, 2024, and the Senate passed it 35-0 on March 11, 2024, with Senate Floor Amendments 1 and 2, both sponsored by Sen. Whitney Westerfield. The House took up the Senate's changes on March 25, 2024, and concurred in both Senate floor amendments, passing the bill 94-0 on March 27, 2024; the enrolled bill was signed by the Speaker and Senate President and delivered to the Governor that same day. Governor Andy Beshear signed HB 15 into law on April 4, 2024, as Acts Chapter 72. Modeled on Virginia's consumer privacy framework, the law establishes consumer rights to access, correct, delete, and obtain a portable copy of personal data, along with rights to opt out of targeted advertising, the sale of personal data, and profiling in furtherance of decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects; it imposes duties on controllers and processors and is enforced exclusively by the Kentucky Attorney General, with a Legislative Research Commission fiscal note projecting a new Consumer Data Privacy division within that office. The law took effect January 1, 2026.
