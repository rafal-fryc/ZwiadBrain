## Summary

Kentucky SB 4 (2025) has two halves. The larger half is state AI governance: it defines high-risk and general-purpose AI systems, defines "deployer" as a state department, agency, or administrative body, and directs the Commonwealth Office of Technology to create an Artificial Intelligence Governance Committee setting standards, transparency requirements, and a central inventory of state generative and high-risk AI systems — government own-use, and out of scope on its own. The bill is tracked for its second half, which amends KRS 117.001 and adds a synthetic-media provision to the election code: a candidate whose appearance, action, or speech is altered by synthetic media in an electioneering communication may seek injunctive relief against the sponsor, with a clear-and-conspicuous disclosure as an affirmative defence, and — critically — a medium or its advertising sales representative may itself be held liable if it intentionally removes such a disclosure and does not restore it or pull the communication when notified, or changes the content so that it qualifies as synthetic media. That medium-facing duty is why this law is tracked where purely election-actor disclosure laws are not.
