## Summary

The Louisiana Data Privacy Act (LDPA), signed May 29, 2026 as the nation's 22nd comprehensive state privacy law, takes effect January 1, 2027 and applies to businesses operating in Louisiana that either exceed $25 million in annual gross revenue, process personal data of 75,000 or more consumers/households/devices annually for commercial purposes, or derive at least 50% of annual revenue from selling personal information; it exempts GLBA financial institutions, HIPAA-covered entities, nonprofits, higher-education institutions, and employment-context data. It grants consumers rights to access, correct, delete, and port their data, and to opt out of targeted advertising, sale of data, and profiling used for automated decisions in areas like financial services, employment, and education, including recognition of authorized agents and browser/device opt-out preference signals; controllers must limit data collection to what is adequate and reasonably necessary, obtain consent for secondary uses, post privacy notices, conduct data protection assessments for higher-risk processing, and — notably — display specific statutory notices when selling sensitive or biometric data ("NOTICE: We may sell your sensitive personal data" / "...biometric personal data"), with sensitive categories (race/ethnicity, religion, health, sexual orientation, immigration status, biometrics, children's data under 13, precise geolocation) requiring affirmative opt-in consent. Enforcement rests exclusively with the Louisiana Attorney General under the state's Unfair Trade Practices law, with no private right of action and a 30-day cure period that sunsets July 31, 2027.
