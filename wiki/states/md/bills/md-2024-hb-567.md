## Summary

Maryland's HB 567 (Chapter 454), the Maryland Online Data Privacy Act of 2024, adds a new subtitle to the Commercial Law Article regulating how controllers and processors may process consumers' personal data. It gives Maryland residents rights over their personal data, lets them use an authorized agent to opt out of processing, and requires controllers to provide exercise methods and a privacy notice, to bind processors by contract, and to conduct and document data protection assessments for processing that presents a heightened risk of harm. Sensitive data is defined to include consumer health data (including gender-affirming and reproductive or sexual health care), genetic and biometric data, a child's data, and precise geolocation within a 1,750-foot radius. A violation is an unfair, abusive, or deceptive trade practice enforceable and penalized under the Maryland Consumer Protection Act.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

House Bill 567, the Maryland Online Data Privacy Act of 2024, was introduced by Delegate Sara Love and crossfiled with Senate companion SB 541 (Sen. Dawn Gile). Following First Reading on 2024-01-24, the bill was referred to the House Economic Matters Committee, which held a public hearing on 2024-02-13, with HB 567 as the lead bill on that day's agenda. On 2024-03-13 the committee reported the bill favorable with amendments, adopting committee amendment 193527/1. On the House floor on 2024-03-14, Delegate Buckel offered three amendments — 433229/1, 113026/1, and 603129/1 — all of which were rejected, by votes of 33-101, 32-103, and 35-100 respectively, before the bill passed Second Reading as amended. The House passed HB 567 on Third Reading on 2024-03-16 by a vote of 105-32. The bill crossed to the Senate, was referred to the Finance Committee, which held its own hearing on 2024-03-21 and reported the bill favorable with amendments on 2024-04-04, adopting amendment 903224/1; the Senate then passed the bill on Third Reading on 2024-04-05 by a vote of 42-2. The House concurred in the Senate amendments and gave the bill final passage on 2024-04-08 by a vote of 104-34, sending it to the Governor. Governor Wes Moore approved the bill on 2024-05-09 as Chapter 454. As enacted, MODPA requires controllers to limit collection of personal data to what is reasonably necessary and proportionate to provide the product or service requested, imposes a stricter 'strictly necessary' standard for sensitive data, grants consumers rights to access, correct, delete, and opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and certain profiling, and is enforced exclusively by the Maryland Attorney General's Division of Consumer Protection under the Maryland Consumer Protection Act. The law took effect 2025-10-01.
