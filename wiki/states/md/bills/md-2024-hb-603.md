## Summary

Requiring a certain entity that offers an online product reasonably likely to be accessed by children to complete a certain data protection impact assessment by April 1, 2026, under certain circumstances; requiring certain privacy protections for certain online products; prohibiting certain data collection and sharing practices; authorizing certain monitoring practices to allow a child's parent or guardian to monitor the child's online activity or location without providing an obvious signal to the child; etc.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB 603, the Maryland Kids Code (Consumer Protection – Online Products and Services – Data of Children), was introduced by Del. Jared Solomon with Del. C.T. Wilson and Del. Sara Love among its sponsors and referred to the House Economic Matters Committee, which held a hearing on the bill on February 13, 2024. The committee reported the bill Favorable with Amendments (Amendment 843323/1), and the House gave it a Second Reading with amendments on March 13, 2024, before passing it on Third Reading by a vote of 135-0 on March 14, 2024. The bill then moved to the Senate, where the Finance Committee reported it favorably on March 28, 2024, ahead of a Second Reading on March 29. The Senate passed the bill on Third Reading 47-0 on April 3, 2024; following a reconsideration motion by Sen. Beidle, the Senate revisited the bill the same day and passed it with further amendments, 46-0. The House refused to concur in the Senate's amendments on April 5, 2024, sending the bill back to the Senate, which took a final Third Reading vote and passed it 43-0 on April 6, 2024; the bill was returned passed on April 8, 2024. Governor Wes Moore approved the bill on May 9, 2024, enacting it as Chapter 461, part of the national wave of Age-Appropriate Design Code legislation. As chaptered, the law imposes a data protection impact assessment duty and design-code obligations on covered entities offering online products or services reasonably likely to be accessed by children under 18, prohibits certain data uses and design features harmful to children, and vests enforcement in the Maryland Attorney General under the Consumer Protection Act, with an effective date of October 1, 2024.
