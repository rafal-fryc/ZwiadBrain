## Summary

Maine's LD 1727 enacts a new chapter of the Unfair Trade Practices law barring any person from using an AI chatbot or similar computer technology to engage in trade and commerce with a consumer in a way that might mislead a reasonable consumer into believing they are talking to a human, unless the consumer is given clear and conspicuous notice that they are not interacting with a person. A violation is treated as a violation of the Maine Unfair Trade Practices Act, enforceable through that statute's existing remedies. It applies broadly to any business or seller using AI-driven customer communications in the state.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

LD 1727, "An Act to Ensure Transparency in Consumer Transactions Involving Artificial Intelligence," was sponsored by Rep. Amy Kuhn of Falmouth, with cosponsors Sen. Anne Carney and Reps. Adam Lee, Jennifer Poirier, Eleanor Sato, and Daniel Sayre, and introduced in the Maine House (as HP 1154) on April 17, 2025, during the 132nd Legislature's First Regular Session. The bill was referred to the Joint Standing Committee on Health Coverage, Insurance and Financial Services, which vets Maine's Unfair Trade Practices Act measures, for review before returning to the floor. Both chambers engrossed the bill on June 9, 2025, and it was passed to be enacted in the House on June 10, 2025, with the Senate concurring the same day, without a recorded roll-call tally appearing in the legislature's LawMaker record for final passage. Governor Janet Mills signed the bill into law on June 12, 2025, and it was enacted as Public Law 2025, Chapter 294, adding a new chapter to the Maine Unfair Trade Practices Act that bars using an AI chatbot or similar technology to mislead a reasonable consumer into believing they are interacting with a human, absent clear and conspicuous notice otherwise, with violations enforceable under UTPA's existing remedies.
