## Summary

Maine's LD 2082 prohibits any person from providing, advertising, or offering therapy or psychotherapy services to the public, including through internet-based artificial intelligence, unless the services are provided by a licensed professional; a violation is a violation of the Maine Unfair Trade Practices Act, with an exception for IRB-approved research. Licensed practitioners may use AI only for administrative support (such as scheduling and billing) or supplementary support (such as notes, anonymized progress analysis, and referral organization), and must remain fully responsible for its use. Supplementary support requires a recorded or transcribed session, advance written notice to the client of the AI's purpose and data storage, retention, training, and deletion practices, and the client's written, revocable consent. A licensee may not deny treatment for refusing consent or let AI make independent therapeutic decisions, communicate therapeutically with clients, or generate treatment recommendations or plans without licensee review and approval. Violations are subject to board discipline, licensing boards must adopt rules, client waivers are void, and civil actions remain available.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

LD 2082 (HP 1397), "An Act to Regulate the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Providing Certain Mental Health Services," was introduced by Rep. Amy Kuhn (D-Falmouth), with Sen. Teresa Pierce as a cosponsor, and referred to the Joint Standing Committee on Health Coverage, Insurance and Financial Services on January 7, 2026. The committee held work sessions on February 25, 2026 (where the bill was tabled) and March 4, 2026, at which it voted Ought To Pass As Amended, adopting Committee Amendment "A" (H-980); the committee formally reported the bill out on April 6, 2026. The bill was engrossed by both the House and the Senate on April 7, 2026, and passed to be enacted in both chambers on April 8, 2026 — no recorded roll-call tally for these actions appears on the official bill-status page, consistent with passage without a division vote. Governor Janet Mills signed the bill on April 13, 2026, enacting it as Public Law Chapter 687, with an effective date of July 29, 2026. As enacted, the law bars AI systems from making independent therapeutic decisions, engaging directly in therapeutic communication with clients, or generating treatment plans or recommendations without review by a licensed clinician, while permitting AI to be used for administrative and supplementary support so long as the licensee retains full responsibility for care.
