## Summary

Maine's LD 2176 creates a new tenant-privacy protection barring landlords, their agents, or anyone acting under a landlord's direction from disclosing a tenant's, occupant's, or applicant's personal information (identifying details, protected characteristics, government ID or Social Security numbers, financial data, or crime-victim status) to third parties without consent, when done to harass, intimidate, or force someone out of a rental unit outside the judicial eviction process and without a legitimate business purpose; it lists exceptions such as subpoenas, law enforcement requests, sale/refinancing, or affordable-housing administration. Violators are liable for actual damages or $1,000 (whichever is greater), injunctive relief, and attorney's fees after a contested hearing. The bill also raises the damages recoverable for unlawful landlord entry from $100 to $250.
