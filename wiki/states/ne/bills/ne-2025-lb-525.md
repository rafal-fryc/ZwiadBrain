## Summary

Nebraska LB 525 enacts two separate laws. The Agricultural Data Privacy Act declares that agricultural producers own and control the agricultural data (agronomic, climate/weather, land, livestock, management, and sustainability data) generated from their farms, land, devices, and equipment; it bars a controller or processor from selling that data without the producer's express written consent, requires contracts entered on or after January 1, 2027 to state that prohibition, mandates reasonable data-security safeguards, voids any contract clause waiving these protections, and gives the Attorney General exclusive enforcement authority (injunctive relief plus a $1,000 civil penalty per violation, with a notice-and-cure period for certain violations) rather than a private right of action; it also adds agricultural data to the public-records exceptions under section 84-712.05. The Conversational Artificial Intelligence Safety Act, whose substantive provisions become operative July 1, 2027, requires operators of public-facing conversational AI services to clearly disclose to minor account holders that they are interacting with AI, bars reward mechanics designed to spur engagement by minors, requires reasonable measures preventing the AI from producing sexually explicit content or statements simulating human sentience, emotional dependence, or romantic/sexual interest with minors, requires privacy-management tools for minors (and their parents if under 13), requires a protocol for responding to user prompts about suicide or self-harm by referring users to crisis services, and prohibits an operator from representing that the AI provides professional mental or behavioral health care; the Attorney General may bring civil actions for actual damages, civil penalties of $1,000 to $500,000 per operator, and fees, but the Act creates no private right of action and does not impose liability on an AI model's developer for a third-party operator's violations.
