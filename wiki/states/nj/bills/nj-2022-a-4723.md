## Summary

Requires motor vehicle dealer to offer to delete personal information in motor vehicles in certain situations.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Assembly Bill 4723 was introduced on October 11, 2022 by Assemblyman John F. McKeon, with co-sponsors including Assemblymen Paul Moriarty and Kevin Rooney, and referred to the Assembly Consumer Affairs Committee. That committee reported the bill favorably by a 5-0 vote on November 14, 2022, and it was then reported and referred to the Assembly Science, Innovation and Technology Committee. The bill's first reprint reflects committee-stage amendments to the introduced text, narrowing and clarifying the dealer's data-deletion obligation and adding fee-disclosure and definitional provisions. The bill did not move again until March 23, 2023, when it was reported out of committee with further amendments and advanced to second reading, and it picked up an additional Assembly floor amendment sponsored by Assemblyman Wimberly on December 21, 2023. The Assembly passed the bill unanimously, 76-0-0, on January 8, 2024. The same day, the Senate received the bill without reference, gave it a second reading, substituted it for the identical companion bill S2740, and passed it 35-0, sending it to the Governor as a single enrolled act. Governor Phil Murphy approved the measure on January 16, 2024, as P.L.2023, c.314. As enacted, the law requires a motor vehicle dealer that takes possession of a vehicle from a consumer for resale or lease to offer to delete the consumer's personal information stored in the vehicle's electronic systems — expressly including navigation history, paired-phone data, and garage-door codes — using National Institute of Standards and Technology-aligned data-sanitization methods, manufacturer-specified overwrite techniques, or a factory reset; dealers may charge a disclosed, reasonable fee for the service and must inform consumers they may instead attempt deletion themselves or use another vendor, with violations subject to civil penalties of $500 for a first offense and $1,000 for subsequent offenses enforced by the Division of Consumer Affairs.
