## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S332, the New Jersey Data Privacy Act, was introduced in the Senate and referred to the Senate Commerce Committee on 2022-01-11. The committee, with Sen. Troy Singleton as lead sponsor, reported the bill favorably by a 3-2 vote on 2022-06-09, and a series of sponsor floor amendments in August, November, and December 2022 refined the bill's definitions of "consumer," "personally identifiable information," and "operator" and clarified that the bill creates no private right of action. The full Senate passed the bill 27-11 on 2023-02-02. It then moved to the Assembly, where it was referred to the Science, Innovation and Technology Committee on 2023-02-06, which reported it out with amendments on 2023-05-11. The bill was recommitted to the Assembly Judiciary Committee, which reported it out with amendments on 2023-12-18, followed by a floor amendment offered by Assemblyman Benjie Wimberly (with Assemblyman Mukherji separately proposing amendments) on 2023-12-21. The Assembly then substituted the amended S332 for its companion A1971 and passed the bill 46-27 on 2024-01-08. The Senate concurred in the Assembly amendments the same day, giving the bill final passage in both houses by a 21-14 vote. Governor Phil Murphy signed the bill on 2024-01-16 as P.L.2023, c.266, an act concerning online services, consumers, and personal data supplementing Title 56 of the Revised Statutes. As enacted, the law imposes duties on data controllers and processors, grants consumers rights to access, correct, delete, and obtain a portable copy of their personal data, requires opt-outs from targeted advertising, sale, and profiling, authorizes the Division of Consumer Affairs to adopt implementing rules, and vests enforcement in the Attorney General, with an effective date of 2025-01-15.
