## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

The Stop Hacks and Improve Electronic Data Security (SHIELD) Act was introduced in the New York State Assembly on February 14, 2019, as A5635, sponsored by Assembly Member Michael DenDekker at the request of the Department of Law, with Assembly Member Amy Paulin as co-sponsor; the bill was referred to the Committee on Consumer Affairs and Protection, then advanced sequentially through the Codes, Ways and Means, and Rules committees. Senate companion bill S5575 was introduced on May 7, 2019, by Senator Kevin Thomas, Chair of the Senate Consumer Protection Committee, with co-sponsors David Carlucci and Alessandra Biaggi; after referral to the Internet and Technology Committee and discharge to the Consumer Protection Committee, it was reported favorably 7–0 on May 30, 2019. The Senate passed S5575-A on June 5, 2019, by a vote of 42–21; the bill was amended on third reading to print number S5575-B on June 13, 2019, and the amended version passed the Senate 41–21 on June 17, 2019, the same day the Assembly substituted S5575-B for A5635-B and passed it. The enrolled bill was delivered to Governor Andrew Cuomo on July 22, 2019, and he signed it as Chapter 117 of the Laws of 2019 on July 25, 2019. Most provisions took effect 90 days after enactment; the new reasonable data-security safeguard requirements became effective 240 days after enactment, on March 21, 2020.
