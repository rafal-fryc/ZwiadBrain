leg_history:
  narrative: Assembly Bill A3904, sponsored by Assemblymember Michael Cusick with
    cosponsors including Steven Englebright, Kenneth Zebrowski, Patricia Fahy, William
    Magnarelli, Fred Thiele, John T. McDonald III, Thomas Abinanti, Peter Abbate,
    and Andrew Hevesi, was introduced on January 28, 2021 and referred to the Assembly
    Energy Committee. The bill, which amends the Energy Law, Executive Law, and Public
    Service Law to strengthen protection of critical energy infrastructure from cyberattacks
    -- citing the 2015 Ukraine power grid hack and a 2017 breach at Fort Drum as justification
    -- was amended (print 3904a) and carried over into the 2022 session, where it
    was further amended (print 3904b) in April 2022 and reported to the Rules Committee
    in May 2022. A companion bill, S5579 (later S5579A), was introduced in the Senate
    on March 11, 2021 by Senator Kevin S. Parker and followed a parallel path through
    the Senate Energy and Telecommunications and Finance Committees. The Assembly
    passed A3904B on May 11, 2022, and the Senate passed its companion, S5579A, by
    a vote of 61-0 on May 17, 2022, substituting in A3904B, which was then returned
    to and passed by the Assembly. Following delivery to the Governor on December
    12, 2022, Governor Kathy Hochul signed the bill into law on December 23, 2022
    as Chapter 743 of the Laws of 2022, enacting new statewide requirements for cyberattack
    response planning, vulnerability assessment, and continuous monitoring of energy
    infrastructure control systems.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Assembly Bill A3904, sponsored by Assemblymember Michael Cusick with cosponsors including Steven Englebright, Kenneth Zebrowski, Patricia Fahy, William Magnarelli, Fred Thiele, John T. McDonald III, Thomas Abinanti, Peter Abbate, and Andrew Hevesi, was introduced on January 28, 2021 and referred to the Assembly Energy Committee. The bill, which amends the Energy Law, Executive Law, and Public Service Law to strengthen protection of critical energy infrastructure from cyberattacks -- citing the 2015 Ukraine power grid hack and a 2017 breach at Fort Drum as justification -- was amended (print 3904a) and carried over into the 2022 session, where it was further amended (print 3904b) in April 2022 and reported to the Rules Committee in May 2022. A companion bill, S5579 (later S5579A), was introduced in the Senate on March 11, 2021 by Senator Kevin S. Parker and followed a parallel path through the Senate Energy and Telecommunications and Finance Committees. The Assembly passed A3904B on May 11, 2022, and the Senate passed its companion, S5579A, by a vote of 61-0 on May 17, 2022, substituting in A3904B, which was then returned to and passed by the Assembly. Following delivery to the Governor on December 12, 2022, Governor Kathy Hochul signed the bill into law on December 23, 2022 as Chapter 743 of the Laws of 2022, enacting new statewide requirements for cyberattack response planning, vulnerability assessment, and continuous monitoring of energy infrastructure control systems.
