## Summary

Signed as Chapter 67 of 2023, this law re-enacts and carries forward the critical energy infrastructure security and responsibility framework established in the 2021-22 session. It imposes security obligations on private operators of energy generation, transmission, and storage facilities in New York. The legislation serves as the durable statutory successor to the earlier approval-memo enactment, placing the requirements on firm legislative footing.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Assembly Bill A2896, sponsored by Assemblymember George Alvarez with Senate same-as bill S1345 sponsored by Sen. Kevin S. Parker, was introduced on January 31, 2023 and referred to the Assembly Committee on Energy. It advanced quickly: the Energy Committee reported it favorably 14-0 on February 13, 2023, the Rules Committee reported it favorably 26-0 on February 14, 2023, and it was ordered to third reading the same day. On February 15, 2023 the Assembly passed the bill 139-0; it was delivered to the Senate, where A2896 was substituted for S1345, passed the Senate 59-0 (with 4 excused), and was returned to the Assembly. The bill was delivered to Governor Kathy Hochul on March 3, 2023 and signed the same day as Chapter 67 of the Laws of 2023. As described in the sponsor's Memorandum in Support, A2896 is a chapter amendment making technical amendments to Chapter 743 of the Laws of 2022 -- the state's critical energy infrastructure security law -- refining definitions of 'critical energy infrastructure' and 'industrial control systems,' removing customer consumption data from those definitions while separately designating such data confidential, and adjusting Public Service Commission audit and advanced-metering-infrastructure provisions to ensure the law's protections for critical infrastructure and customer information are workable as intended.
