## Summary

New York S 2659 amends the state's data-breach notification law to require a business to notify affected New York residents of a data breach within 30 days of discovery, and expands the set of entities that must be notified. It imposes the duty on businesses that own or license New York residents' private information.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S2659, sponsored by Senator Leroy Comrie, was introduced in the New York Senate on January 24, 2023, and its same-as companion, A8872, was carried in the Assembly by Member of Assembly Nader Sayegh. The bill amends General Business Law 899-aa (part of the SHIELD Act) to require businesses to notify affected New York residents of a data breach within 30 days of its discovery and to add the Department of Financial Services to the list of state entities -- alongside the Attorney General, Department of State, and Division of State Police -- that must be notified. The Senate first passed the bill 60-1 on June 7, 2023, and passed it again 60-0 on June 3, 2024; the Assembly companion cleared the Consumer Affairs (16-0), Codes (21-0), and Rules (28-0) committees before the Assembly passed it 140-5 on June 6, 2024. After passage in both houses, the bill was delivered to Governor Kathy Hochul on December 12, 2024, and she signed it into law on December 21, 2024, as Chapter 647 of the Laws of 2024. While the 2024 Senate passage and the committee votes were unanimous, the 2023 Senate vote (60-1) and the 2024 Assembly vote (140-5) each drew recorded opposition; the bill nonetheless passed by wide margins reflecting broad consensus around strengthening the state's data breach notification regime by closing the gap between breach discovery and mandated public and regulatory notice.
