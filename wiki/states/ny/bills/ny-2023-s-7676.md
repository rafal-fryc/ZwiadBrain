## Summary

Establishes contract requirements for contracts involving the creation and use of digital replicas.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S 7676, sponsored by Senator Jessica Ramos, was introduced in the New York Senate on October 2, 2023, and referred to the Rules Committee, then referred to the Judiciary Committee on January 3, 2024; its Assembly same-as bill, A 8138, sponsored by Assemblymember Weinstein with co-sponsors Rosenthal, Bronson, and Bores, was introduced October 13, 2023, and followed a parallel path through the Assembly Judiciary and Ways and Means Committees. Both bills amend the General Obligations Law to void, as against public policy, any personal- or professional-services contract that allows creation and use of an individual's digital voice or likeness replica in place of the individual's own performance unless the contract reasonably specifically describes the intended uses and the individual was represented by legal counsel or a labor union at the time of the agreement. After amendment to its final print (S7676B/A8138B), the Senate passed the bill 61-0 on June 6, 2024, and the Assembly substituted and passed the Senate version on June 7, 2024. The bill was delivered to Governor Kathy Hochul on December 2, 2024, and she signed it into law as Chapter 581 of the Laws of 2024 on December 13, 2024, with an effective date of January 1, 2025. The measure responded to concerns, voiced in the sponsor's memorandum and echoed by SAG-AFTRA, about AI-driven digital-replica technology being used without performers' informed consent, and it sought to guarantee that any transfer of digital-replica rights be accompanied by specific contract disclosures and professional representation.
