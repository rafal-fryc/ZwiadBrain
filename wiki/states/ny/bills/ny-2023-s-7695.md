## Summary

New York S 7695 enacts the New York Child Data Protection Act, which restricts how operators of websites, online services, applications, and connected devices may collect, use, and disclose the personal data of minors (users under the age of 18) without informed consent, imposing children-privacy obligations on covered businesses.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S 7695-B, the New York Child Data Protection Act, was introduced by Senator Andrew Gounardes on October 13, 2023, and initially referred to the Senate Rules Committee before being rereferred to the Senate Internet and Technology Committee on January 3, 2024. The bill was amended to S 7695-A on March 12, 2024 and to S 7695-B on June 3, 2024, with the final version reported through the Rules Committee and passed by the Senate unanimously, 61–0, on June 6, 2024. The following day, June 7, 2024, the Assembly substituted S 7695-B for its companion measure A 8149 — sponsored by Assemblymember Nily Rozic — and passed it 144–0. Governor Kathy Hochul signed the legislation on June 20, 2024 as Chapter 121 of the Laws of 2024. The Act amends the General Business Law to prohibit online operators from collecting, using, sharing, or selling the personal data of any user they know to be under 18 except where strictly necessary to provide the service or where the user provides informed consent; for children under 13, the Act aligns with federal COPPA requirements. Enforcement authority rests with the New York Attorney General, who may seek civil penalties of up to $5,000 per violation, and the law takes effect one year after enactment.
