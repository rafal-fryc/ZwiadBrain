## Summary

New York's RAISE Act (Responsible AI Safety and Education Act), enacted in 2025, adds a new Article 44-B to the general business law regulating "large developers" of "frontier models" — AI models trained using more than 10^26 computational operations at a compute cost exceeding $100 million (or distilled models exceeding $5 million in compute cost), built by developers who have spent over $100 million aggregate on frontier-model training. Before deploying such a model, a large developer must implement and publish (with limited redactions for trade secrets, security, or legal confidentiality) a written safety and security protocol addressing risk of "critical harm" — death or injury to 100+ people or $1 billion+ in damage from a CBRN weapon or an autonomous AI act that would be a crime if done by a human — and must disclose safety incidents to the attorney general and the division of homeland security and emergency services within 72 hours of discovery. The law bars deployment that would create an unreasonable risk of critical harm and bars knowingly false or misleading statements in required disclosures, with enforcement solely by the state attorney general (civil penalties up to $10 million for a first violation, $30 million for subsequent violations; no private right of action). Unlike California's SB 53, which layers in additional transparency mechanisms and whistleblower protections, the RAISE Act as enacted centers on the safety-protocol/incident-disclosure/AG-enforcement structure described above.
