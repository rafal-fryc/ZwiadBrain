## Summary

New York's S8391, signed as Chapter 616 of 2025, amends Civil Rights Law §50-f, the state's post-mortem right-of-publicity statute for deceased performers and personalities. It redefines "digital replica" as a newly created, computer-generated, highly realistic representation that is readily identifiable as an individual's voice or visual likeness embodied in a sound recording, image, or audiovisual work, where either the individual did not actually perform/appear or the fundamental character of their actual performance was materially altered (excluding authorized sampling, remixing, or remastering). Most significantly, it removes the prior "likely to deceive the public" standard and its safe-harbor disclaimer defense for unauthorized digital replicas of deceased performers in audiovisual works, sound recordings, or live musical performances, replacing it with strict liability for any person who uses such a replica knowing it was unauthorized by the applicable right holder, without requiring proof of public deception. It also adds a notice-and-takedown protection for platforms distributing or licensing works containing an unauthorized digital replica, shielding them from liability unless they received notice from the rights successor and failed to remove the work as soon as technically and practically feasible.
