## Summary

Provides that data broker may not collect, sell or license brokered personal data within this state unless data broker first registers with Department of Consumer and Business Services. Provides that data broker may not collect, sell or license brokered personal data within this state unless data broker first registers with Department of Consumer and Business Services. Specifies form, method and contents of application. Specifies exemptions. Provides civil penalty in amount not to exceed $500 for each violation of Act or, for continuing violation, for each day in which violation continues. Caps amount of civil penalty at $10,000 in calendar year. Declares emergency, effective on passage.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

House Bill 2052 was introduced in the Oregon House on January 9, 2023, at the request of Attorney General Ellen Rosenblum, and referred to the House Committee on Business and Labor on January 11. Following a public hearing on January 18, the committee held a work session on February 6, 2023, and voted 10-1 to send the bill to the Joint Committee on Ways and Means with amendments printed as the A-Engrossed version, which added a definition of "license," extended the government exemption to public corporations, and aligned exemption language with the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act and Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act. After assignment to the Ways and Means Subcommittee on Transportation and Economic Development on May 22 and further work sessions on May 30 and June 2, the full Joint Committee on Ways and Means voted 22-0 on June 2, 2023 to recommend the B-Engrossed bill, along with a budget of $393,156 in Other Funds and two positions (1.76 FTE) for the Department of Consumer and Business Services to administer the new registry. The House passed the B-Engrossed bill on third reading on June 7, 2023 by a vote of 49-9, carried by Rep. Kevin Mannix, and the Senate concurred on third reading on June 22, 2023 by a vote of 23-1 (the lone Nay cast by Sen. Bonham), carried by Sen. Mark Meek. The Speaker and Senate President signed the enrolled bill on June 24, 2023, and Governor Tina Kotek signed it on July 27, 2023, enacting it as Chapter 395 of 2023 Oregon Laws with an emergency clause making it effective immediately upon signing. As enacted, the law prohibits a data broker from collecting, selling, or licensing brokered personal data within Oregon unless the data broker first registers with the Department of Consumer and Business Services, specifies the form, method, and contents of the registration application, sets out exemptions (including for consumer reporting agencies, financial institutions and their affiliates, and government entities and public corporations), and authorizes a civil penalty of up to $500 per violation — or per day for a continuing violation — capped at $10,000 in a calendar year.
