## Summary

Oregon's SB 619 creates a comprehensive consumer privacy law that applies to persons doing business in Oregon or serving its residents who, in a calendar year, control or process the personal data of 100,000 or more consumers (excluding data processed solely to complete a payment transaction), or the data of 25,000 or more consumers while deriving 25 percent or more of annual gross revenue from selling personal data. Consumers may confirm and obtain a copy of their data, correct it, delete it (including derived data), and opt out of targeted advertising, sale, and profiling for decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. Controllers must respond within 45 days (extendable by 45), run an appeal process, and give a privacy notice. They need consent to process sensitive data (which includes a child's data under 13, precise location within 1,750 feet, and genetic or biometric data) and to use data of consumers aged 13 to 15 for targeted advertising, sale, or profiling, and must conduct data protection assessments for high-risk processing. The Attorney General may serve investigative demands, and the law broadly exempts HIPAA, GLBA, and FCRA-regulated data, financial institutions, insurers, and public bodies.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

The Oregon Consumer Privacy Act, SB 619, grew out of a Consumer Privacy Task Force the Oregon Attorney General convened in 2019 to study standards for businesses handling consumers' online data, and was carried in the Senate by Judiciary Chair Sen. Floyd Prozanski. The Senate Committee on Judiciary held a public hearing on the bill on 2023-03-07 and, after a work session, voted Do Pass with amendments on 2023-04-03, referring the A-Engrossed measure to Ways and Means. The bill was assigned to the Ways and Means Subcommittee on Public Safety on 2023-05-22, which held work sessions on 2023-05-24 and 2023-05-31 before returning it to the floor as the B-Engrossed version. The full Senate passed SB 619 on Third Reading on 2023-06-20 by a vote of 23-2, with Sens. Bonham and Smith opposed, Prozanski carrying the bill. The House passed it unanimously, 54-0, on Third Reading on 2023-06-22, carried by Rep. Paul Holvey. The Senate President signed the bill on 2023-06-24 and the Speaker on 2023-06-25, and Governor Tina Kotek signed it into law on 2023-07-18 as Chapter 369, Oregon Laws 2023. As enacted, SB 619 gives Oregon consumers rights to confirm, access, correct, delete, and obtain a portable copy of their personal data, and to opt out of targeted advertising, sale of data, and certain profiling, while imposing privacy-notice, data-minimization, and risk-assessment duties on covered controllers and processors; the Oregon Attorney General is the exclusive enforcer, and coverage extends to some nonprofit organizations. The measure took effect 2024-01-01, with the nonprofit provisions delayed until 2025-07-01.
