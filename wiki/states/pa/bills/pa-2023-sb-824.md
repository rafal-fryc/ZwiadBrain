## Summary

Pennsylvania's SB 824 (enacted as Act 33 of 2024) amends the Breach of Personal Information Notification Act to add several new obligations for entities that suffer a data breach affecting Commonwealth residents. It requires notice to the Pennsylvania Attorney General, concurrent with resident notice, whenever a breach affects more than 500 Pennsylvania individuals, with the notice specifying the organization's name and location, the breach date, a summary of the incident, and the estimated number of individuals affected nationally and in Pennsylvania; entities already regulated under the state's Insurance Data Security Act (40 Pa.C.S. Ch. 45) are exempt from this Attorney General notice requirement. It also lowers the threshold for notifying nationwide consumer reporting agencies from more than 1,000 to more than 500 affected persons, updates the definition of "personal information," and adds a new Section 5.4 requiring an entity that must notify consumers of a breach involving a name combined with a Social Security number, driver's license/state ID number, or bank account number to provide, at no cost, one independent credit report and 12 months of credit monitoring (or notice of free monitoring availability) to affected individuals. The act took effect 90 days after signing.
