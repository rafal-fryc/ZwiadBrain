## Summary

Rhode Island H 7350 creates a new commercial-law chapter (title 6, chapter 63) regulating 'AI companion' systems -- those that retain memory of prior interactions, ask unprompted emotion-based questions, and sustain ongoing personal dialogue with a user (excluding pure customer-service, productivity, or internal business tools). It makes it unlawful for an 'operator' to provide an AI companion without a built-in protocol that detects a user's expressed suicidal ideation/self-harm or expressed intent to harm others and, when triggered, notifies the user and refers them to crisis services such as a suicide hotline or crisis text line; it also requires a clear notification, given at the start of an interaction and at least every three hours thereafter, that the user is not communicating with a human. Beginning July 1, 2027, operators must file annual reports with the attorney general on safety-protocol activations, which the AG's office will publish in aggregate. The attorney general enforces the chapter and may seek civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day (directed to suicide-prevention programs); the act takes effect January 1, 2027.
