## Summary

As enacted, defines and adds "voice" as a protected personal right; adds commercial availability of a sound recording or audiovisual work in which the individual's name, voice, likeness, or image is readily identifiable to considerations for determining whether non-use has occurred; makes other related changes. - Amends TCA Title 39, Chapter 14, Part 1 and Title 47.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB 2091, the ELVIS Act (Ensuring Likeness, Voice, and Image Security Act), was filed for introduction on January 24, 2024, by House Majority Leader William Lamberth and Rep. Mark Cochran, with Senate Majority Leader Jack Johnson carrying the companion SB 2096; the bill was a priority of Governor Bill Lee. After introduction and first consideration on January 25, the House referred the bill to the Commerce Committee on January 29, which assigned it to the Banking & Consumer Affairs Subcommittee on February 6. The subcommittee recommended the bill for passage on February 13, 2024, and the full Commerce Committee recommended it for passage if amended on February 27, sending it to Calendar & Rules, which placed it on the House Regular Calendar for March 4. On the House floor that day, members adopted Amendment HA0578 and then passed the bill as amended by a vote of 93 ayes to 0 nays. On March 7, 2024, the Senate substituted HB 2091 for its companion SB 2096, withdrew its own Senate Amendment SA0574, and passed HB 2091 by a vote of 30 ayes to 0 nays. The bill was signed by the Speaker of the House on March 14 and the Speaker of the Senate on March 18, transmitted to the Governor on March 19, and signed into law by Governor Bill Lee on March 21, 2024, becoming Public Chapter 588 (enacted March 26), with an effective date of July 1, 2024. As chaptered, the ELVIS Act amends Tennessee's Protection of Personal Rights law (TCA Title 47, ch. 25, Part 11) to add a person's 'voice' alongside name, photograph, and likeness as a protected personal right, and creates secondary liability for those who distribute or make available an algorithm, software, tool, or other technology whose primary purpose or function is the production of an individual's photograph, voice, or likeness without authorization — making Tennessee among the first states to extend right-of-publicity protection explicitly to AI voice cloning.
