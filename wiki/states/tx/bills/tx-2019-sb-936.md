## Summary

Texas SB 936 directs the Public Utility Commission of Texas, in coordination with ERCOT, to establish an independent cybersecurity monitor that oversees and coordinates cybersecurity efforts across electric utilities operating in the state. Participation is mandatory for utilities within the ERCOT footprint and available to certain utilities outside it. The law adds provisions to the Utilities Code imposing cybersecurity-program obligations on private electric-utility operators as critical infrastructure.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 936, by Senator Kelly Hancock, was filed in the Texas Senate on February 21, 2019, and referred to the Senate Business & Commerce Committee, which held hearings in March 2019 and reported the bill favorably without amendment on March 27, 2019 (8 ayes, 0 nays). The Senate passed the bill on April 4, 2019, and sent it to the House on April 5, where it was referred to the House State Affairs Committee under sponsor Representative Ana Hernandez. That committee adopted a committee substitute and reported it favorably on April 18, 2019 (9 ayes, 0 nays), and the full House passed the substituted bill on third reading on May 17, 2019. The Senate concurred in the House's changes on May 21, 2019, and the bill was signed in the Senate on May 23 and in the House on May 25 before being sent to the Governor. Governor Greg Abbott signed SB 936 into law on June 10, 2019, directing the Public Utility Commission of Texas to contract with an entity — administered through ERCOT — to serve as a cybersecurity monitor conducting outreach, reviewing utility self-assessments, and reporting on cybersecurity preparedness for participating electric utilities, including those outside the ERCOT power region; the Act took effect September 1, 2019. Bill analyses accompanying the legislation noted that, prior to the bill, individual utilities' cybersecurity efforts lacked coordinated oversight, and specified that the program conferred no new enforcement authority on the PUC while keeping shared cybersecurity information confidential and exempt from public disclosure.
