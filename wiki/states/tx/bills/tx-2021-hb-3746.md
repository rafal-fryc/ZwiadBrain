## Summary

Texas HB 3746 amends the Texas Identity Theft Enforcement and Protection Act to tighten breach-notification obligations for persons and businesses that own or license computerized personal data. Entities experiencing a breach affecting 250 or more Texans must now notify the Attorney General, and the AG is required to post those notifications publicly on its website.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

House Bill 3746, authored by Representative Giovanni Capriglione, was filed on March 11, 2021, during the 87th Texas Legislature, Regular Session, to amend the Texas Identity Theft Enforcement and Protection Act's data-breach notification provisions in Business & Commerce Code Chapter 521. The bill was referred to the House Business & Industry Committee, which heard it on April 13, 2021, and reported it favorably without amendments on April 14, 2021, by a 7-0 vote (with 2 members absent); the House passed the bill on April 29, 2021 after an amendment offered by Representative Stucky. In the Senate, sponsored by Senator Jane Nelson, the bill was heard by the Business & Commerce Committee on May 18 and 20, 2021, reported favorably without amendments on May 20, 2021, on a 9-0 vote, and the full Senate passed it on May 26, 2021. The bill requires that breach notifications sent to the Texas Attorney General for incidents affecting 250 or more Texas residents include additional detail, including the number of residents who have been notified, and directs the Attorney General to maintain a public, online listing of received breach notifications, updated within 30 days of receipt. HB 3746 was enrolled, sent to Governor Greg Abbott on May 31, 2021, and signed into law on June 14, 2021, taking effect September 1, 2021.
