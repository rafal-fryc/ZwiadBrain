## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 15, filed March 1, 2021 by Senator Robert Nichols, amends the Motor Vehicle Records Disclosure Act to restrict how Texas state agencies (chiefly the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles and Texas Parks and Wildlife Department) may disclose personal information tied to motor vehicle records, and to bar redisclosure or resale of that information to private marketing and technology companies, while adding criminal penalties (up to $100,000) and civil liability for violations. Despite its short title referencing the 'Texas Consumer Privacy Act Phase I,' the bill was handled throughout as a transportation-records privacy measure: it was approved unanimously by the Senate Transportation Committee (9-0) and passed the Senate on April 14, 2021. The House Transportation Committee, chaired by Representative Terry Canales, reported a substitute (C.S.S.B. 15) — with Representative Phil King serving as the bill's House sponsor (companion bill HB 3471) — that the House passed with amendments on May 25, 2021 (Record Vote #1423). Because of the House amendments, the bill went to a conference committee -- Senate conferees Nichols (chair), Blanco, Hancock, Nelson, and West, and House conferees King (chair), Canales, Perez, Rogers, and Thompson -- which reconciled the chambers' versions; both houses adopted the conference committee report on May 30, 2021. Governor Greg Abbott signed the bill on June 18, 2021, and it took effect immediately, completing a broadly bipartisan path from introduction to enactment in under four months.
