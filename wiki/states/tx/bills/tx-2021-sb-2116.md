## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S.B. 2116, the Lone Star Infrastructure Protection Act, was filed in the Texas Senate on March 12, 2021, by Sen. Donna Campbell (R–New Braunfels) with joint author Sen. Bob Hall and seven Senate cosponsors — Sens. Birdwell, Buckingham, Kolkhorst, Nelson, Paxton, Springer, and Taylor — prompted in significant part by public concern over a proposed Chinese-national-linked wind farm on land near Laughlin Air Force Base. The bill aimed to prohibit business entities and governmental bodies from entering contracts or agreements with companies owned or controlled by citizens or entities tied to China, Iran, North Korea, or Russia that would grant those companies direct or remote access to Texas critical infrastructure, defined to include the electric grid, water treatment facilities, communications systems, critical cyber infrastructure, and chemical facilities. The Senate Committee on Business & Commerce held a public hearing on April 20, 2021, took testimony, and reported a committee substitute favorably the same day; the full Senate suspended its three-day reading rule and passed C.S.S.B. 2116 unanimously, 31-0, on April 26, 2021. In the House, Rep. Tan Parker carried the bill; the Committee on State Affairs held a formal meeting on May 14, 2021, and reported the bill favorably as substituted. The House passed the measure on May 24, 2021, by a vote of 141-0 (Record Vote No. 1382) after adopting floor amendments by Rep. Parker and Rep. Cyrier. The Senate concurred in the House amendments on May 29, 2021, again by a vote of 31-0, and both chambers signed the enrolled bill on May 31. Governor Greg Abbott signed S.B. 2116 on June 18, 2021, enacting it as Acts 2021, 87th R.S., ch. 975, with immediate effect.
