## Summary

This law addresses electric grid security on two fronts. It requires ERCOT to identify employee positions critical to grid security and obtain criminal history records before filling those roles. It also adds Utilities Code section 39.360, under which market participants must attest that their purchases of critical electric grid equipment or services do not involve covered foreign entities with ties to China, Russia, Iran, or North Korea, and must report any such purchases to prevent those entities from gaining access to or control of critical grid infrastructure.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 2013, which amends the Lone Star Infrastructure Protection Act (enacted in 2021 as SB 2116), was filed in the Texas Senate by Senator Charles Schwertner (joined by Senate coauthors including Senator Phil King (SD-10), Creighton, Kolkhorst, Parker, and Angela Paxton) and carried in the House by Rep. Todd Hunter; it had an identical House companion, HB 4438. The bill was received by the Senate on March 9, 2023, referred to the Senate Business & Commerce Committee, which reported a committee substitute out on an 11-0 vote, and the Senate passed the bill on April 4, 2023. In the House, the bill was reported favorably by the State Affairs Committee (12-0-0-1) with its own committee substitute, and the House passed the amended bill on May 23, 2023. The Senate concurred in the House amendments on May 25, 2023, sending the bill to the Governor. As enacted, SB 2013 amends the Lone Star Infrastructure Protection Act and related Utilities Code provisions governing the electric grid. It adds a definition of "affiliate"; authorizes the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) to obtain criminal-history record information and conduct background checks for positions critical to grid security; requires entities registered with ERCOT to file attestations of compliance with Chapter 113; requires reporting and attestation regarding purchases of critical grid equipment from companies owned or controlled by, or headquartered in, designated countries (China, Iran, North Korea, or Russia), with a five-year lookback; and authorizes suspension of registration and audits by the Attorney General. Governor Greg Abbott signed the bill into law on June 9, 2023, and it took effect immediately upon signing.
