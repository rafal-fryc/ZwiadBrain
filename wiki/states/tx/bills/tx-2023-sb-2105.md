## Summary

Texas's data-broker law requires data brokers to register with the Secretary of State and pay authorized registration fees. Registered brokers must meet notice and duty requirements, including maintaining a comprehensive information security program. Civil penalties apply for violations of the registration and compliance obligations.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 2105, authored by Senator Nathan Johnson (D-Dallas) with co-authors Senator Lois Kolkhorst and Senator José Menéndez and House sponsor Representative Justin Holland, was filed on March 9, 2023, in the 88th Texas Legislature's Regular Session. Referred to the Senate Committee on Business & Commerce, the bill received a public hearing on April 18, 2023, where proponents from the Texas Council on Family Violence, Texas Appleseed, and the Texas Public Policy Foundation testified in support, and representatives of RELX and Texas For Liberty testified on the bill. On April 25, the committee adopted a committee substitute — removing the consumer opt-out registry from the introduced version while adding comprehensive information-security program requirements — and voted favorably 11-0, recommending the bill for the local and uncontested calendar; the committee report was filed April 28. The Senate passed SB 2105 unanimously on May 3, 2023, suspending the three-day rule and advancing the bill directly to final passage. In the House, the bill was referred to the Committee on Business & Industry, which met in formal session on May 17 and recommended it favorably without amendments (9-0); the committee report was distributed May 20. The full House passed SB 2105 on the local and consent calendar on May 24 (Roll Vote 2039), and Governor Greg Abbott signed the bill on June 18, 2023. The act took effect September 1, 2023, establishing Chapter 509 of the Texas Business & Commerce Code and making Texas the third state — after Vermont and California — to require annual registration of data brokers with a state authority, impose a $300 annual registration fee payable to the Secretary of State, and authorize the Attorney General to pursue civil penalties of up to $10,000 per 12-month period for non-compliance.
