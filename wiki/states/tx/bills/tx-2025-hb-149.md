  narrative: Texas HB 149, the Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance
    Act (TRAIGA), was introduced by Rep. Giovanni Capriglione (R-Southlake) on March
    14, 2025, drawing on findings of the House Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence
    & Emerging Technologies that unchecked AI systems could compromise data privacy,
    perpetuate bias, and harm consumers. The House Committee on Delivery of Government
    Efficiency — chaired by Capriglione himself — heard public testimony on March
    26, 2025, and reported a committee substitute favorably on April 2 by a vote of
    13–0. The full House passed the bill on second reading on April 22 (137–10, Record
    322, with a constitutional-rights amendment by Capriglione) and on third reading
    on April 23 (146–3, Record 376). The Senate Committee on Business & Commerce held
    its hearing on May 1, 2025, voted favorably 11–0 on May 20–21, and Senate sponsor
    Sen. Charles Schwertner (R-Georgetown) brought CSHB 149 to the floor on May 23,
    where three floor amendments — adding a constitutional non-expansion clause, excluding
    hospital districts and higher-education institutions from the term "governmental
    entity," and providing a funding-contingency deferral mechanism for state agencies
    — were adopted without objection and the bill passed unanimously 31–0. The House
    concurred in the Senate amendments on May 30 (121–17, Record 4059), the bill was
    signed by both chambers on June 1, presented to Governor Greg Abbott on June 2,
    and signed into law on June 22, 2025, with an effective date of January 1, 2026.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Texas HB 149, the Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (TRAIGA), was introduced by Rep. Giovanni Capriglione (R-Southlake) on March 14, 2025, drawing on findings of the House Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence & Emerging Technologies that unchecked AI systems could compromise data privacy, perpetuate bias, and harm consumers. The House Committee on Delivery of Government Efficiency — chaired by Capriglione himself — heard public testimony on March 26, 2025, and reported a committee substitute favorably on April 2 by a vote of 13–0. The full House passed the bill on second reading on April 22 (137–10, Record 322, with a constitutional-rights amendment by Capriglione) and on third reading on April 23 (146–3, Record 376). The Senate Committee on Business & Commerce held its hearing on May 1, 2025, voted favorably 11–0 on May 20–21, and Senate sponsor Sen. Charles Schwertner (R-Georgetown) brought CSHB 149 to the floor on May 23, where three floor amendments — adding a constitutional non-expansion clause, excluding hospital districts and higher-education institutions from the term "governmental entity," and providing a funding-contingency deferral mechanism for state agencies — were adopted without objection and the bill passed unanimously 31–0. The House concurred in the Senate amendments on May 30 (121–17, Record 4059), the bill was signed by both chambers on June 1, presented to Governor Greg Abbott on June 2, and signed into law on June 22, 2025, with an effective date of January 1, 2026.
