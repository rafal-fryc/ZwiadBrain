  narrative: 'HB 150, filed by Rep. Giovanni Capriglione on March 11, 2025 with numerous
    House joint/co-authors (Bonnen, Hefner, Lujan, R. Lopez, Parker, Campos, C. Harris,
    Harris Davila, Lalani, J. Lopez, Morales Shaw, Plesa, and Raymond), creates the
    Texas Cyber Command and transfers certain cybersecurity powers and duties from
    the Department of Information Resources to the new agency. The House Delivery
    of Government Efficiency Committee held a public hearing on March 19, 2025, and
    reported the bill favorably as substituted on March 27, 2025. On the House floor
    on April 15, 2025, members engaged in an extended amendment fight: a Campos amendment
    was adopted and the bill passed second reading 136-9 (Record Vote #185); a Hopper
    amendment was tabled 117-27 (Record Vote #186); a Rule 11, Section 2 motion passed
    89-54 (Record Vote #187); a further procedural vote passed 132-9 (Record Vote
    #188); and the bill was passed to engrossment as amended, 133-12 (Record Vote
    #189). The House gave the bill final passage on third reading on April 16, 2025
    by a vote of 130-13 (Record Vote #215). The Senate received the engrossed bill
    and referred it to the Business and Commerce Committee, which held a hearing on
    May 20, 2025 and reported the bill favorably as substituted on May 27, 2025. That
    same day, the Senate suspended its three-day rule, adopted Sen. Tan Parker''s
    floor amendment (FA1), and passed the bill 31-0. The House then concurred in the
    Senate amendments on May 29, 2025 by a vote of 115-21 (Record Vote #4057). The
    bill was enrolled on May 30, 2025, signed in the House on May 31 and in the Senate
    on June 1, sent to Governor Greg Abbott on June 1, and signed into law on June
    2, 2025, with an effective date of September 1, 2025. Notably, the bill was substantially
    reworked between chambers: earlier committee-substitute and engrossed versions
    structured the Command as a University of Texas System component institution administratively
    attached to the University of Texas at San Antonio, but that structure did not
    survive into the enacted law. As enacted, HB 150 establishes the Texas Cyber Command
    as its own state agency (Sec. 2063.002(a)), led by a chief appointed by the Governor
    and confirmed by the Senate, and organized around a Cybersecurity Threat Intelligence
    Center, a Cybersecurity Incident Response Unit, and a Digital Forensics Laboratory;
    the law authorizes an interagency agreement for a San Antonio facility with a
    sensitive compartmented information facility, and requires the Department of Information
    Resources to transfer its cybersecurity-related functions to the Command by December
    31, 2026.'

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB 150, filed by Rep. Giovanni Capriglione on March 11, 2025 with numerous House joint/co-authors (Bonnen, Hefner, Lujan, R. Lopez, Parker, Campos, C. Harris, Harris Davila, Lalani, J. Lopez, Morales Shaw, Plesa, and Raymond), creates the Texas Cyber Command and transfers certain cybersecurity powers and duties from the Department of Information Resources to the new agency. The House Delivery of Government Efficiency Committee held a public hearing on March 19, 2025, and reported the bill favorably as substituted on March 27, 2025. On the House floor on April 15, 2025, members engaged in an extended amendment fight: a Campos amendment was adopted and the bill passed second reading 136-9 (Record Vote #185); a Hopper amendment was tabled 117-27 (Record Vote #186); a Rule 11, Section 2 motion passed 89-54 (Record Vote #187); a further procedural vote passed 132-9 (Record Vote #188); and the bill was passed to engrossment as amended, 133-12 (Record Vote #189). The House gave the bill final passage on third reading on April 16, 2025 by a vote of 130-13 (Record Vote #215). The Senate received the engrossed bill and referred it to the Business and Commerce Committee, which held a hearing on May 20, 2025 and reported the bill favorably as substituted on May 27, 2025. That same day, the Senate suspended its three-day rule, adopted Sen. Tan Parker's floor amendment (FA1), and passed the bill 31-0. The House then concurred in the Senate amendments on May 29, 2025 by a vote of 115-21 (Record Vote #4057). The bill was enrolled on May 30, 2025, signed in the House on May 31 and in the Senate on June 1, sent to Governor Greg Abbott on June 1, and signed into law on June 2, 2025, with an effective date of September 1, 2025. Notably, the bill was substantially reworked between chambers: earlier committee-substitute and engrossed versions structured the Command as a University of Texas System component institution administratively attached to the University of Texas at San Antonio, but that structure did not survive into the enacted law. As enacted, HB 150 establishes the Texas Cyber Command as its own state agency (Sec. 2063.002(a)), led by a chief appointed by the Governor and confirmed by the Senate, and organized around a Cybersecurity Threat Intelligence Center, a Cybersecurity Incident Response Unit, and a Digital Forensics Laboratory; the law authorizes an interagency agreement for a San Antonio facility with a sensitive compartmented information facility, and requires the Department of Information Resources to transfer its cybersecurity-related functions to the Command by December 31, 2026.
