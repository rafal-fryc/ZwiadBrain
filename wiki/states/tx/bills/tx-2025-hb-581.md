## Summary

Texas HB 581 extends the state's age-verification law for sexual material harmful to minors (Civil Practice and Remedies Code ch. 129B) to AI-generated content. It defines "artificial sexual material harmful to minors" as computer-generated sexual material harmful to minors that was produced, adapted, or modified with an AI application or other software, and in which a person is recognizable as an actual person by face, likeness, or another distinguishing characteristic. A commercial entity that runs a website with a publicly accessible tool for creating such material, or otherwise makes an application for creating it publicly available, must use reasonable age verification to confirm users are 18 or older. The verification must use digital identification, government-issued ID, or a commercially reasonable method based on public or private transactional data, and neither the entity nor a third-party verifier may retain identifying information. These entities must also ensure that any individual used as a source for the material is 18 or older and has consented to the use of their face and body.

An entity is exempt from both requirements if its terms or use policies prohibit generating such material and must be acknowledged before access, and it takes affirmative technical steps to limit creation, such as filtering, reporting tools, or cleaning training datasets. Civil penalties for violations can reach $10,000 per day of noncompliant operation, $10,000 per instance of retaining identifying information, and an additional $250,000 if a minor accesses sexual material harmful to minors because of the violation. Internet service providers, search engines, and cloud providers are not liable solely for providing access to content they did not create. The act took effect September 1, 2025.
