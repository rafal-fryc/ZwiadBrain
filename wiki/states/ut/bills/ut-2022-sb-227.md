## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S.B. 227, the Consumer Privacy Act, was introduced by Sen. Kirk A. Cullimore in the Utah Senate on February 17, 2022, and referred to the Senate Revenue and Taxation Standing Committee. On February 23, 2022, that committee replaced the bill with 1st Substitute S.B. 227 by a 9-0 vote and then advanced it with a favorable recommendation by a 6-0 vote, after testimony from industry representatives including the Utah Retail Merchants Association, Salt Lake Chamber, Microsoft, and TechNet in support, and Consumer Reports in opposition. The full Senate passed the bill on second reading 28-0 on February 24, 2022, and on third reading 28-0 on February 25, 2022, after substituting in the 2nd Substitute version, before transmitting it to the House. The House Transportation Standing Committee, with House floor sponsor Rep. Brady Brammer's chamber handling the bill, took it up on March 1, 2022, adopting Amendment #1 by an 11-0 vote and then voting 12-0 to send 2nd Substitute S.B. 227 to the House floor with a favorable recommendation, over testimony from Libertas Institute and Consumer Reports in opposition. The full House passed the bill on third reading by a vote of 71-0 on March 2, 2022. The Senate concurred with the House amendment on March 3, 2022, by a vote of 25-0, and the bill was signed by the Speaker and President and sent for enrolling; the enrolled bill was returned on March 11, 2022, and transmitted to Governor Spencer Cox on March 15, 2022. Governor Cox signed the Consumer Privacy Act into law on March 24, 2022, making Utah the fourth state to enact a comprehensive consumer privacy statute, with the law's substantive provisions taking effect December 31, 2023.
