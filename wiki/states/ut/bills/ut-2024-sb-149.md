## Summary

Utah SB 149 (effective May 1, 2024) creates the Artificial Intelligence Policy Act. Using generative AI is not a defense to violating any consumer-protection statute the Division of Consumer Protection administers, and a person who causes generative AI to interact with someone in such a transaction must clearly and conspicuously disclose that it is AI if asked or prompted. Providers of services in a regulated occupation (one requiring a state license or certification) must prominently disclose AI interaction, verbally at the start of an oral exchange and by electronic message before a written one. The division can impose administrative fines up to $2,500 per violation and seek injunctions and disgorgement in court. The act also creates the Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy and an AI Learning Laboratory Program offering temporary regulatory mitigation agreements (initially up to 12 months, with one 12-month extension), and makes committing an offense with the aid of generative AI a basis for guilt. The enrolled text repeals the AI chapter on May 1, 2025.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S.B. 149, the Artificial Intelligence Policy Act, was introduced in the Utah Senate on January 25, 2024, by Sen. Kirk A. Cullimore, with Rep. Jefferson Moss as House sponsor. Assigned to the Senate Business and Labor Committee, the bill was amended (Amendment #1) and given a favorable recommendation on February 8, 2024, then passed the Senate on 2nd reading 22-0 on February 12 and 3rd reading 26-0 on February 13, sending it to the House. There the House Economic Development and Workforce Services Committee recommended it favorably on February 20, and the full House passed it 73-0 on 3rd reading on February 28, 2024, without further amendment, so the bill went directly to enrolling. Following enrollment on March 8 and transmittal to the Governor on March 12, Governor Spencer Cox signed S.B. 149 into law on March 13, 2024, with an effective date of May 1, 2024. As enacted, the law creates a new Artificial Intelligence Policy Act in the Utah Code establishing liability under existing consumer-protection statutes for undisclosed use of generative AI, requiring that a person disclose that a consumer is interacting with generative AI if asked, and imposing prominent, up-front disclosure duties on regulated occupations; it also creates the Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy within the Department of Commerce and establishes the Artificial Intelligence Learning Laboratory Program, a regulatory sandbox allowing temporary, negotiated regulatory mitigation agreements for AI pilot testing. The bill drew unanimous or near-unanimous support at every recorded vote, with committee hearings featuring testimony from Department of Commerce officials and a range of AI-industry witnesses in favor of the measure.
