## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S.B. 215, "Motor Vehicle Consumer Data Protection," was introduced by Sen. Chris H. Wilson (with Rep. Steve Eliason as House sponsor) and numbered and distributed on February 8, 2024, before being formally introduced in the Senate on February 9. Referred to the Senate Transportation, Public Utilities, Energy, and Technology (TPUET) Committee on February 12, the bill received a favorable recommendation by a 6-0 vote on February 13, and the committee's favorable report was placed on the second reading calendar on February 14. On February 16, the Senate substituted the bill with the 1st Substitute (S01) on second reading and passed it 19-0; Senator Wilson's substitute made only minor technical and clarifying changes to the introduced text — including a housekeeping cross-reference fix in the "consumer data" definition, a restructured (but substantively unchanged) definition of "protected dealer data," a minor wording tweak to the "required manufacturer data" marketing-purposes clause, and renumbered internal subsection cross-references in Section 13-70-202 — without altering the bill's core framework. The Senate passed the substituted bill on third reading by a vote of 22-0 on February 20 and transmitted it to the House, where it was referred to the House Transportation Committee on February 21. That committee gave the bill a favorable recommendation by an 11-0 vote on February 22, the same day the bill was placed on the House second reading calendar; the House then passed the bill on third reading 67-0 on February 29. Following enrollment (the bill was signed by the Speaker and President and sent for enrolling on March 1, and transmitted to the Governor on March 12), Governor Spencer Cox signed S.B. 215 into law on March 13, 2024. As enacted, the bill creates a new Utah Code Chapter 70 in Title 13 (Sections 13-70-101 through 13-70-203) establishing a "Motor Vehicle Consumer Data Protection" framework governing "protected dealer data" held in a franchised dealership's dealer data system: it bars a franchisor or third party from requiring direct or indirect access to a franchisee's dealer data system, prohibits accessing, sharing, selling, copying, using, or transmitting protected dealer data without the franchisee's "prior express written consent," bans "cyber ransom" tactics and unreasonable fees or restrictions on a franchisee's or authorized integrator's access to that data, sets requirements for service-provider and vendor contracts (including audit rights and 12-month compliance testing), allows a franchisee to revoke consent on 60 days' notice (or immediately for cause), requires indemnification for violations, and obligates dealer data vendors to provide standardized, STAR-compliant integration frameworks for authorized integrators, including a secure data-transition process if a franchisee terminates an agreement on 90 days' notice. The act carves out required manufacturer data (e.g., safety/recall notices, warranty transactions, and vehicle-diagnostic data needed for legally required purposes) from these restrictions and expressly does not extend to data generated by a vehicle or a consumer's connected device outside the dealer data system. The enrolled act contained no special effective-date clause, so under Utah's default rule (60 days after the Legislature's adjournment sine die) the law took effect May 1, 2024.
