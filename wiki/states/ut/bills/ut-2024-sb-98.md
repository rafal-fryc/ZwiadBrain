## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S.B. 98, Online Data Security and Privacy Amendments, was numbered and distributed on January 15, 2024, and introduced in the Utah Senate on January 16, 2024, by Sen. Wayne A. Harper, with Rep. Jefferson S. Burton later joining as House sponsor. Referred to the Senate Transportation, Public Utilities, Energy, and Technology Committee on January 17, the bill was substituted (adopting a 2nd substitute) and given a favorable recommendation on January 23, 2024, both by 3-0 committee votes; the Senate substituted the bill on January 24. The Senate passed the bill on second reading with floor amendments on January 30 (27-0) and on third reading on January 31 (29-0), sending it to the House. After committee referral to House Government Operations on February 9, that committee adopted a 3rd substitute, an amendment, and a favorable recommendation on February 13, 2024, each by an 8-0 vote; the House then passed the bill on third reading February 14, 2024 by a 71-0 vote. The Senate concurred with the House amendment on February 16, 2024 by a 23-0 vote, clearing the bill for enrollment. Governor Spencer Cox signed the enrolled bill on March 19, 2024, and it took effect May 1, 2024. As enacted, S.B. 98 amends the Protection of Personal Information Act (Utah Code 13-44-202) and the Utah Technology Governance Act, renumbering and updating the Utah Cyber Center statutes (new Part 11, Sections 63A-16-1101 to -1103) that were originally created the prior year by Laws of Utah 2023, Chapter 496 (S.B. 127). The bill's own Attorney General/Cyber Center notification duty for breaches affecting 500 or more Utah residents was already existing law carried forward from that 2023 act; S.B. 98's changes itemize what must be included in those notifications (the breach and discovery dates, total people and Utah residents affected, the type of personal information involved, and a short description of the breach), restructure the confidentiality provision so that AG/Cyber Center notifications and Cyber Center work product may be deemed confidential protected records under the Government Records Access and Management Act when statutory conditions are met, add parallel reporting duties for governmental entities reporting data breaches to the Cyber Center (including mitigation steps taken), and separately require governmental entities to use authorized .gov/.edu/.mil domain extensions for websites and email beginning July 1, 2025. The measure moved through three substitutes (S1, S2, S3) plus Senate and House floor amendments, but drew no recorded opposition at any stage, passing every committee and floor vote unanimously.
