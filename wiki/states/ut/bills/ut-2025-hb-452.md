## Summary

Utah's HB 452 enacts a new Chapter 72a regulating AI-powered mental health chatbots, defined as generative-AI systems that engage users in conversations similar to a licensed therapist's confidential communications and that suppliers represent (or a reasonable person would believe) can provide mental health therapy. Suppliers are barred from selling or sharing a Utah user's individually identifiable health information or chatbot input with third parties (with narrow exceptions), from using user input to target or customize advertising, and must clearly disclose that the chatbot is AI and not human before use and periodically thereafter. The Division of Consumer Protection enforces the law with administrative fines up to $2,500 per violation and court-ordered penalties up to $5,000 per violation of an order, while suppliers who file a compliant written safety/privacy policy with the division gain an affirmative defense to certain unprofessional-conduct claims; the law took effect May 7, 2025.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

H.B. 452, "Artificial Intelligence Amendments," was introduced in the Utah House on February 7, 2025 by Rep. Jefferson Moss, with Sen. Kirk Cullimore as Senate sponsor, to regulate AI-powered mental health chatbots under a new Utah Code Chapter 13-72a. The bill was assigned to the House Economic Development and Workforce Services Committee, which adopted a substitute and then gave it a favorable recommendation by votes of 9-0 (1 absent) on February 25, 2025; the House then passed the substituted bill on third reading 70-0 (5 absent) on February 27, 2025, sending it to the Senate. In the Senate, the Health and Human Services Committee gave the bill a favorable recommendation 5-0 (2 absent) on March 3, 2025, and the full Senate passed it on second and third readings by suspension of the rules, 28-0 (1 absent), on March 5, 2025. With no amendments differing between the chambers, the bill was signed by the House Speaker and enrolled in early-to-mid March before being transmitted to Governor Spencer Cox, who signed it into law in March 2025. As enacted, H.B. 452 bars mental-health-chatbot suppliers from selling or sharing a user's individually identifiable health information or chatbot input, restricts using that input for ad targeting, requires clear AI-not-human disclosures before and during use, gives the Division of Consumer Protection enforcement authority (fines up to $2,500 per violation), and affords suppliers an affirmative defense for filing a compliant safety and privacy policy; the law took effect May 7, 2025.
