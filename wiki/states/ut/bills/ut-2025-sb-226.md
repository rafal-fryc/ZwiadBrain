## Summary

Utah's SB 226 enacts a new Chapter 75 governing generative AI used in consumer transactions and regulated occupations, requiring suppliers to disclose when a consumer is interacting with generative AI (rather than a human) if the consumer asks, and requiring individuals in state-licensed or certified occupations to proactively and prominently disclose AI use when it constitutes a "high-risk artificial intelligence interaction" (e.g., involving health, financial, or biometric data, or consequential advice). It also clarifies that using generative AI is not a defense to violations of consumer protection laws the Division of Consumer Protection administers, creates a safe harbor for suppliers who clearly and conspicuously disclose AI use throughout an interaction, and sets enforcement penalties of up to $2,500 per violation administratively and $5,000 per violation of a court/administrative order. The bill also extends the repeal date of the Artificial Intelligence Policy Act to July 1, 2027, and took effect May 7, 2025.
