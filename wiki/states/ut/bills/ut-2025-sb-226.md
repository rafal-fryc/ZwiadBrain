## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

S.B. 226, the Artificial Intelligence Consumer Protection Amendments, was introduced in the Utah Senate on February 4, 2025 by Sen. Kirk A. Cullimore, with Rep. Jefferson Moss carrying it in the House. The bill moved to the Senate Business and Labor Committee, which on February 14, 2025 adopted a substitute (5-0-3) and then gave a favorable recommendation (5-0-3), sending the substituted bill to the Senate floor. The Senate passed it on second reading 22-0-7 on February 21, 2025, and on third reading 24-0-5 on February 24, 2025, before transmitting it to the House. In the House, the Economic Development and Workforce Services Committee gave S.B. 226 a favorable recommendation by a vote of 7-0-3 on March 4, 2025, and the full House passed the bill on third reading 69-0-6 on March 7, 2025. After enrolling, Governor Spencer Cox signed the bill into law on March 27, 2025 as Chapter 465, Laws of Utah 2025, with the new Title 13, Chapter 75 disclosure and liability provisions, the repeal of Section 13-2-12, and the extension of the Artificial Intelligence Policy Act's repeal date to July 1, 2027 all taking effect May 7, 2025. The bill passed both chambers without a single recorded 'no' vote at any stage, reflecting broad bipartisan support for the consumer-disclosure and safe-harbor framework it created for generative AI use in consumer transactions and regulated occupations.
