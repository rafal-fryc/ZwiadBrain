## Summary

Utah HB 276 enacts two new chapters of the Utah Code effective January 1, 2027. The Digital Voyeurism Prevention Act (13-72b) bars generation services and covered platforms from distributing counterfeit intimate images without obtaining and verifying the depicted individual's consent, requires platforms to run notice-and-takedown procedures and disclosures, and creates civil liability for actual and punitive damages, attorney fees, and injunctive relief — with a safe harbour for services that implement reasonable safeguards and respond to notices, and a heightened pleading standard requiring plaintiffs to plead specific facts. The digital content provenance chapter (13-72c) requires large online platforms to detect, disclose, and preserve provenance data in distributed content and bars them from knowingly stripping it where technically feasible; requires capture device manufacturers to embed a latent disclosure identifying the manufacturer or device type; and requires covered providers to include a latent disclosure in image, video, and audio content produced by their generative AI systems. The Division of Consumer Protection administers and enforces the chapters, with administrative fines up to $2,500 per violation and court-imposed civil penalties up to $5,000 per violation.
