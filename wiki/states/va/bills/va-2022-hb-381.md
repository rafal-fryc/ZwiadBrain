## Summary

Consumer Data Protection Act; data deletion request. Provides that a controller that has obtained personal data about a consumer from a third party shall be deemed in compliance with a consumer's request to delete such data if the controller either (i) retains a record of the deletion request and the minimum data necessary for the purpose of ensuring that the consumer's personal data remains deleted and does not use such retained data for any other purpose or (ii) opts the consumer out of the processing of that data for any purpose except those purposes exempted pursuant to the Consumer Data Protection Act.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB 381 was prefiled by Del. Glenn Davis on 2022-01-11 and referred to the House Committee on General Laws, which assigned it to Subcommittee #1 on 2022-01-25; the subcommittee recommended reporting the bill by an 8-0 vote on 2022-01-27, and the full committee reported it favorably 21-0 on 2022-02-03. After engrossment, the House passed the bill on a block vote of 99-0 on 2022-02-09 and transmitted it to the Senate, where it was referred to the Committee on General Laws and Technology on 2022-02-10. That committee reported the bill with a substitute (printed 22106693D-S1) by a vote of 13-0 on 2022-02-16; the Senate dispensed with a constitutional reading 38-0 on 2022-02-18 and passed the bill with its substitute 40-0 on 2022-02-21. The House agreed to the Senate substitute 99-0 on 2022-02-23, and the bill was enrolled and signed by the presiding officers on 2022-02-25 before being communicated to the Governor on 2022-03-11. Governor Glenn Youngkin approved the bill on 2022-04-11 as Chapter 423 of the 2022 Acts of Assembly, with an effective date of 2022-07-01. As enacted through the Senate committee substitute, HB 381 amends the right-to-delete provision of the 2021 Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act (Va. Code § 59.1-577(B)) to add an alternative compliance pathway for controllers that obtained a consumer's personal data from a source other than the consumer: such a controller is deemed to comply with a deletion request by either (i) retaining a record of the deletion request together with the minimum data necessary to ensure the consumer's personal data remains deleted and using that retained data for no other purpose, or (ii) opting the consumer out of the processing of that data for any purpose other than one exempted under the Act. The bill moved in tandem with an identical Senate companion, SB 393 (patron Sen. Adam P. Ebbin), and passed both chambers of the General Assembly without a single dissenting vote at any recorded stage.
