## Summary

Consumer Data Protection Act; enforcement; Consumer Privacy Fund. Authorizes the Attorney General to pursue actual damages to consumers to the extent they exist if a controller or processor of the personal data of Virginians continues to violate the Consumer Data Protection Act (the Act) following a 30-day cure period offered by the Attorney General or breaches an express written statement provided to the Attorney General. Political organizations and certain § 501(c)(4) organizations are classified as nonprofit organizations and thus exempt from the Act. In addition, the bill abolishes the Consumer Privacy Fund and provides that all civil penalties, expenses, and attorney fees collected from enforcement of the Act shall be deposited into the Regulatory, Consumer Advocacy, Litigation, and Enforcement Revolving Trust Fund.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

HB 714, chief-patroned by Del. C.E. "Cliff" Hayes Jr. with Del. Israel O'Quinn as co-patron, was prefiled and referred to the House Committee on Commerce and Energy on January 11, 2022. A House subcommittee recommended reporting the bill with a substitute by an 8-0 vote on February 1, 2022, and the full Commerce and Energy Committee reported it with that substitute (printed as 22105707D-H1) on February 10, 2022 by a 22-0 vote. The House engrossed the substitute and passed the bill on a block vote, 100-0, on February 15, 2022, before transmitting it to the Senate, where it was referred to the Committee on General Laws and Technology. That committee reported the bill 14-0 on February 23, 2022 and rereferred it to the Committee on Finance and Appropriations, which reported it 13-1 on March 1, 2022. The Senate then dispensed with the constitutional reading and passed the bill 40-0 on March 2, 2022. The bill was enrolled on March 7, 2022, signed by the Speaker and Senate President on March 8, communicated to Governor Glenn Youngkin on March 22, and approved by the Governor on April 11, 2022 as Chapter 451, with the amendments taking effect July 1, 2022. As enacted, HB 714 amends the 2021 Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act to authorize the Attorney General to pursue actual damages for aggrieved consumers where a controller or processor continues violating the Act after the Attorney General's existing 30-day cure-notice period (a mechanism the 2021 VCDPA already established and which HB 714 left in place) or breaches an express written statement of cure given to the Attorney General; to classify political organizations and certain organizations exempt under IRC § 501(c)(4) as exempt nonprofit organizations under the Act; and to repeal § 59.1-585, abolishing the Consumer Privacy Fund, with civil penalties, expenses, and attorney fees collected under the Act redirected instead to the Regulatory, Consumer Advocacy, Litigation, and Enforcement Revolving Trust Fund. HB 714 moved through the General Assembly alongside an identical Senate companion, SB 534 (patroned by Sen. David W. Marsden), which the Governor approved the same day as Chapter 452.
