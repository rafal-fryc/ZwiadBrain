## Summary

Genetic data privacy; civil penalty. Establishes requirements for direct-to-consumer genetic testing companies, defined in the bill, including requirements related to information to be provided to consumers, express consent requirements, requirements related to security of and consumer access to genetic data, requirements for contracts between direct-to-consumer genetic testing companies and service providers, and prohibitions on disclosure of genetic data by direct-to-consumer genetic testing companies. The bill also prohibits discrimination against a consumer based on exercise of rights related to genetic data privacy and imposes civil penalties for violations of the provisions of the bill.

## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

SB 1087, patroned by Senator Adam P. Ebbin, was prefiled and referred to the Senate Committee on General Laws and Technology on January 9, 2023. The committee reported the bill with amendments on February 1, 2023, by a vote of 14-0, and the full Senate passed it on February 6, 2023, by a vote of 39-0. The bill then crossed to the House, where it was referred to the Committee on Commerce and Energy; Subcommittee #1 recommended reporting it with a substitute on February 14, 2023 (7-0), and the full committee reported the substitute on February 16, 2023, by a vote of 22-0. The House passed the bill with the substitute by block vote, 99-0, on February 21, 2023. The Senate agreed to the House substitute on February 22, 2023, by a vote of 40-0, resolving the two chambers' versions without need for a conference. The bill was enrolled on March 7, 2023, and Governor Glenn Youngkin approved it on March 26, 2023, enacting it as Chapter 526 of the 2023 Acts of Assembly, with an effective date of July 1, 2023. Throughout its journey the measure — which sets consumer consent, data security, and access requirements for direct-to-consumer genetic testing companies and establishes civil penalties for violations — advanced without a single recorded dissenting vote in either chamber.
