## Summary

Vermont's H.639 (Act 135) adds the Genetic Information Privacy Act (9 V.S.A. chapter 61A, subchapter 1), regulating direct-to-consumer genetic testing companies and their service providers. Companies must provide a plain-language privacy summary and detailed privacy notice, and obtain separate express consent for collecting, using, and disclosing genetic data, storing biological samples, each secondary use, each third-party transfer, and marketing based on genetic data. Consent may be revoked as easily as given, and revocation must be honored, and samples destroyed, within 30 days. Companies must maintain reasonable security, let consumers access and delete their genetic data and request sample destruction (with third parties notified to delete within 30 days), and may not store genetic data or samples in sanctioned or foreign-adversary countries or move them outside the U.S. without express consent. Service provider contracts must restrict use and require destruction on termination, and discrimination against consumers who exercise their rights is prohibited.
