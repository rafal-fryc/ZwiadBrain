## Legislative History

### Legislative Journey

Substitute House Bill 1071, "Protecting personal information," was prefiled on December 31, 2018 by prime sponsor Rep. Shelley Kloba with Reps. Dolan, Tarleton, Slatter, Valdez, Ryu, Appleton, Smith, Stanford, and Frame, at the request of the Attorney General, to expand Washington's data-breach notification law. After first reading and referral to the House Committee on Innovation, Technology & Economic Development (ITED) on January 14, 2019, the committee held a public hearing on January 16, 2019 and took executive action on February 6, 2019, recommending a first substitute that broadened the statutory definition of "personal information" (adding, among other elements, date of birth, student/military/passport ID numbers, health insurance information, biometric data, and username-or-email-plus-password combinations) and shortened the notification deadline from 45 to 30 days. The House passed the substitute 94-0 on March 1, 2019. In the Senate, the bill was referred to the Committee on Environment, Energy & Technology, which held a public hearing on March 20, 2019 and voted to recommend the bill as amended, referring it to Ways & Means, on March 26, 2019; Ways & Means further amended and passed the bill on April 8, 2019. The Senate passed the bill as amended by a vote of 46-0 on April 15, 2019. The House then concurred in the Senate amendments and gave the bill final passage by a vote of 96-0 on April 22, 2019. Following signature by the Speaker (April 24) and President of the Senate (April 25) and delivery to the Governor (April 26), Governor Jay Inslee signed the bill on May 7, 2019, enacting it as Chapter 241, Laws of 2019, with the amended data-breach notification provisions taking effect March 1, 2020.
