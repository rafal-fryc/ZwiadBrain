## Summary

Washington's Engrossed Second Substitute HB 1170 (chapter 167, Laws of 2026) requires a "covered provider" — an entity whose generative AI system produces images, audio, or video and has more than 1,000,000 monthly users publicly accessible in Washington — to embed provenance data in content that system creates or materially alters, using commercially and technically reasonable methods (e.g., watermarking or C2PA-compatible metadata) that resist removal or tampering; minor edits like cropping, resizing, or filtering don't count as "material alteration." The law exempts business-to-business AI transactions, video game/interactive-experience products, and systems used solely for upscaling, noise reduction, or compression, and it doesn't require disclosure of trade secrets or identifying information about individuals. A separate provision requires state, local, and tribal government agencies deploying consumer-facing AI systems to clearly and conspicuously disclose, in plain language and without dark patterns, that a person is interacting with AI, regardless of whether that would otherwise be obvious. The attorney general enforces the law as an unfair or deceptive practice under the state Consumer Protection Act; it takes effect February 1, 2027.
